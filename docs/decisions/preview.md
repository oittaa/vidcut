# The preview: vidcut's own player, drawn with wgpu

The GUI previews through the player engine ([player.md](player.md)) and draws with eframe on wgpu. [`crates/vidcut/tests/live.rs`](../../crates/vidcut/tests/live.rs) tests it without a window (gui.md).

## 1. What plays

- **Plans.** The timeline plays `render::flatten_lanes(project, 0, end)`, the source `render::tape` (one clip, or the Source Tape): the plans export renders, so every picture is the one `frame render` makes (the engine reads them with the export's readers). Fresh proxies replace their media's pictures where the project plays proxies (§6). A frame of black and silence follows the content, where the playhead stands at the end (after an append, decision D15).
- **One player for both** (`live/player.rs`). One engine plays whichever the viewer shows. The other keeps its plan and playhead in the session, and the engine keeps the picture there ready (`Player::prepare`, player.md §3): decoded while the engine is paused with nothing else to decode, and held whatever the budget. Switching (Q, `viewer.set`) pauses, plays the other plan from its playhead (`set_plan_at`), and shows its picture at once. Then the frame left is prepared. An edit function from the source moves the timeline's playhead (decision D15): its new plan and frame are prepared the same way, so Q shows the result at once. `player.state`'s `ready` says whether each one's picture is decoded, and the GUI's snapshot changes when the one kept ready lands.
- **Memory:** `Budget::automatic` (player.md §3). The preferences' `player.memory_mb` and `player.video_memory_mb` (File > Preferences: Memory; `0`, the default, is automatic) set other limits, which a running GUI takes at once. The source and the timeline share the budgets.
- **Edits** give the engine a new plan (`set_plan`, player.md §3). Reloads after edits coalesce (gui.md §7). Another sample rate or channel count makes a new engine (its device was opened for them).
- **Nothing to convert.** Index-less containers (MPEG-TS), stills, image sequences at any rate, interlaced sources, turned pictures and HDR play as export reads them. The engine needs no remuxes, image lists, filler media or patches at cuts.

## 2. Threads

The threads are in gui.md §1. The viewer has a thread of its own because on GNOME Wayland with NVIDIA's driver, presenting a hidden (minimized) window blocks the thread until the window shows again, with Mailbox and FIFO alike. Submissions to the same device from another thread go on meanwhile, so presents, seeks and screenshots don't depend on the UI. Wayland can't show a minimized window again by request, so `gui.quit` ends the process without the window when the window hasn't closed in 5 s.

## 3. Decoding: on the GPU when there is one

- **The GPU decodes every stream it can** while `gpu.acceleration` and `gpu.decoding` are on. `preferences::gpu_decoding` decides it for the viewer, exports, proxies, tiles, the two-up, `frame render`, storyboards, the pool's pictures, picture measurements, measured light and the first frame a probe decodes. The decoder is on the GPU in use (`gpu.device`, else NVIDIA's, else the first other; `GpuDecoder::best`): NVDEC on NVIDIA's, VA-API on the others on Linux, Direct3D 11 on the others on Windows, VideoToolbox on a Mac. FFmpeg's own decoders do it, so every one gives the same frames. On NVIDIA's GPU, ProRes, which NVDEC doesn't decode, decodes through FFmpeg's Vulkan decoder (FFmpeg 8.1 and later: a `prores` decoder with a Vulkan configuration, `GpuDecoder::kind_for`), on the Vulkan device made from NVDEC's CUDA device (`CudaDevice::vulkan`). Only NVIDIA's GPUs decode on Vulkan, by their PCI vendor (`GpuDecoder::why_not`): FFmpeg's decoder crashes on AMD's Windows driver (gotchas.md). The CPU decodes what the GPU doesn't (ProRes elsewhere, VP8, a profile the GPU lacks), stills and image sequences, and H.264 with open GOPs on AMD's Direct3D 11 and VideoToolbox (`GpuDecoder::why_not`, gotchas.md). The GPU decodes even where a CPU with many cores decodes H.264 faster, as it leaves the CPU to the rest of the work (AGENTS.md rule 9). Long GOPs, with keyframes seconds apart, are what proxies are for (§6).
- **In video memory.** A frame the GPU decoded stays on it, NV12, P010, P210 or P416 (VideoToolbox's ProRes and ProRes 4444), and so do the pictures the engine keeps: up to a gigabyte of video memory (`Config::gpu_pictures`, half the GPU's memory once CUDA says it, the memory budget on Apple silicon). Kept frames don't run the decoder out. FFmpeg copies each frame out of NVDEC's few surfaces into one of its own. VA-API's decoder's pool grows as it needs, so a kept VA-API frame is the decoder's surface itself. Direct3D 11's are copied out of the decoder's texture array into textures of their own (`hw::Keeper`), made to be shared, the copy waited for (the device that reads them next can't wait for Direct3D 11's work); one that fails comes down. Vulkan's (ProRes on NVIDIA's GPU) come from a pool that grows too, and are kept once the GPU is done decoding them: the decode thread waits for each frame's timeline semaphores (`hw::vulkan::keep`), as wgpu's device can't wait on FFmpeg's. FFmpeg has no call that hands a frame's images to another device (its release to the external queue family is internal to its copy to CUDA), and a submission of vidcut's own on FFmpeg's queues needs `lock_queue`, which FFmpeg deprecates. NVDEC's and Vulkan's pictures share the video memory budget, as they share the GPU. Frames passed over on the way to a seek are decoded and not kept.
- **Shown from there.** NVDEC's (`gui/shared.rs`, `vidcut_av::cuda::share`): wgpu's Vulkan device makes a buffer whose memory it exports (`VK_KHR_external_memory_fd`; on Windows as a Win32 handle, `VK_KHR_external_memory_win32`), CUDA imports it, and to show a picture CUDA copies its planes into the buffer and wgpu copies them on into the plane textures (§4): two copies on the GPU, nothing through memory or the CPU. A few such buffers take turns, each free again once the queue has done the copy out of it (`Queue::on_submitted_work_done`): waiting for the GPU (`Device::poll`) would hold wgpu's device and stall the UI thread. VA-API's (Linux, `gui/picture.rs`): the surface is exported as DMA-BUFs (FFmpeg's DRM PRIME map, which waits for the GPU's work on it), each plane imported as a texture of wgpu's Vulkan device with its DRM format modifier (`texture_from_dmabuf_fd`, Intel's are Y-tiled), taken over from VA-API by an acquire from the external queue family (`gui::acquire`: wgpu can't record it, and its first barrier from `UNDEFINED` lets a driver drop a compressed picture), and copied into the plane textures. The picture is held until that copy is done, so its surface isn't reused under it. Direct3D 11's (Windows): wgpu's Vulkan device on the same adapter (their LUIDs) opens the texture through an NT handle (`texture_from_d3d11_shared_handle`, as NV12, or P010 where the device has P010 textures), takes it over by an acquire from the external queue family as VA-API's, and the shader draws from views of its two planes: nothing copied at all. VideoToolbox's (macOS): each plane of the pixel buffer's IOSurface becomes a Metal texture of wgpu's Metal device, and the shader draws from it: nothing copied. Vulkan's (`gui/vulkan.rs`, `vidcut_av::hw::vulkan`), where wgpu's Vulkan device is on the GPU of CUDA's device in use (their UUIDs) and has 16-bit textures: FFmpeg's device exports each plane's memory as an opaque handle, and wgpu's imports it into an image made as FFmpeg made its own, the memory allocated as FFmpeg allocated it (an opaque handle takes nothing else). wgpu acquires the images from the external queue family in the layout FFmpeg's frame records (`GENERAL` after the decode) and records in the frame where it left them (`hw::vulkan::acquired_outside`), so FFmpeg's next barrier on them acquires them back. The shader draws from views of the three planes: nothing copied. Each memory object is exported once, its handle kept until FFmpeg frees the frames context (gotchas.md). FFmpeg's pool hands the same frames out again until then, so the viewer keeps its import of each frame it draws, found again by the frame's memory objects (`hw::vulkan::Identity`). It keeps those of one frames context, the one of the picture drawn last. Another picture lets them go, and a thread of their own frees them: the driver takes a third of a millisecond for each plane. A picture of odd sides is in a texture rounded up to even ones, and the shader samples only the picture's part.
- **Copied down** where the viewer can't do that: NVDEC's with another backend than Vulkan, CUDA on another GPU than the display's, NVDEC's 4:2:2 (P210), Vulkan's on another GPU than the display's or without 16-bit textures, VideoToolbox's ProRes 4444 with alpha (AYUV64, its samples packed in one plane), a player without a viewer (tests); VA-API's, Direct3D 11's and Vulkan's where the display imports no DMA-BUFs, Direct3D 11 textures or Vulkan memory, or an import fails (another GPU's). A player keeps each decoder's frames in video memory where its display shows that decoder's from there (`gpu_pictures`). The frame comes to memory at its own size with its properties and is uploaded (§4). Interlaced frames from VA-API, Direct3D 11 and VideoToolbox come down before `bwdif`, and so do NVDEC's and Vulkan's where `bwdif_cuda` or `bwdif_vulkan` fails its check (export.md §4). Where it passes, they are deinterlaced on the GPU.
- **Switching.** The session watches the preferences file. A change, from anywhere, switches the engine's decoder at once (`Player::set_hardware`), and it decodes its pictures anew, the one shown first. `player.state`'s `hwdec` says what decoded the picture shown: `nvdec`, `vaapi`, `d3d11va`, `videotoolbox`, `vulkan` or `no`.
- **Frame-exact:** every GPU decoder's pictures are the CPU's, barcode for barcode, and within two levels in their pixels (`seek_check`, tools.md). Where a decoder isn't (H.264 with open GOPs on AMD's Direct3D 11 and VideoToolbox), the CPU decodes those streams (gotchas.md). `decode::h264_open_gop` tells such a stream by its first two keyframes and the first from each fifth of the file, and calls it open when they don't tell.

## 4. The picture on screen

- **One pass** (`gui/picture.rs`, `picture.wgsl`). A picture's planes go into textures at its own size, uploaded from memory or copied on the GPU from video memory (§3): Y, then U and V (planar) or UV interleaved (NV12, P010, P210, P416): half as wide as Y and half as tall for 4:2:0, half as wide for 4:2:2, Y's size for 4:4:4. An alpha plane (ProRes 4444's) is left out, as the export leaves it out. Samples of more than 8 bits go in 16-bit textures where the adapter has `TEXTURE_FORMAT_16BIT_NORM`, else as their top 8 bits. One draw into an RGBA8 target the size of the viewer's picture in physical pixels:
  - **placed** as export fits it (`Picture::placement`), black around it;
  - **turned** upright by the display matrix (`Picture::rotation`);
  - **scaled in YUV**, as export scales before converting: going down, the average of up to 8×8 bilinear taps over the samples a pixel covers; going up, bilinear;
  - **converted** with the export's math (`vidcut_av::color::shader_colors`): the picture's matrix and range to BT.709 RGB; HDR (PQ, HLG) to linear light with the reference white at 1.0, BT.2020 to BT.709, the mobius curve on the brightest channel and BT.709's transfer as a 2.4 power: the GPU export shader's math (export.md §5), in 32-bit floats throughout.
- **Dissolves** (spec D50): a picture's pictures over it are drawn after it, each in its own textures and at its level as the blend constant, over what the target holds. One quantization per picture over keeps flat areas within a level of `Picture::to_rgba`'s mix.
- **Shown one to one.** egui shows the target as a native texture, nearest-sampled, in a rectangle rounded to physical pixels. A target is drawn again only when the engine's picture or the size changes.
- **Against the export:** at a picture's own size, flat areas are within a level of `Picture::to_rgba` (the export's filters), PQ and HLG too. `crates/vidcut/tests/picture.rs` checks it for the planar layouts. Edges differ: at its own size the export repeats each chroma sample where the shader interpolates, and scaled, swscale's bicubic is sharper than the box. Barcodes read the same at every size.
- **Present mode:** Mailbox (Immediate where there is one), so a new picture shows at the next refresh and no frames queue.

A seek, step, load or edit reload returns once its frame is on screen. The contract is [gui.md §4](gui.md). Held keys: player.md §2.

## 6. Proxies

Rules, where the files live, and what still reads the originals: spec D41 and deviation X18. Jobs: gui.md §9. The GPU encode path, including `scale_cuda`: export.md §5.

Each proxy frame keeps the source frame's presentation timestamp (the source time base, or microseconds when that base is not a sane clock). The viewer's plan swaps only the video path (`vidcut_core::preview::apply_proxies`). An image sequence's proxy is picture `k` at `k/rate` with origin 0.

**Files** are named `<origin>-<width>x<height>-<codec>.mkv`, the origin as the thumbnails' (gui.md §11): the first 128 bits of the SHA-256 of the source's path, its size and its modification time. The tag `VIDCUT_ORIGIN` holds the source's size, modification time and path. `proxy.generate` keeps a file of that name only when its tag is the media's, and makes it again otherwise.

**10-bit sources** (more than 8 bits, by the first frame's layout) make 10-bit AV1, HEVC and ProRes proxies where the encoder takes them (spec D41): P010 for a hardware encoder the encoder check found working with it (`vidcut encoders list`), 10-bit 4:2:0 for SVT-AV1 and for x265 where its FFmpeg's takes 10 bits, and ProRes, which is 10-bit 4:2:2 for every source. HEVC on the GPU's video engine is Main 10 then, as exports set it. A GPU that decodes them in P010 then fits them for its encoder in their own layout. H.264 and DNxHR LB proxies are 8-bit.

**4:2:2 sources** (4:2:2 color or more, by the first frame's layout) keep it in ProRes and DNxHR LB proxies (spec D41). Apple's ProRes hardware takes their pictures as P210, or NV16 from 8 bits, where the encoder check found it working with both. A ProRes source's frames are P210 as VideoToolbox decodes them, so they go to the encoder on the GPU as they are. A 4:2:0 source's pictures stay 4:2:0 for it (P010, NV12): `scale_vt` keeps a frame's layout, and there is no more color to keep. Where the check finds no 4:2:2, every source's pictures are 4:2:0, and the encoder's name says so in `preferences.get`'s `uses` and in `made_with`: `ProRes Proxy from 4:2:0, Apple hardware (VideoToolbox)`.

**HDR sources** keep their colors in a 10-bit proxy (spec D41): PQ or HLG in BT.2020, and a PQ source's mastering display and light levels from its first frame. They are written as an export writes them: on the encoder, on each frame and in each HEVC and AV1 keyframe that lacks them. The source's HDR10+ and Dolby Vision are taken off its frames. With nothing to convert, each GPU fits the frames for its encoder in their own colors: NVDEC's for NVENC, Direct3D 11's for AMF where the video processor keeps the test picture's levels (PQ's within video range, gotchas.md), and VideoToolbox's. The viewer plays such a proxy by its own tags and tone-maps it as it does the original. An 8-bit proxy of an HDR source is tone-mapped as before: by libplacebo on NVIDIA's GPU, elsewhere on the CPU once the GPU has shrunk the frames.

**ProRes through Vulkan** (`prores_ks_vulkan`, FFmpeg 8.1 and later) leaves the CPU free. It is slower than `prores_ks` on a CPU with many cores, as FFmpeg's encoder works on one frame at a time (gotchas.md). On NVIDIA, NVDEC's frames and Vulkan's (a ProRes source's) stay on the GPU for it: libplacebo turns, converts and scales them into its 10-bit 4:2:2 pictures on the Vulkan device made from NVDEC's CUDA device, where the encoder works too (`cuda::CudaPictures`, as for NVENC, export.md §5). Vulkan's go in as they are, nothing copied. The encoder opens on frames of its own there and takes libplacebo's as they are: FFmpeg's Vulkan encoders read each frame through the frame's own frames context. The planner sends a CUDA frame there because CUDA's frames reach Vulkan's encoder (`hw::reaches`). A ProRes source's frames go to NVENC's proxies on the GPU too, libplacebo's picture copied into NVENC's CUDA frames. Pictures made on the CPU go up into the encoder's frames.

**Automatic** goes by what the GPU in use makes and plays (spec D41). It takes ProRes Proxy first where the GPU makes and plays it: on a Mac (VideoToolbox), and on NVIDIA, where Vulkan's ProRes encoder takes the decoder's frames on the GPU and counts as made there because the GPU plays ProRes on Vulkan (`proxy::round`). What it plays is the player's decoder there decoding a short clip of each codec (`hw::plays`, the clips of `vidcut decoders list`), tested once for each GPU and kept in `~/.cache/vidcut/plays.json` as the encoders are (export.md §5). Each entry names the codecs it tested, and one that doesn't name a codec `hw::plays` tests is tested again. `preferences.get` says for each codec what makes it and what plays it (`plays_on`: `NVDEC`, `Vulkan` for ProRes on NVIDIA's GPU, `null` for the CPU). An entry that doesn't read as codecs with their decoders is tested again.

**Decoded on the GPU without NVENC** (the decoder the viewer takes, `GpuDecoder::best`: NVDEC, VA-API, Direct3D 11, VideoToolbox, on a stream or device of its own), a proxy's frames stay on the GPU where its encoder is on the decoder's device. VAAPI on a VA-API decoder's render node, AMF on a Direct3D 11 decoder's adapter and VideoToolbox's encoders on a VideoToolbox decoder take them fitted there, as an export's encoder does (`Output::FittedOnGpu`, export.md §5). The rest, but NVDEC's for Vulkan's ProRes encoder (above), are shrunk on the GPU to the size the proxy shows them at, in their own orientation and layout, and come down that small for the CPU to turn, convert and encode (`hw::Shrinker`). NVDEC's scale with `scale_cuda` in halving steps (export.md §5), VA-API's with `scale_vaapi`, VideoToolbox's with `scale_vt`. Vulkan's scale with `scale_vulkan`, in halving steps as NVDEC's do, once their passes have given back a test picture of grays and colors at its levels (`hw::vulkan_scale`). Direct3D 11's scale on the GPU's video processor, told the frames' matrix in and out as full range where DXGI has such a space, so it keeps every level (PQ's aside, gotchas.md), once it has given back a test picture of grays and colors as it went in (`hw::d3d11::Scaler`; FFmpeg's `scale_d3d11` fails on AMD, gotchas.md). Where a GPU won't scale a layout, the frames come down whole.

**Tests:** `tests/proxy.rs` makes x264, SVT-AV1, x265, ProRes and DNxHR proxies, and H.264 and HEVC proxies with each GPU's own encoders from that GPU's decoder, and checks that every frame's barcode and timestamp are the source's. It makes proxies of a 10-bit HEVC source and of a PQ one with the software encoders that take 10 bits, and on each GPU AV1, HEVC and ProRes ones with the encoders there that take 10 bits, and checks that they are 10-bit. On NVIDIA it makes ProRes proxies with Vulkan's encoder from NVDEC's frames of H.264, 10-bit HEVC, 10-bit AV1 and PQ sources, and checks that the frames stayed on the GPU with nothing copied down or up, and that the proxies are 10-bit 4:2:2. The PQ source's are PQ in BT.2020, its barcodes' white (SDR white at 203 nits) within 4 levels of the source's, with its static HDR metadata. Its H.264 proxies are SDR BT.709. `proxy.rs`'s unit tests check automatic's order, the pictures each encoder takes, and the colors and HDR metadata a proxy keeps. `proxy.generate` says how each proxy was made (`made_with`: `decoded on the GPU (Direct3D 11), frames came down at 960×540, H.264, software (x264)`). `tests/live.rs` checks that a proxy shows the same frames as its original, a PQ source's 10-bit AV1 proxy too. `tests/cli.rs` checks that a file under a media's proxy name made from another file is made again from the media.

## 7. Sound

Mix, clock, backward play and other speeds: player.md §4. Scrubbing: spec D42.
- **The device won't open:** the engine plays to a silent stand-in and the status bar says why, once. The picture plays all the same.
- **Faders while dragged** (`mixer.preview`): a gain on top of the lane while the timeline plays (player.md §4), held while the source is shown and heard again when the timeline is. Releasing saves the level with `track.set`. A track with no sound in the mix (muted, left out by solo, empty) is an error. While the source is shown it answers `live: false`.
- **Monitoring mute** (`player.mute`) silences playback only (spec §9).

## 9. The two-up: two players

The Trim Editor's and Dynamic Trim's two-up show frames of a copy of the project that the viewer's player doesn't play. Each cell is a player of its own (`gui/cells.rs`), without sound, playing the whole media of the clip it shows (`render::tape`), so a trim moves it along one plan and its pictures stay kept. The cell tells its player the clip's source frame there as the trim moves (`Player::follow`), and draws what it presents with the viewer's shader (§4), from GPU memory where the display shows that decoder's pictures.

- **Runs** (Dynamic Trim's J and L): the cell follows at the run's speed, so its player decodes ahead and keeps windows behind as playing that way does, and comes first (player.md §1–3). Standing still or stepped, it is a paused player, with 8 frames decoded ahead and the windows behind, which decode on a thread and reader of their own, one window at a time across both cells and none while either runs forward, so a run either way starts from kept frames: two decoding sessions a cell.
- **Memory:** each cell keeps a quarter of the viewer's budgets (`Budget::part`), half of them together. The frame a cell stands on, and those just ahead, are kept as decoded, so a cell drawn larger shows it sharp. A run's frames and the windows behind come down from the GPU shrunk to the cell's size in physical pixels (`Player::shrink_to`): a 4K 10-bit picture is 24 MB as decoded, so a cell's 2 GB of video memory would hold 86, fewer than the runs over a cut keep. A cell drawn more than an eighth larger or smaller lets the shrunk ones go. The players go when the two-up closes, on a thread of their own (`vidcut-two-up-close`): a player ends once its decoding stops and its decoder and converter are torn down, behind the window the other cell is still decoding, and the UI doesn't wait for that.
- **Counts:** `player.state`'s `two_up` counts the frames the cells were asked for and those passed over: asked for, and another asked for before the frame showed (cli.md).
