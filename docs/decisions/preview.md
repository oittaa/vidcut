# The preview: vidcut's own player, drawn with wgpu

The GUI previews through the player engine ([player.md](player.md)) and draws with eframe on wgpu. [`crates/vidcut/tests/live.rs`](../../crates/vidcut/tests/live.rs) tests it without a window (gui.md).

## 1. What plays

- **Plans.** The timeline plays `render::flatten_lanes(project, 0, end)`, the source `render::tape` (one clip, or the Source Tape): the plans export renders, so every picture is the one `frame render` makes (the engine reads them with the export's readers). Fresh proxies replace their media's pictures where the project plays proxies (§6). A frame of black and silence follows the content, where the playhead stands at the end (after an append, decision D15).
- **One player for both** (`live/player.rs`). One engine plays whichever the viewer shows. The other keeps its plan and playhead in the session, and the engine keeps the picture there ready (`Player::prepare`, player.md §3): decoded while the engine is paused with nothing else to decode, and held whatever the budget. Switching (Q, `viewer.set`) pauses, plays the other plan from its playhead (`set_plan_at`), and shows its picture at once. Then the frame left is prepared. An edit function from the source moves the timeline's playhead (decision D15): its new plan and frame are prepared the same way, so Q shows the result at once. `player.state`'s `ready` says whether each one's picture is decoded, and the GUI's snapshot changes when the one kept ready lands.
- **Memory:** `Budget::automatic` (player.md §3). The preferences' `player.memory_mb` and `player.video_memory_mb` (File > Preferences: Player; `0`, the default, is automatic) set other limits, which a running GUI takes at once. The source and the timeline share the budgets.
- **Edits** give the engine a new plan (`set_plan`, player.md §3). Reloads after edits coalesce (gui.md §7). Another sample rate or channel count makes a new engine (its device was opened for them).
- **Nothing to convert.** Index-less containers (MPEG-TS), stills, image sequences at any rate, interlaced sources, turned pictures and HDR play as export reads them. The engine needs no remuxes, image lists, filler media or patches at cuts.

## 2. Threads

The threads are in gui.md §1. The viewer has a thread of its own because on GNOME Wayland with NVIDIA's driver, presenting a hidden (minimized) window blocks the thread until the window shows again, with Mailbox and FIFO alike. Submissions to the same device from another thread go on meanwhile, so presents, seeks and screenshots don't depend on the UI. Wayland can't show a minimized window again by request, so `gui.quit` ends the process without the window when the window hasn't closed in 5 s.

## 3. Decoding: on the GPU when there is one

- **The GPU decodes every stream it can** while `gpu.acceleration` and `gpu.decoding` are on. `preferences::gpu_decoding` decides it for the viewer, exports, proxies, tiles, the two-up, `frame render`, storyboards, the pool's pictures, picture measurements, measured light and the first frame a probe decodes. The decoder is on the GPU in use (`gpu.device`, else NVIDIA's, else the first other; `GpuDecoder::best`): NVDEC on NVIDIA's, VA-API on the others on Linux, Direct3D 11 on the others on Windows, VideoToolbox on a Mac. FFmpeg's own decoders do it, so every one gives the same frames. The CPU decodes what the GPU doesn't (ProRes, VP8, a profile the GPU lacks), stills and image sequences, and H.264 with open GOPs on AMD's Direct3D 11 and VideoToolbox (`GpuDecoder::why_not`, gotchas.md). The GPU decodes even where a CPU with many cores decodes H.264 faster, as it leaves the CPU to the rest of the work (AGENTS.md rule 9). Long GOPs, with keyframes seconds apart, are what proxies are for (§6).
- **In video memory.** A frame the GPU decoded stays on it, NV12 or P010, and so do the pictures the engine keeps: up to a gigabyte of video memory (`Config::gpu_pictures`, half the GPU's memory once CUDA says it). Kept frames don't run the decoder out. FFmpeg copies each frame out of NVDEC's few surfaces into one of its own. VA-API's decoder's pool grows as it needs, so a kept VA-API frame is the decoder's surface itself. Direct3D 11's are copied out of the decoder's texture array into textures of their own (`hw::Keeper`), made to be shared, the copy waited for (the device that reads them next can't wait for Direct3D 11's work); one that fails comes down. Frames passed over on the way to a seek are decoded and not kept.
- **Shown from there.** NVDEC's (`gui/shared.rs`, `vidcut_av::cuda::share`): wgpu's Vulkan device makes a buffer whose memory it exports (`VK_KHR_external_memory_fd`; on Windows as a Win32 handle, `VK_KHR_external_memory_win32`), CUDA imports it, and to show a picture CUDA copies its planes into the buffer and wgpu copies them on into the plane textures (§4): two copies on the GPU, nothing through memory or the CPU. A few such buffers take turns, each free again once the queue has done the copy out of it (`Queue::on_submitted_work_done`): waiting for the GPU (`Device::poll`) would hold wgpu's device and stall the UI thread. VA-API's (Linux, `gui/picture.rs`): the surface is exported as DMA-BUFs (FFmpeg's DRM PRIME map, which waits for the GPU's work on it), each plane imported as a texture of wgpu's Vulkan device with its DRM format modifier (`texture_from_dmabuf_fd`, Intel's are Y-tiled), taken over from VA-API by an acquire from the external queue family (`gui::acquire`: wgpu can't record it, and its first barrier from `UNDEFINED` lets a driver drop a compressed picture), and copied into the plane textures. The picture is held until that copy is done, so its surface isn't reused under it. Direct3D 11's (Windows): wgpu's Vulkan device on the same adapter (their LUIDs) opens the texture through an NT handle (`texture_from_d3d11_shared_handle`, as NV12, or P010 where the device has P010 textures), takes it over by an acquire from the external queue family as VA-API's, and the shader draws from views of its two planes: nothing copied at all. VideoToolbox's (macOS): each plane of the pixel buffer's IOSurface becomes a Metal texture of wgpu's Metal device, and the shader draws from it: nothing copied. A picture of odd sides is in a texture rounded up to even ones, and the shader samples only the picture's part.
- **Copied down** where the viewer can't do that: NVDEC's with another backend than Vulkan, CUDA on another GPU than the display's, a player without a viewer (tests); VA-API's and Direct3D 11's where the display imports no DMA-BUFs or Direct3D 11 textures, or an import fails (another GPU's). The frame comes to memory at its own size with its properties and is uploaded (§4). Interlaced frames from VA-API and Direct3D 11 come down before `bwdif` (NVDEC's go through `bwdif_cuda`).
- **Switching.** The session watches the preferences file. A change, from anywhere, switches the engine's decoder at once (`Player::set_hardware`), and it decodes its pictures anew, the one shown first. `player.state`'s `hwdec` says what decoded the picture shown: `nvdec`, `vaapi`, `d3d11va`, `videotoolbox` or `no`.
- **Frame-exact:** every GPU decoder's pictures are the CPU's, barcode for barcode, and within two levels in their pixels (`seek_check`, tools.md). Where a decoder isn't (H.264 with open GOPs on AMD's Direct3D 11 and VideoToolbox), the CPU decodes those streams (gotchas.md).

## 4. The picture on screen

- **One pass** (`gui/picture.rs`, `picture.wgsl`). A picture's planes go into textures at its own size, uploaded from memory or copied on the GPU from video memory (§3): Y, then U and V (8-bit planar) or UV interleaved (NV12, and P010 in 16-bit textures where the adapter has `TEXTURE_FORMAT_16BIT_NORM`; without them P010 goes up as its high bytes). One draw into an RGBA8 target the size of the viewer's picture in physical pixels:
  - **placed** as export fits it (`Picture::placement`), black around it;
  - **turned** upright by the display matrix (`Picture::rotation`);
  - **scaled in YUV**, as export scales before converting: going down, the average of up to 8×8 bilinear taps over the samples a pixel covers; going up, bilinear;
  - **converted** with the export's math (`vidcut_av::color::shader_colors`): the picture's matrix and range to BT.709 RGB; HDR (PQ, HLG) to linear light with the reference white at 1.0, BT.2020 to BT.709, the mobius curve on the brightest channel and BT.709's transfer as a 2.4 power: the GPU export shader's math (export.md §5), in 32-bit floats throughout.
- **Shown one to one.** egui shows the target as a native texture, nearest-sampled, in a rectangle rounded to physical pixels. A target is drawn again only when the engine's picture or the size changes.
- **Against the export:** at a picture's own size, flat areas are within a level of `Picture::to_rgba` (the export's filters), PQ and HLG too. Edges differ: at its own size the export repeats each chroma sample where the shader interpolates, and scaled, swscale's bicubic is sharper than the box. Barcodes read the same at every size.
- **Present mode:** Mailbox (Immediate where there is one), so a new picture shows at the next refresh and no frames queue.

## 5. When a frame is on screen

A seek, step, load or edit reload returns once its frame is on screen. The contract is [gui.md §4](gui.md). Held keys: player.md §2.

## 6. Proxies

Rules, where the files live, and what still reads the originals: spec D41 and deviation X18. Jobs: gui.md §9. The GPU encode path, including `scale_cuda`: export.md §5.

Each proxy frame keeps the source frame's presentation timestamp (the source time base, or microseconds when that base is not a sane clock). The viewer's plan swaps only the video path (`vidcut_core::preview::apply_proxies`). An image sequence's proxy is picture `k` at `k/rate` with origin 0.

**ProRes through Vulkan** (`prores_ks_vulkan`, FFmpeg 8.1 and later) leaves the CPU free. It is slower than `prores_ks` on a CPU with many cores, as FFmpeg's encoder works on one frame at a time (gotchas.md).

**Automatic** goes by what the GPU in use makes and plays (spec D41). What it plays is the player's decoder there decoding a short clip of each codec (`hw::plays`, the clips of `vidcut decoders list`), tested once for each GPU and kept in `~/.cache/vidcut/plays.json` as the encoders are (export.md §5). `preferences.get` says for each codec what makes it and what plays it (`plays_on`: `NVDEC`, `null` for the CPU).

**Decoded on the GPU** (the decoder the viewer takes, `GpuDecoder::best`: NVDEC, VA-API, Direct3D 11 or VideoToolbox, on a stream or device of its own), a proxy's frames are made as an export's are (`export::video::Maker`, export.md §5). An encoder that takes the decoder's frames on its device gets them fitted there, never copied: NVENC NVDEC's, Vulkan's ProRes encoder NVDEC's and Direct3D 11's through libplacebo on the same GPU (export.md §5), VAAPI those of the VA-API decoder on its render node, AMF those of the Direct3D 11 decoder on its adapter (colors and HDR through libplacebo, export.md §5), VideoToolbox those of the VideoToolbox decoder where they fill the picture in its layout. For the other encoders, and frames the GPU can't fit, they are shrunk on the GPU to the size the proxy shows them at, in their own orientation and layout. They come down that small for the CPU to turn, convert and encode (`hw::Shrinker`). NVDEC's scale with `scale_cuda` in halving steps (export.md §5), VA-API's with `scale_vaapi`, VideoToolbox's with `scale_vt`. Direct3D 11's scale on the GPU's video processor, told the frames' matrix in and out as full range where DXGI has such a space, so it keeps every level (PQ's aside, gotchas.md), once it has given back a test picture of grays and colors as it went in (`hw::d3d11::Scaler`; FFmpeg's `scale_d3d11` fails on AMD, gotchas.md). A shrink to less than half each way goes in halvings, each pass checked (gotchas.md). Where a GPU won't scale a layout, the frames come down whole.

**Tests:** `tests/proxy.rs` makes x264, SVT-AV1, ProRes and DNxHR proxies and checks that every frame's barcode and timestamp are the source's. `proxy.rs`'s unit tests check automatic's order. `proxy.generate` says how each proxy was made (`made_with`: `decoded on the GPU (Direct3D 11), frames stayed there, H.264, AMD hardware (AMF)`) and the frames copied down from the GPU and up to it (`copies`). The `proxy_check` tool (tools.md) makes proxies of the barcode fixtures on every GPU decoder with every encoder here and checks each frame. `tests/live.rs` checks that a proxy shows the same frames as its original.

## 7. Sound

Mix, clock, backward play and other speeds: player.md §4. Scrubbing: spec D42.
- **The device won't open:** the engine plays to a silent stand-in and the status bar says why, once. The picture plays all the same.
- **Faders while dragged** (`mixer.preview`): a gain on top of the lane while the timeline plays (player.md §4), held while the source is shown and heard again when the timeline is. Releasing saves the level with `track.set`. A track with no sound in the mix (muted, left out by solo, empty) is an error. While the source is shown it answers `live: false`.
- **Monitoring mute** (`player.mute`) silences playback only (spec §9).

## 9. Exact frames for the two-up: decoded once either way

The Trim Editor's and Dynamic Trim's two-up show frames of a copy of the project that the player doesn't play: `vidcut_av::export::renderer::StillRenderer`, the export engine's renderer, draws them at the cell's size (`gui/stills.rs`), a thread per cell. The engine's pictures going back follow its rule.

- **The problem it solves.** Stepping back with exact seeks decodes each step from the keyframe before it. On files with keyframes seconds apart that is a few hundred frames again for every step.
- **Forward:** the file and decoder stay open, and the next frame decodes on from the one before.
- **Back:** a window renders at once, from a keyframe up to the frame asked for, and is kept. The keyframe is found with one seek and one decoded frame (where the seek lands), and the window starts at the first frame sampled at or after it, so a seek for it lands on that keyframe and nothing in the window decodes twice. A window reaches back over more keyframes to hold at least 2 s, and holds at most half the memory budget (256 MB a cell).
- **Ahead:** windows render on a second thread (`Prefetch`) before they are needed. Wherever the position is, even after a jump, at least 2 s behind it are kept. Going back, a whole window more is kept, so the next window back renders while the position goes through this one. Asked for a frame a window is rendering, the renderer waits for it.
- **Floor:** the cell says where the shown stretch of the file starts. Windows never reach behind it: frames before a clip's first one aren't shown there.
- **On the GPU:** while `gpu.acceleration` and `gpu.decoding` are on, the streams the GPU decodes decode on the GPU the viewer decodes on (`StillRenderer::decoding_on`, on a stream or device of their own; the preferences are read as each frame is asked for), shrunk there to the cell's size. Interlaced ones of VA-API and Direct3D 11 come down whole, for the CPU to deinterlace.
- **Eviction:** over the budget, the pictures kept or looked at least recently go first. Moving back and forth over a cut keeps both sides whole: a hole in the kept frames would decode from a keyframe while the cell waits.
