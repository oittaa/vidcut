# Export

**Tests:** [`crates/vidcut-av/tests/export.rs`](../../crates/vidcut-av/tests/export.rs) (frame and sample exactness, A/V offset, rate conversion, presets, cancelling) and [`crates/vidcut/tests/cli.rs`](../../crates/vidcut/tests/cli.rs) (export through the CLI).

## 1. In-process and streaming

**Where it runs:**
- Export runs in-process on the FFmpeg libraries (`vidcut-av::export`), with no ffmpeg CLI subprocesses (AGENTS.md rule 5).
- The work is planned by `vidcut_core::render::flatten`, a pure function, then encoded by the engine.

**Plan:**
- Video is a list of pieces: source ranges, black, or a dissolve's mix of them, each over the ones before at its level (spec D50).
- Audio is one or more lanes of pieces: source ranges, a cross fade's with its gain curve, or silence. On a track with cross fades the sound after each one's cut goes on the track's other lane, so both play through it.
- Every boundary is computed with exact integer math. Audio boundaries use `Rational::frame_to_sample` (i128, rounded), so lengths never drift. For example, 29.97 fps at 48 kHz alternates 1601 and 1602 samples per frame.

**Engine:**
- For each output frame, it pulls the next video frame from the current piece's decoder (one for each picture of a mix) and makes its picture, then exactly the audio samples that frame spans from each lane.
- The encoders and the muxer work on a thread of their own (`export/worker.rs`), fed in order through a channel that holds three frames, so decoding and making the next pictures go on while an encoder waits for the GPU. Proxies encode the same way. An error on either thread stops the export with the error a single thread would have met first: after a decoding error the encoders still take the frames handed over before it. A cancel stops them at once.
- Memory stays flat with the segment count: each reader (video, and each audio lane) has its current file open, plus the next one while it opens. A test with 400 segments checks this, in a process of its own.
- A two-pass export (§5, Passes) runs the video through the encoder twice: the first time to measure, muxing nothing, then as above.

**Starting a piece** (`export/video.rs`, `export/audio.rs`, `export/ahead.rs`):
- **Same file as the piece before:** the reader reads on. A piece that starts at most 1 s past where the decoder is decodes on to its first frame (or skips samples). Anything else seeks the open file. A frame repeated across the cut needs neither.
- **Another file:** opening it, seeking and decoding up to its first frame happen on a helper thread while the piece before encodes, from the piece's second frame on. A failure there is the same error the piece would have hit.
- **One file ahead at a time** (`Ahead`): a newer piece asked for replaces one not begun, and a source nobody wants any more (the pieces were made anew by a seek, and the next file is another) stops opening and closes on the helper thread, never on the reader's. The same file asked for again is taken up. A seek a frame, such as a dragged playhead, opens nothing ahead.
- **One opener thread per reader for the whole export.** glibc gives every new thread its own malloc arena, and decoder memory freed across many arenas stays resident.

**Output:** goes to a hidden `.<name>.part` file, renamed into place on success. Errors and cancellation delete it.

**Damaged sources:** a packet the decoder rejects as invalid data is skipped, as players skip it, and the export goes on.
- Video shows the next frame in its place (§2). Frames that refer to it show what the decoder makes of them, up to the next keyframe.
- Audio has silence there (§3).
- Any other decoder error fails the export, and so does the 101st damaged frame in a row. Audio levels, scene measurements and waveforms decode by the same rule (`decode::decode_stream`).

## 2. Sampling rule (preview and export)

Output frame `k` of a source piece shows the **first source frame whose timestamp is at least `origin + (source_frame + k)/fps − ½ frame`**.
- Sources at the project rate: frame-exact, checked with barcodes across H.264, HEVC 10-bit, VP9, ProRes, MPEG-TS and VFR fixtures.
- Other rates: nearest-frame sampling with ties going to the later frame. A 25 fps clip on a 30 fps timeline shows source frames `0 1 2 3 3 4 5 6 7 8 8 9…`.
- The preview (the player engine, [preview.md](preview.md)) reads with these same readers, so it shows the export's frames at every rate.

**Stills and image sequences:**
- A still's one picture is decoded once and shows for every frame of its pieces. It is never sought.
- A sequence is read by FFmpeg's `image2` demuxer (`decode::Input::Sequence`): picture `k` has timestamp `k` in a time base of one frame at the sequence's rate, so the rule above picks pictures as from a video file. The run is the contiguous files from its first number: image2's `start_number_range` is 1, so a missing first file fails the open. The last picture lasts its own frame, as a video's does. A run that ends before the sequence's count (a missing file) fails the export rather than repeating the picture before the hole.

**Speed changes** (spec D48): frame `k` of a piece at another speed shows source frame `source_frame + ⌊(skip + k) × speed⌋ − ⌊skip × speed⌋` (`render::Pace`; `skip` is how far into its clip's pace the piece starts). A piece walking its source backward has its pictures made from the keyframe before them up, as many at a time as fit in 256 MB (at most 64; 4 for an encoder's frames on the GPU, whose pools are small).

## 3. Audio

**Reading:**
- Each source piece is read sample-exactly: seek before the start, decode, find the first frame's timestamp, and drop samples up to the start time.
- Decoders get the packet time base (`pkt_timebase`). FFmpeg needs it to shift timestamps past skipped priming samples (Opus pre-skip, AAC priming).
- **Gaps follow the timestamps.** A frame that starts more than 2 ms after the one before it ended follows a gap: a packet the file lacks, or one the decoder rejected.
  - The gap becomes silence, made a second at a time as it is read, so what follows stays in sync with the picture.
  - Audio that starts after a piece's start (a stream that begins after the picture, or a lost first packet) is silent until it does.
  - A frame stamped past the file's end has a broken timestamp: it plays in order, with no gap. That end is the container's start plus its duration. With no container start, the duration is added to the audio stream's start, and with neither the end is unknown.
  - When the duration is missing or not positive, a jump of more than a second plays in order too. A shorter one is still silence.
  - Smaller differences are timestamp rounding (Matroska keeps milliseconds).
  - Audio levels and waveforms count gaps the same way, so silence found in a measurement is where the export has it.

**Conversion and mixing:**
- libswresample converts to interleaved `f32` at the export rate and layout: mono is upmixed, 5.1 is downmixed. vidcut sizes the output buffers itself, as ffmpeg-next's wrapper can truncate when upsampling.
- **Loudness normalization** (`audio.loudness`): [agents.md](agents.md) §5.
- Lanes are summed in Rust, with clip gain applied.
- **De-click:** 3 ms linear fades at every cut inside a lane, but none at a cross fade's own edges (on by default; `declick=false` turns them off).
- **Speed changes** (spec D48): a piece at another speed reads its source forward, or backward half a second at a time with each read reversed, through the WSOLA stretch (`stretch.rs`, the player's), keeping the pitch. Starting inside it, the stretch starts 20 ms before and drops that, as its first fragment fades in.

Beeps land within 1 ms of where they should across codec boundaries, and output sample counts are exact.

## 4. Video normalization

- Each source frame goes through a libavfilter graph: `scale` (fit inside the output keeping the display aspect, honoring the sample aspect ratio), `pad` (black, centered), `setsar=1`, `format=<encoder pixel format>`. The fit is computed in Rust (`export::normalize::fit`).
- **Dissolves** (spec D50): each picture of a mix is made into the output by a stage of its own (its own normalizer), then they are mixed sample by sample: `(under × (65536 − w) + over × w + 32768) >> 16`, w the level in 65536ths. For NVENC on NVIDIA's GPU (`cuda/mix.rs`, a PTX kernel the driver compiles), else on the CPU (`export/mix.rs`). The planner brings mixed frames down from other GPUs (`route::Facts::mixed`). The mix keeps the colors and Dolby Vision of the picture it shows most of. Its HDR10+ is, value by value, the brightest picture's, so it never claims less light than it shows.
- **Rotation** (spec D33): a stream's display matrix turns the picture upright first: `transpose=clock` or `cclock`, or `hflip,vflip` for 180°. The fit then uses the shown aspect. Thumbnails turn their pictures the same way.
- **Interlaced sources** (spec D34): `VideoReader` deinterlaces streams flagged interlaced, in decode order and before the sampling rule picks frames, so `bwdif` sees each frame's real neighbors.
  - It runs `bwdif=mode=send_frame:deint=interlaced`: one frame per frame, the first field kept, progressive frames passing through. Frames NVDEC decodes go through `bwdif_cuda` with the same options, and Vulkan's (ProRes on NVIDIA's GPU) through `bwdif_vulkan` (§5), where that filter gives `bwdif`'s frames of their layout. Otherwise they come down for `bwdif`.
  - A check decides it the first time frames of a layout come (`hw::deinterlace`). Three 128×64 interlaced frames, in each field order, go through the GPU's filter and through `bwdif`: black, with white blocks along the top and bottom edges moving each field, so `bwdif` takes its spatial path along the picture's edges and the blocks'. Every row of every component must come out within a level of 8 bits of `bwdif`'s, and no sample may be above its component's bits. The test frames are in frames of their own, made like the stream's (device, layout, and on Vulkan the images' formats, tiling and uses), so nothing lies past their edges. The verdict holds for the process. FFmpeg 9.0.1's filters both fail it (gotchas.md).
  - A seek starts it over, and frames decoded on the way to the target feed it. `bwdif` halves its output time base, so timestamps are rescaled back to the stream's.
  - Thumbnails aren't deinterlaced: at pool size, combing barely shows.
- **Color** (`color.rs`, spec D35). Every picture is converted to the output's colors, from its tags or else what players assume (untagged: BT.709 when 1280 wide or over 576 lines, BT.601 otherwise; HDR untagged in BT.2020):
  - **SDR into SDR:** `scale` converts the YUV matrix and range to BT.709 limited as it scales. Conversions (another matrix or range, YUV to RGB or back) take swscale's `accurate_rnd+full_chroma_int`: without them BT.601's white (235) comes out 233 in BT.709 and 253 in RGB.
  - **HDR into SDR:** `zscale` takes the picture to linear light, with the reference white (203 nits) at 1.0, then to BT.709 primaries. `tonemap=mobius` (transition 0.8, peak 1000 nits) maps it, and `zscale` gives BT.709's transfer. Everything up to 0.8 of the reference white stays as it was, so SDR-graded material in an HDR file looks as it did, and highlights roll off above it. The curve is mobius because `hable` maps that white to about 40% gray.
  - **Into HDR** (PQ or HLG): the same linear light, SDR white at 203 nits (BT.2408), then BT.2020 and the transfer. An SDR source's white lands at PQ code 573 of 1023.
  - **Output tags:** encoders tag what they write: BT.709, or BT.2020 with PQ or HLG.
  - **Which output** (spec D44) is decided in `vidcut_core::export::color` before encoding, from each media's transfer and pixel format in the project (the engine, given `auto` directly, probes the files) and what the encoders can write (the encoder cache and `encode::abilities`). The plan's and the result's `outputs[].color` say what was chosen and why.
  - `zscale` needs explicit output primaries when it converts to linear: untagged frames otherwise give it no path.
  - **Without zimg** (`color::split`): swscale reads the scaled picture as float RGB, and the CPU does `zscale`'s math (the shader's, §5) and writes planar YUV, within a level of zimg's (two for HLG's colors).
  - `scale` is told the source's matrix and range for both sides, so it only scales what `zscale` converts. `zscale` writes the YUV itself, in the planar layouts of NVENC's NV12 and P010, which swscale then only interleaves.
- **HDR metadata** (spec D46; `vidcut_av::hdr`, `vidcut_core::export::metadata`). Chosen per file after the dynamic range, from what the project knows of each piece (the probe reads it from the first decoded frame) or, given `auto` directly, from the files.
  - **Static values** go to the encoder before it opens (`AVCodecContext.decoded_side_data`). libx265 and libsvtav1 write them into the bitstream at every keyframe (x265 with `repeat-headers=1`), and NVENC does when FFmpeg was built with its SDK 13 headers. FFmpeg's `ff_encode_preinit` copies them into the stream's parameters, from which MP4/MOV (`mdcv`/`clli`) and Matroska/WebM write them. Every frame carries them too, for VAAPI, which reads them only there. A keyframe packet still without them gets them from vidcut (`hdr::insert_static`). x265 also gets `hdr10-opt=1` for PQ.
  - **Measured light** (`vidcut_av::light`): the frames a piece shows, copied down whole so the values are the CPU's, read as the export converts them. Each frame gives each channel's peak over every pixel. From a grid of at most half a million pixels it gives the average of each pixel's brightest channel and that channel's percentiles. A file's measurements are kept with the spans they cover, in memory and in `~/.cache/vidcut/analysis` (`light-<origin>`, named and checked as the thumbnails are, gui.md §11, a sequence's first number and rate added), and a piece measures only what isn't there. A piece's MaxCLL and MaxFALL are of the frames the sampling rule picks (`light::sampled`). The session measures before choosing the metadata, reporting progress with `measuring` set. A plan measures nothing. A piece that can't be measured is left out, and the result says so.
  - **Each output frame** loses its source's HDR metadata and gets back only the dynamic kinds the file keeps, from the frame it shows (`VideoFrames::with_dynamic`), after scaling, libplacebo or the upload, which drop or change it.
  - **HDR10+:** FFmpeg's HEVC and AV1 encoders don't write it (9.0's libaom does), so vidcut puts it into their packets (`hdr::insert_hdr10plus`): a prefix SEI before the first slice, or a metadata OBU before the first frame OBU. libvpx-vp9 takes it from the frames, and WebM/Matroska store it as BlockAdditions. Frames without it get it made from their measured light (`hdr::Hdr10Plus`): one window, no target display, each channel's peak, the average and the percentiles at 1, 5, 10, 25, 50, 75, 90, 95 and 99%, no curve. Black frames get zeros. FFmpeg's HEVC decoder repeats the last HDR10+ on frames without their own (gotchas.md), and those keep it.
  - **Dolby Vision:** libx265 and libsvtav1 make an RPU from each frame's `AV_FRAME_DATA_DOVI_METADATA` when `dolbyvision=1` (profile 8 for HEVC, 10 for AV1). A frame without one is an error, so every picture must have it, and a black frame takes the RPU of the last frame shown. x265 needs a VBV for it: peaks of 1.5 times a target bitrate, or 160 Mb/s at constant quality. MP4 writes `dvcC` only with `strict=unofficial`.
  - **Structs:** ffmpeg-sys-next doesn't bind `mastering_display_metadata.h`, so `hdr.rs` mirrors the two structs and takes FFmpeg's allocators' sizes.
  - **Tests** (`vidcut-av/tests/hdr.rs`): HDR10 values and per-frame HDR10+ go through x265, SVT-AV1 and VP9 and come out as they went in. `max_cll=measure` writes the measured levels, and a highlight of two pixels is the peak.

## 5. Encoders

**Settings and presets:**
- Settings are pure data in `vidcut_core::export`: container, video (codec, encoder, quality or bitrate, speed, profile, bit depth, size, dynamic range) and audio (codec, bitrate, rate, channels). The dynamic range and an unset depth are chosen per file (spec D44). Presets leave them to it, except TikTok's, which asks for SDR.
- Built-in presets follow the Quick Export models (spec §10): H.264/H.265 Master, ProRes 422 HQ, YouTube 720p/1080p/2160p, Vimeo, TikTok. There are also AV1, VP9, DNxHR HQ and five audio-only presets. The Quick Export row is deviation X3.
- User presets are TOML files in `~/.config/vidcut/presets/`. `--set key=value` overrides any field.

**CPU encoders:** each codec's first that this FFmpeg has (`vidcut_av::parts`): H.264 x264, OpenH264; HEVC x265, Kvazaar (sizes in multiples of 8 only); AV1 SVT-AV1, libaom, rav1e; ProRes `prores_ks`, `prores_aw`; Opus libopus, FFmpeg's own. The quality stays on the codec's scale: OpenH264 and Kvazaar take it as one quantizer for every frame, rav1e as a quantizer index. `doctor` names the stand-ins in use (`ffmpeg_stand_ins`).

**`auto` encoder:**
- NVENC if a test encode works. Then AMF (AMD) and QSV (Intel) on Windows, VAAPI (AMD and Intel) on Linux, VideoToolbox (H.264, HEVC) on macOS. Then the CPU encoder (`encode::AUTO_GPU`). On Linux QSV needs Intel's VPL GPU runtime (`libmfx-gen1.2` on Ubuntu), and VAAPI serves the same GPUs without it. AMF on Linux needs AMD's closed driver, so vidcut leaves it out there.
- An export takes the first of these that opens (`encode::video_encoders`). NVENC allows a few sessions at a time, so a GPU encoder that tested fine can still fail to open. The report names the encoder used, and the plan names the first choice.
- An encoder asked for by name (`nvenc`, `vaapi`, `amf`, `qsv`, `cpu`) fails the export if it won't open. If none opens, the error says why each didn't.
- An explicit `video.speed` goes to the encoder an export starts with. Encoders don't share speed names, so one it moves on to uses its own default.
- Detection test-encodes every GPU encoder × pixel format × GPU (`vidcut_av::gpus::list`), in about 1 s:
  - NVENC on each NVIDIA GPU's CUDA device (FFmpeg's `gpu` option).
  - VAAPI on each other GPU's render node.
  - QSV on each of Intel's: its render nodes on Linux, its adapters on Windows, as the `child_device` QSV runs on.
  - AMF on each of AMD's adapters.
  - VideoToolbox's H.264, HEVC and ProRes on a Mac (ProRes for proxies only: it opens only on Apple's ProRes hardware).
  - Vulkan's ProRes (`prores_ks_vulkan`, FFmpeg 8.1 and later, for proxies only) on each GPU: NVIDIA's through the Vulkan device made from its CUDA device, the others through their render node (Linux) or their adapter's LUID (Windows).
- Exports and proxies take only the GPU in use's (`Availability::in_use`). Detection is cached in `~/.cache/vidcut/encoders.json`, keyed by FFmpeg versions, the system and the GPUs with their drivers. `vidcut encoders list --refresh` tests again.
- **A crash while testing:** `encoders.testing` beside the cache names the process testing, and is removed when it is done. A test that finds one left by a process that is gone leaves Vulkan's encoders untested, the likeliest to crash, and says so.

**VideoToolbox** takes NV12 or P010 frames from memory, or a VideoToolbox decoder's frames as they are (below). It takes its constant quality as 100 − q × 5/3 of H.264's and HEVC's 0–51 (20 is 67), or a bitrate.

**AMD's and Intel's encoders** take NV12 or P010 frames from memory, each on a device on its GPU: QSV on a device of its own there (a VA-API one beneath it on Linux), AMF on a Direct3D 11 device on its adapter. Where a decoder on the same GPU decodes the pieces, VAAPI and AMF take its frames on its device instead (below). What each makes of the settings:
- **AMF:** one quantizer for every frame (`rc=cqp`, as VAAPI's; its `qvbr` counts the other way and stays under a bitrate), or a bitrate with peaks half again as high (`vbr_peak`); preset `quality`; H.264 High (baseline is its `constrained_baseline`), HEVC Main or Main 10. Its H.264 on the Radeon iGPU writes only the matrix (BT.709) into the bitstream, and players take such HD as BT.709.
- **QSV:** its intelligent constant quality (`global_quality`, 1–51 for every codec), or a bitrate with peaks half again as high; preset `medium`; profiles as AMF's. VP9 only at a bitrate: Intel's VP9 has no intelligent constant quality, and FFmpeg holds QSV's constant quantizer to 51, where VP9's goes to 255. Asked for a quality, it says so, and `auto` passes it by.
- **AV1's quality** is on the 0–63 scale of SVT-AV1's CRF and NVENC's CQ: VAAPI and AMF take it as a quantizer index (0–255, VAAPI's in `global_quality`), QSV scaled to its 1–51. VP9's is on libvpx's CRF scale, also 0–63 (default 32), and VAAPI takes it as AV1's.
- **HDR:** the static values come from each frame (`hdr::attach`), and HEVC's carry them at every keyframe and in the container. HDR10+ goes into their packets (§4).

**Each frame's route** is the planner's (`route::plan`). A frame stays on the GPU where its GPU fits it for an encoder there (each backend's `can_fit`). Otherwise it comes down to the CPU, shrunk first where the output shows it smaller. `VIDCUT_DEBUG` says each file's route.

**Frames on the GPU (NVENC):** this, and the GPU encoders themselves, only while `gpu.acceleration` is on (the default). Off, `export.plan` and `export.start` set every video encoder choice to the CPU (`export::as_preferred`), so a preset naming NVENC exports in software, and proxies take x264 or SVT-AV1.
- NVENC is opened on frames in GPU memory (`cuda::CudaFrames`, NV12, or P010 for 10-bit output). Each source piece that the GPU shows as the CPU would decodes on NVDEC and scales with `scale_cuda` (`Output::Cuda`). Frames are picked by the sampling rule as on the CPU. The reader uses FFmpeg's own decoders: FFmpeg would pick libdav1d for AV1, which decodes on the CPU only. `scale_cuda`'s kernels sample a fixed few pixels per output pixel, so a big shrink would alias: it halves with bilinear steps first, each the rounded average of four pixels, then scales with bicubic. FFmpeg 9's `scale_cuda` shrinks with filters instead, which truncate in every pass, so vidcut asks it for the kernels (`use_filters=0`, gotchas.md).
- **Streams:** each export, and each piece of one, works on a CUDA stream of its own (`CudaDevice::with_own_stream`), and the encoder takes a frame once its stream's work is done. On the context's default stream, a wait for Vulkan would hold up everyone else's decoding and encoding.
- **A piece qualifies** when it is a video file NVDEC decodes, or ProRes, which decodes on Vulkan beside it (preview.md §3). Interlaced ones are deinterlaced by the reader, in decode order and before the sampling rule picks frames, as the CPU reader runs `bwdif` (§4): on the GPU with `bwdif_cuda`, and ProRes with `bwdif_vulkan`, where the filter passes the check (§4), and otherwise in memory. Those frames come down whole, the CPU makes their pictures, and the pictures go up to the encoder's frames. The source plans by where the reader gives its frames (`VideoReader::frames_stay`), so it builds no GPU filters for them, and a frame of another kind than its GPU filters take is an error, never read as theirs. A picture of another aspect is fitted and centered on black with `pad_cuda`, at even offsets as FFmpeg's `pad` rounds them for 4:2:0. `pad_cuda` takes no 10-bit frames, so in 10-bit output libplacebo places it (below). The source builds its GPU filters when it opens, and takes the CPU if they won't build. Other pieces, stills and sequences decode and normalize on the CPU (§4), and black frames are made there. They are uploaded to the encoder's frames.
- **Turned pictures** (spec D33), **other matrices** (BT.601: SD, and VP8, which always signals BT.601; BT.2020 SDR) **and full range** go through libplacebo on Vulkan first (`cuda::CudaPictures`), at the source's size, turned upright with its `rotate`: the CUDA filters neither turn pictures nor change their colors. In 10-bit output a picture of another aspect is also scaled and placed by libplacebo, in one pass with the rest. libplacebo can't clear a border in FFmpeg's Vulkan frames, so it gets a black frame of the output's size under the picture as a second input. The CUDA device is one for the whole process, shared by every export and proxy and never closed (`CudaDevice::open`). The Vulkan device is derived from it the first time it's needed, one thread at a time (the Vulkan loader crashes when two make instances at once), and shared too. When a converter fails, its export or proxy fails, and the next converter gets a new Vulkan device, as a device the GPU has lost stays lost (`CudaDevice::forget_vulkan`). Each frame is copied CUDA → Vulkan, converted, and copied back, never leaving the GPU. Because of a libplacebo bug (gotchas.md), the CPU waits for each copy into Vulkan to finish before the frame goes to libplacebo, and for libplacebo's output before the next copy (`cuda::wait_done`; after 5 s the export fails). The input is tagged with the matrix and range the CPU reads (`color::gpu_color`, untagged SD as BT.601) and with the output's primaries and transfer, so only the matrix and range change, as swscale converts.
- **Vulkan's frames** (ProRes) go to libplacebo as they are, on their own device, and it places every picture of them: FFmpeg copies the small picture into NVENC's CUDA frames, the one copy on the GPU (`hw::reaches`). The CPU waits for each frame's decode before it goes in, and holds the frame until libplacebo's output is done, so the decoder doesn't take it back while libplacebo may still read it (the bug above). A frame with alpha (ProRes 4444's) comes down instead: libplacebo blends alpha onto black, where the CPU leaves it out (`cuda::can_fit`).
- **HDR** (HDR sources into SDR, and anything into PQ or HLG) goes through libplacebo too, with the CPU's math (§4) in a user shader (`color::gpu_color`): PQ's EOTF or HLG's inverse OETF and 1.2 gamma channel by channel, linear light with the reference white at 1.0, the gamut matrix, the mobius curve on the brightest channel, negative channels black, and the output's transfer. libplacebo is told the picture is already in the output's primaries and transfer, so it only reads the matrix, scales, places and writes. The shader hooks the scaled picture, as the CPU scales before converting. HDR kept in the same transfer passes through the CUDA filters. libplacebo's own tone mapping can't give the CPU's curve: it lifts black for an SDR display's contrast and maps on intensity (IPT), not the brightest channel.
  - **Half floats:** libplacebo keeps a picture between passes in half floats, and the GPU stores them rounded toward zero, up to almost half a 10-bit code low on PQ. The shader takes the middle of the step a value it reads came from, and moves what it writes half a step up.
- **libplacebo's quirks:** FFmpeg's filter allocated without its Vulkan device converts on the CPU, so it is made by hand with the device set before it starts. It gives out a frame's picture only once it has the next frame, so a filler frame follows each frame and its pictures are dropped (proxies skip it). Each black frame it is given, filler or background, is one it doesn't hold already (`cuda::Blacks`): a Vulkan frame held twice hangs FFmpeg (gotchas.md). Its frame queue takes a frame within a millisecond of the render time as that time's frame, so the timestamps it sees are counts a second apart. A Vulkan device derived from CUDA keeps each plane in its own image, and libplacebo misses P010's 6-bit shift in such planes, so it gets 10-bit frames as P016, laid out the same with all 16 bits used. NVENC reads the top 10 bits. Its blue-noise dither lifts black by most of a 10-bit level, so it rounds. A frame's side data is dropped before it goes in: libplacebo would turn the picture twice by its display matrix, tone-map by HDR metadata and read ICC profiles. The output's HDR metadata is attached after (§4). Compiled shaders are kept in `~/.cache/vidcut/shaders` (`cuda::keep_shaders_in`), so a new converter starts in 20 ms.
- **Black frames** in P010 keep their 10 bits high: written low, they are a green-tinted black below black.
- `hdr_colors_keep_their_matrix` (`tests/export.rs`) exports PQ stripes to SDR and checks their colors. The report counts the frames made on the GPU (`gpu_frames`).

**Decoded on the GPU, made on the CPU** (`export::run_decoding_on`): while `gpu.acceleration` and `gpu.decoding` are on, an export's pieces decode on the GPU the viewer decodes on (`GpuDecoder::best`: NVDEC, with ProRes on Vulkan beside it, VA-API, Direct3D 11 or VideoToolbox), whatever the encoder. They decode on a CUDA stream or device of their own (`GpuDecoder::own`), so their work doesn't wait in line with playback's. Pieces NVENC takes stay on the GPU as above. The frames come down to be made on the CPU, shrunk on the GPU first when they are shown smaller (`hw::Shrinker`: `scale_cuda` or `scale_vulkan` in halving steps, `scale_vaapi`, `scale_vt`, or the video processor, preview.md §6). The GPU's encoders take them back up, except where they stay on the GPU throughout (below). The report counts them (`gpu_decoded`).

**Frames on the GPU (VAAPI):** where an export's or a proxy's VAAPI encoder is on the render node of its VA-API decoder, it opens on that decoder's device (`encode::open_video`'s `shared`). A piece's frames that need nothing but turning and fitting then stay on the GPU from the decoder to the encoder (`Output::FittedOnGpu`, `hw::vaapi::can_fit`, `hw::Fitter`). `transpose_vaapi` turns them upright as the CPU's `transpose` and flips do, `scale_vaapi` scales them into the output's layout (10-bit sources into 8-bit output too), and `pad_vaapi` centers them on black, at even offsets as the CPU pads. Pieces in other colors than the output's (`color::kept`: BT.601, full range, HDR into SDR), interlaced ones (which come down for `bwdif`) and black are made on the CPU and go up. `gpu_frames` counts the frames that stayed.

**Frames on the GPU (AMF):** AMF takes Direct3D 11 textures only of the frames context it was opened with. So where its adapter is the Direct3D 11 decoder's, an export's or a proxy's opens on frames made on that decoder's device (`encode::Upload::d3d11`), and the video processor draws the pieces' frames into them (`hw::d3d11::Scaler::into`): turned upright (where it has the stream rotation), scaled into the output's layout (10 bits into 8 too) and placed on a black background where their aspect differs. The same pieces as VAAPI's stay on the GPU, except 8-bit ones into 10-bit output: AMD's video processor refuses to draw a decoder's NV12 into P010 (`E_INVALIDARG`, `hw::d3d11::can_fit`). A fit that fails leaves the file's frames to the CPU rather than failing the export or proxy. AMD's driver reads a background color given as YCbCr as RGB, so it is given as RGB: 16 of 255 where the frames are told full range (gotchas.md), which is video-range black there, and 0 for PQ's studio range.

**Frames on the GPU (VideoToolbox):** where the pieces decode on VideoToolbox, its encoders open on that decoder's device and take its frames as they are (`encode::Upload::videotoolbox`). The encoder takes each frame's own pixel buffer (videotoolboxenc.c). `transpose_vt` turns the frames upright and `scale_vt` scales them, in their own layout (vf_scale_vt.c). Neither places a picture on black. So pieces of another aspect come down to the CPU, and so do 10-bit pieces for 8-bit output and pieces in other colors than the output's (`hw::vt::can_fit`). VideoToolbox's H.264 encoder takes 8-bit frames only (FFmpeg 9.0.2 lists `videotoolbox_vld`, `nv12` and `yuv420p`), so 10-bit pieces for H.264 come down. Its HEVC encoder takes P010, so 10-bit HEVC stays on the GPU. The pieces that come down, interlaced ones and black are made on the CPU and go up.

**Passes** (spec D45, `video.passes`, `first_pass`, `multipass`, `lookahead`):
- **Who makes two:** x264, x265 and VP9 encode the range twice (`Passes::TwoRuns`). The first run feeds every frame to an encoder opened with `AV_CODEC_FLAG_PASS1`, drops its packets and writes nothing but statistics. The second opens the encoder again with `PASS2` and the statistics, and encodes as in one pass, with the audio. NVENC looks at each frame twice inside one encode (`multipass`, `Passes::TwoInside`). VAAPI, AMF, QSV, ProRes and DNxHR make one. SVT-AV1 makes one in FFmpeg 8.0, whose wrapper ignores the pass flags ([todo](../todo/svt-av1-two-passes.md)).
- **Unset `passes`:** 2 with a target bitrate, 1 with constant quality, where the encoder makes two; one elsewhere. Two asked for where an encoder can't is an error for that encoder, so `auto` moves on to one that can. Two need a bitrate.
- **Statistics:** x264 writes a file it names with the `stats` option (and its MB-tree file). x265 takes `pass` and `stats` in `x265-params`, its path escaped (FFmpeg 8.0's wrapper ignores the flags), and writes a `.cutree` file beside it. Both finish their files when the encoder closes, so the first run's encoder is freed before the second opens. libvpx hands its statistics back in `stats_out` (base64, at the flush). vidcut copies them into an `av_malloc`'d `stats_in` for the second, frees both itself, and keeps them in memory. The files live in a hidden `.<name>.passes` folder beside the output, on the output's disk: x264's and x265's statistics grow to tens of kilobytes a frame at 4K, too much for a RAM-backed `/tmp`. The folder is removed when the export ends, however it ends.
- **First pass (`first_pass`):** `fast` (the default) is x264's `fastfirstpass`, x265 without `slow-firstpass`, and VP9 at speed 4 or faster in the first run. It saves a quarter to a third of x264's time for at most 0.06 dB. `full` gives the first run the second's settings.
- **NVENC:** `multipass` `quarter` (the default, `qres`) or `full` (`fullres`), which adds time for no better result. `lookahead` (`rc-lookahead`) is 32 frames unless set, 0 for none, in both rate controls. It adds 0.1 to 0.9 dB.
- **Progress** reports the run (`pass`, `passes`), each counting its frames. The CLI's bar, a job's `progress` and `eta` cover both, and the Jobs window says "pass 1 of 2". Cancelling in either run stops it, and the `.part` file and the statistics go. A per-clip export makes both runs for each file.
- **Loudness normalization** measures the mix before the second run, as before a one-pass export. It decodes only audio, so it isn't folded into the first run.
- Two passes land within about 1% of the target, where one is off by up to 4% with x264 and x265 and 28% with VP9. `examples/passes.rs` measures a setting (tools.md).

**Details:**
- Quality 0 with libx264 means lossless, which requires the High 4:4:4 Predictive profile.
- SVT-AV1's info logging is silenced through `SVT_LOG=2`, set by the binary at startup.

## 6. Quick Export semantics

- With no range given, export covers the timeline In–Out when both marks are set, otherwise the whole timeline (p. 789).
- With no preset given, the output extension picks one: `h264-master` settings for video containers (container from the extension), and the matching `audio-*` preset for audio extensions.
- **Per-clip export** writes one file per clip in the range, without neighbors' J/L audio, using a name template (`{index:03}-{name}`).
- Existing files are kept unless `overwrite` is set.

## 7. Logging

FFmpeg's own logging defaults to fatal only. Decoders recovering after a seek log harmless errors, and real failures surface as vidcut errors with context. `VIDCUT_AV_LOG` ([tools.md](../tools.md)) shows more.
