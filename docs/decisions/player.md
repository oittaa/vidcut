# The player engine: vidcut's own playback, headless

The GUI's viewer plays through it, one engine for the source and the timeline ([preview.md](preview.md)). This record is the engine itself, tested headless.

**Tests** (no display):
- [`crates/vidcut-av/tests/player.rs`](../../crates/vidcut-av/tests/player.rs): pictures against `frame render` at seeks and steps both ways across cuts, files, codecs, black, stills and image sequences; playing shows every frame in order; the beep lands on its sample; 2×, reverse; pieces at other speeds, at seeks, steps and playing both ways.
- [`tests/player_sound.rs`](../../crates/vidcut-av/tests/player_sound.rs): backward sound reversed to the sample; other speeds both ways keeping the pitch; audio scrubbing: steps play their frame's sound.
- [`tests/player_decodes.rs`](../../crates/vidcut-av/tests/player_decodes.rs): pictures playing on across an edit, after an edit that moves every frame, and stepping back over a keyframe.
- [`tests/player_back.rs`](../../crates/vidcut-av/tests/player_back.rs): playing 300 frames backward on the CPU decodes about 300, as the windows behind are kept whole.

## 1. Threads

| Thread | Does | Never |
|---|---|---|
| **The caller** (the live session, which requests; the viewer thread, which presents) | sends requests, which return at once; `present` looks a picture up | decodes or waits |
| **Decode** (`vidcut-play-video`) | the picture of the frame the viewer is at, then pictures ahead of it | touches the device |
| **Audio** (`vidcut-play-audio`) | mixes the plan's lanes into the ring; paused, a step's frame | waits on the device |
| **Device callback** (the output's thread) | copies from the ring, notes when it will be heard | locks, allocates or decodes |
| **Openers** (`vidcut-open`, the export's, one per reader) | open the next file ahead | |
| **Scrubber** (`vidcut-scrub`, only with a `Scrubber`) | mixes the frame asked for | |

vidcut-av starts them as it starts its other threads (`std::thread::Builder`, named `vidcut-…`). Someone watches what they make, so they keep their priority: the app's own rule (`threads::spawn`, gui.md §9) gives interactive work no other.

**Ending.** Dropping a player joins the decode and audio threads, frees the kept pictures and stops the output; a followed player's windows thread isn't waited for. Each thread's reader goes with it: the GPU's converters first, then the open files with their decoders, then the GPU's decoder. Freeing GPU memory waits its turn on the GPU behind other players ending or decoding, so under `VIDCUT_DEBUG` the player says how long each of these took.

**The player comes first** (`vidcut_av::first`). While a player plays, or decodes the picture its viewer waits for, the app's jobs stop decoding between frames: exports, proxies, filmstrips, pool pictures and analyses. They go on when it stops (`tests/first.rs`). They share the GPU's decoder with the player, and no thread priority reaches that. The GUI's two-up is two players more (preview.md §9).

## 2. Requests: one wanted state, merged and cancelled

- **Wanted state.** One mutex holds what is wanted: the plan, the mode (paused at a frame, or playing from a sample at a speed), lane gains, the mute and audio scrubbing. A request changes it and wakes both threads, which read the newest. Requests that arrive while a thread works merge into the last: nothing queues.
- **Position.** Paused, the frame asked for; playing, the clock (§4). `step` pauses where the viewer is and moves. `play` while playing goes on from the sample heard now at the new speed. `play` forward from the last frame starts over from the first (backward from the first, from the last).
- **Cancelling** (the decode thread's flag is `vidcut_av::cancel`'s, which the decoders check). When the new frame's picture isn't kept, the decode in progress is cancelled if it was only decoding ahead, if the clock runs, or once it has run for a second. Otherwise the seek finishes (paused, or playing before the sound of the play request is heard) and the newest runs next, so a held key or a dragged playhead moves the picture at the speed frames decode and the last request lands exactly (gui.md §4).
- **Overtaken seeks show.** Until the frame the viewer is at is decoded, `present` shows the newest picture a seek landed since the one shown, else the one shown.
- **Playing from a seek** decodes the frame play starts at. The clock moves once its sound is heard. A player late while the clock moves seeks ahead to where the clock will be.
- **After a cancel** the reader forgets where it stands (the next piece of the file seeks), and the file stays open.

## 3. Pictures: decoded YUV, for a shader

- **Picked by the export's reader** (`export::video::VideoFrames` in its `decoded` mode): the sampling rule (export.md §2), deinterlacing, stills and image sequences, so each is the frame export shows. The tests compare every picture's barcode with `frame render`'s.
- **Dissolves** (spec D50): a frame of a mix is its pictures as decoded, the first with those over it and their weights (`Picture::over`). Each comes from a file of its own. Kept pictures are keyed by all of them, and each picture counts in the budget of where its own frame is. Going back, a dissolve's window starts at a keyframe of its first file, and the other decodes on through it.
- **As decoded.** A picture is the decoded frame, held by reference: its own size, its own colors, not turned, in a layout a display takes: 4:2:0 in 8 bits (planar, or NV12 as hardware decoders give it) or 10 (P010, or planar as the CPU's decoders give it), 4:2:2 in 8 or 10 bits planar (DNxHR, ProRes) or in 10 interleaved (P210, as VideoToolbox gives ProRes), or 4:4:4 in 12 bits planar (ProRes 4444, its alpha left out) or in 16 interleaved (P416, as VideoToolbox gives ProRes 4444). Others are brought to the nearest at their own size (`Relayout`): other 4:4:4 and more than 10 bits are resampled to 4:2:0 keeping their matrix and range, and RGB images are coded BT.709 video range. Black is the plan's size in 8-bit 4:2:0. Nothing copies a picture's pixels after the decoder.
- **The display does the rest**, on the GPU: fitting into the viewer as export fits (`Picture::placement`), turning (`rotation`, the display matrix), and YUV to BT.709 RGB from `Picture::colors` (matrix, range, HDR), which are `vidcut_av::color`'s reading of the frame: its tags, or for untagged pictures BT.709 when HD and BT.601 otherwise, BT.2020 when HDR. `Picture::to_rgba` is that whole path on the CPU with the export's own filters: what `frame render` gives and what a shader must match.
- **Decoded on the GPU** when asked (`Config::hardware`, `set_hardware`): every stream the GPU in use decodes (`VideoReader::open_gpu_or_cpu`), ProRes on Vulkan beside NVDEC, the rest on the CPU. A decoder's frames stay in video memory where the display shows that decoder's from there (`Config::gpu_pictures`), and come down to memory otherwise (preview.md §3). A switch of decoder decodes the pictures anew. Each picture says what decoded it (`Picture::decoder`). `Stats::copies` counts the frames its decode thread and the viewer copy down to memory and up to the GPU since the player started, a switch of decoder included.
- **GPU surfaces.** `Picture::gpu` says which GPU decoder's memory holds a picture, and `bytes` what its layout takes (a CUDA picture's: all of its memory, rows padded to CUDA's alignment; a VA-API surface's: what its DRM export says, once for each kind of surface), so the budget counts video memory as it counts memory. The engine never reads pixels. `copied_down` brings a picture to memory, for a display that can't show it from there.
- **Kept by what they show:** the file, its origin, still or sequence, the source position and the rate (black by the plan's size). Pictures survive edits that move them (a ripple), and seeking to a kept one shows it without waiting for the decode thread. Memory budgets hold them (`Config::budget`), one for pictures in memory and one for pictures in video memory, each counted where it lives. Over one, that kind's least recently kept or shown go first, never the prepared picture, and their memory with them: the CPU's decoders decode into buffers of vidcut's own (`buffers.rs`), a frame kept in memory from any other pool (libdav1d's, a filter graph's: the deinterlacer's, a relayout's) is copied into such buffers (`buffers::owned`), NVDEC's kept pictures are copied into memory of their own (`cuda::keep`), and [a todo](../todo/kept-pictures-pools.md) has the decoders whose pools a kept picture still holds. A picture whose surface is a pool's that decoders share (VA-API's on Mesa, `Picture::pooled`) frees nothing when it goes alone: one of another pool than the picture being kept goes with all of its pool's pictures, the prepared one brought down to memory instead, and a prepared picture of such a pool comes down to memory when it is decoded. `Budget::automatic` is a viewer's: the larger of 1 GB and half the computer's memory (POSIX `sysconf`), and half the GPU's memory once the decode thread opens NVDEC (CUDA says how much; VA-API and Direct3D 11 don't, so a gigabyte there). On Apple silicon (`SHARED_MEMORY`) the GPU's memory is the computer's, so an automatic video budget is the memory budget: both kinds count in it, and the least recently kept or shown of either go first. A video budget given keeps its own (`Store::share`). `DEFAULT_BUDGET` (1 GB of each) is for tests and tools. `set_budget` changes them while it plays. A viewer's source and timeline share the budgets, so the windows of the one not shown may go, and its first step back after a switch decodes one again. The prepared picture stays.
- **Forward**, the reader decodes on from frame to frame, and the next file opens ahead on the export's opener thread. Frames the clock has already passed are decoded and not kept. A reader far behind (more than a second) seeks to where the clock will be once the seek is done (the recent seek time × speed) and goes on from there while the clock comes.
- **Ahead:** a quarter of a second × speed while playing (at most a quarter of the budget); paused, three frames when the reader stands right there.
- **Back:** a window from a keyframe up to the frame, kept, at most half the budget. It reaches back over another keyframe only to hold that GOP whole: started between two keyframes, the frames before it decode for nothing. Backward 4K in long GOPs decodes most windows from the keyframe again, so the budget decides whether frames drop.
- **At another speed** (spec D48) a window back is two seconds of a piece (its reader seeks once and finds the keyframe; a backward piece's pictures it makes from keyframes itself, export.md §2), a held piece's its one picture.
- **Playing backward**, a frame with nothing kept there decodes from its own keyframe, so it shows after one GOP's decoding, not two. Behind the window shown two more wait where they fit, and once one is left the one before it decodes. The first behind is the nearest, from its own keyframe, as the playhead may be close. The second holds at least two seconds where keyframes are close and decodes once it fits in the budget beside the frames still to be shown. Before a window decodes, those count as looked at, so the frames shown go first when the budget is full.
- **Around a paused playhead**, whichever way it last went: at least two seconds behind it (at most half the budget), decoded while nothing else is wanted, nearest first, so the first steps back find their pictures. A window goes from the keyframe of the latest frame missing up to it: in video memory, all of it at once; in memory, half a second of it at a time (each kept picture there takes new memory, and a 10-bit one a conversion), so a step right after a seek waits for half a second's frames, not the GOP's. A step back into the window being decoded waits for it (`Busy::Window`), and a step past what is kept decodes such a chunk itself.
- **A seek** decodes from the keyframe before its frame and keeps what it decodes on its way: in video memory all of it, in memory the last half second. The seek's own first picture says which, not the one shown before it. The steps back right after it find their pictures, and the rest behind comes while nothing else is wanted. Finding a window's keyframe takes one seek and one decoded frame, and the window decodes on from there (`VideoReader::from_keyframe`), however far into the GOP it starts.
- **Edits.** `set_plan` keeps the position, the open file (the reader reads the new plan's pieces: a piece of the open file decodes on or seeks) and the kept pictures. The sample rate and channels must stay (the device was opened for them): another is refused before anything changes, and the same plan again changes nothing, so neither cancels a decode. Playing, the sound goes on from the same time. At another frame rate the picture shown stays until the new plan's is decoded.
- **The plan played next.** `prepare(plan, frame)` names a frame of another plan, such as the timeline's while a viewer shows the source. Paused with nothing else to decode, the decode thread decodes it on a reader of its own, made for that picture, so the reader it plays with stays where it stands. A new request cancels it as it cancels decoding ahead. The store holds its picture whatever the budget until another is prepared (`None` lets it go), `Status::prepared` says whether it is decoded, and `on_picture` is called when it lands. `set_plan_at(plan, frame)` then plays that plan from that frame in one change: the picture is there, so the next `present` shows it. A frame not decoded yet keeps the picture shown before until its own lands. Which way the viewer last went is forgotten with a new plan, so no window behind decodes there.

## 4. Sound, and the clock

- **The mix** is the export's (`export::audio::AudioMix`): every lane, clip gains and faders as the plan has them, de-click fades at cuts. The audio thread mixes 10 ms at a time into a ring of a second, keeping a tenth of a second queued. A chunk is never dropped: the thread waits for room.
- **Live faders.** `set_lane_gain` puts a gain on top of a lane's (the Mixer's fader while dragged), heard within a tenth of a second plus the device's latency. A new plan drops them: by then the saved level is in the plan.
- **Mute** writes silence. The clock runs on.
- **The ring** is lock-free, one writer and one reader. Positions count sample frames since the player started and never wrap. Seeks, pauses, new plans and new speeds drop what was queued.
- **Markers** say where each run of ring samples is on the timeline: from ring position *c*, timeline sample *s*, moving *v* timeline samples per ring frame (negative backward), for play request *e*. The end of the plan (or its start, backward) gets one that holds.
- **The device's callback** copies from the ring (silence when it is empty) and records, lock-free, the ring position of its first sample, how many ring frames it took and when the first is heard: now plus the output's latency.
- **The clock** at time *t*: the newest buffer heard by *t*, the ring position as far into it as *t* reaches (no further than the frames it held), and through the markers a timeline sample. The viewer shows the frame holding that sample: frame *j* spans samples `[frame_to_sample(j), frame_to_sample(j+1))`, as the plan counts them.
- **What follows:**
  - Pictures follow the sound as it is heard, the device's latency made up for.
  - When the mix is late, sound and pictures stop together.
  - At the end the clock holds and the player pauses on the last frame.
  - Play starts by showing its first frame until that frame's first sample is heard.
  - `frame_at(t)` reads the clock at any instant without changing anything: a display asks for the frame of its next refresh, a test for exact instants a moment ago (the clock remembers the device's last 64 buffers).
  - No half-frame lead: the sound and the picture meet on the frame's first sample.
- **Backward** the sound plays reversed: the mix is read forward a block at a time (0.2 s of play, one seek of each lane's stream a block), from further and further back, and each block is reversed a sample frame at a time. It is the clock as forward sound is: a beep's first sample is the last of it heard, and the picture moves to the frame before right after it.
- **Other speeds**, both ways, go through the engine's own time stretch (`Stretch`, WSOLA), which keeps the pitch and says where its output came from:
  - The mix is cut into fragments of 21 ms that overlap by half in the output, Hann-shaped and added. Fragment *k* would start *k* hops × speed into the input. It moves by up to 5 ms to where its first half best matches what would have followed the fragment before, so the overlap adds alike sound.
  - Each output block (a hop, 10.4 ms) runs from the middle of one fragment to the middle of the next, and both input positions are known: every block is marked as it is queued, so the clock follows what is heard. A block blends two fragments a hop × |speed − 1| apart in the input, so a sound may be heard up to half that from where its block's mark puts it: 2.6 ms of play at 2×, 3.9 ms at 4×.
  - FFmpeg's `atempo` can't be the clock: it moves its fragments by up to 21 ms where nothing outside can see.
  - At 4× and above a sound much shorter than a fragment may blend away (a 10 ms beep lasts 2.5 ms at 4×). Tones, speech and music keep their pitch.

## 5. Audio scrubbing (spec §4.5, decision D42)

- **Frame steps and paused seeks** play the sound of the frame arrived at, when `audio_scrubbing` is on (the default; `set_audio_scrubbing`): its samples of the mix (every lane, gains, faders, lane gains on top), faded in and out over 3 ms. A drag of the playhead is paused seeks, so it scrubs too. Muted, nothing plays. Playing, steps don't happen and seeks don't scrub.
- **Forward, either way:** a step back plays its frame forward.
- **One at a time:** the audio thread keeps the newest frame's sound until the one in the ring has (all but) played out, then queues it whole, so a sound is never cut and a newer one replaces any still waiting. Held keys play frame after frame, steps faster than that skip to the newest, and nothing piles up. The mix seeks per frame, and stepping on decodes on.
- **`Scrubber`** does the same without a player, for a viewer whose pictures come from elsewhere: `Scrubber::new(rate, channels, output)`, `set_plan` on an edit, `play(frame)` for each step, `set_lane_gain` for a fader being dragged. Its own thread mixes, and its own output plays.
- **Quiet seeks:** `seek_quietly` moves without the frame's sound, for moves the viewer makes by itself (opening at a remembered frame, after an edit).
- **Following:** `follow(frame, speed)` is a quiet seek by a viewer that moves the position itself at `speed` (a trim's two-up during a run, preview.md §9). Paused there, the decode thread works as playing at that speed does: ahead going forward, windows behind going back, and the player comes first. Standing still, it keeps 8 frames ahead, not 3. Its windows, behind it at rest and while it follows back, decode on a thread of their own (`vidcut-play-windows`, started when it is first followed) with a second reader, its own cancel flag and busy state: one window at a time across all followed players, none while any moves forward, so opening a file or a window never holds up the reader that decodes on forward. Running forward it keeps every frame it decodes, so catching up leaves no holes for a run back. Shrunk pictures' windows decode from their keyframe at once, not half a second at a time. `shrink_to(size)` has the pictures of a run and of windows behind come down from the GPU shrunk to fit `size`, kept apart from those as decoded; a lookup takes either, as decoded first. A size more than an eighth off forgets the shrunk ones. A decode ahead goes on when the new frame is up to a second ahead, on its way. A seek, step, play or pause stands still again.

## 6. Outputs

`AudioOutput` starts pulling from the engine's `AudioSource` on a thread of its own and stops when dropped.
- **`CpalOutput`**: an output device through cpal (ALSA on Linux, which PipeWire and PulseAudio serve; WASAPI on Windows; Core Audio on macOS). It plays on the system's default, or the one the preferences' `player.audio_device` names by cpal's device id (the same across runs; `output_devices` lists them), found by its id exactly as listed (cpal's own lookup misses ALSA's, gotchas.md). While that device isn't connected it plays on the default, and `PlaysOn` says which device plays. Names repeat (ALSA's outputs of one card share its name), ids don't: `output_devices` tells them apart by their description's next line ("Front output / input"), else numbers them. A changed choice opens the engine anew on its device, paused where it was, `f32` at the plan's rate and channels. The latency is the callback's playback time minus its callback time. The stream lives on its own thread, as it can't move between threads on every platform. Device errors show in `Status::error`. A device that won't open at the plan's rate isn't resampled for.
- **`PacedOutput`**: a stand-in that pulls in real time, each buffer heard a set latency after it is due. It can record what it pulled and when it would be heard.
- **`ManualOutput`**: pulls when a test says, so the test sets the clock.
- **cpal** is Apache-2.0 (with the MIT/Apache `alsa` crate and the MIT `alsa-sys`): compatible with MIT distribution. It builds against ALSA's headers, `libasound2-dev`.
