# Cut page behavioral spec

vidcut copies the behavior of DaVinci Resolve's **Cut page** by default. This document describes that behavior in our own words.

It gives the page numbers of the *DaVinci Resolve 21.1 Reference Manual*. Most pages are in its Cut chapters (33–39, pp. 635–790). Pages of other chapters are marked. The printed page numbers are equal to the PDF page numbers.

[deviations.md](deviations.md) lists where vidcut is different. §12 decides the points that the manual leaves open. §13 gives the order in which a picture is processed.

**Reading the manual:**
- Extract the text with `pdftotext -f 635 -l 790 <manual>.pdf -`.
- The developer's copy is in `$VIDCUT_TEST_DATA` (default `~/Downloads/vidcut-tmp`).
- Do not copy its text into the repository.

**Legend:** **(later)** is not built yet, and comes after the work in progress. [todo/cut-page-gaps.md](todo/cut-page-gaps.md) lists the Cut-page behavior that vidcut does not have.

## 1. Scope

**In scope:**
- media pool, viewer, dual timeline
- editing and trimming, speed changes and speed keyframes (D48)
- dissolves: cross dissolves, fades to and from black, audio cross fades (§8.11)
- clip/track audio levels and waveforms
- volume keyframes of a clip (§9, D81)
- keyframes of Transform, Crop and Composite (§8.15, D75)
- Voice Isolation, free (X64)
- ducking, the Ducker Track FX (§9)
- subtitle tracks: cues, subtitle files in and out, Create Subtitles from Audio (§8.12)
- titles: Text, the three lower thirds and Scroll (§8.14)
- a clip's Transform, Crop, Dynamic Zoom, composite mode and opacity (§8.15)
- generators: the twelve kinds of the Generators section (§8.17)
- the input LUT and the output LUT, from a `.cube` file ([§13](#the-lut-tables))
- Quick Export, plus our Export dialog

**Later (later):**
- other effects, other transitions, other titles, other generators
- keyframes other than Transform, Crop, the clip volume and the Position, Zoom, Rotation and Opacity of a title
- color ([todo](todo/auto-color.md)), Fusion
- the display LUT, and LUT files other than `.cube` ([todo](todo/luts.md))
- Stabilization ([todo](todo/stabilization.md))
- Lens Correction ([todo](todo/lens-correction.md))
- Smart Reframe and Close Up ([todo](todo/smart-reframe.md))
- angle switches ([todo](todo/sync-bin.md)), Live Overwrite
- making a compound clip from timeline clips ([todo](todo/compound-clips.md))
- Replay, voiceover
- AI tools other than Create Subtitles from Audio and Voice Isolation: the other voice and dialogue tools
- Mixer FX other than the Ducker, Paste Attributes, guides
- interchange formats: DRT, AAF, XML

## 2. Layout (pp. 636–637)

- **Main areas:**
  - The Media Pool is on the left. A single Viewer is on the right.
  - The timeline area is across the bottom.
  - You can move the seam between them, and change the timeline height.
- **Top bar:**
  - On the left: the Media Pool and Mixer tabs (p. 649).
    - A click on the Media Pool tab while the pool shows hides the pool. The Viewer takes its room (p. 636).
    - With the pool hidden, the tabs are in a row above the Viewer. A click on a tab shows what that tab holds.
    - The edit-function buttons go with the pool, as they do for the other tabs. vidcut: no measurement tells where they go in Resolve.
    - `gui.close media_pool` hides the pool. `gui.show media_pool` shows it.
  - On the right: Quick Export and the Full Screen viewer.
    - vidcut's Full Screen button is on the viewer (deviation X86).
  - A Transitions tab shows the transitions in the place of the Media Pool (p. 759, §8.11).
  - A Titles tab shows the title kinds in the place of the Media Pool (p. 762).
  - An Effects tab shows the video effects in the place of the Media Pool (p. 765, §8.16).
  - The Effects tab also has a Generators page (p. 766, §8.17).
  - The Inspector button is at the top right of the UI toolbar (§8.15). vidcut has an Inspector tab beside the Titles tab instead (deviation X70).
  - A Keyframes tab sits beside the Inspector ([X87](deviations.md#x87-the-keyframes-tab-has-an-ease-menu)).
  - The Timeline menu opens that tab.
  - `gui.show keyframes` opens that tab.
  - The tab shows the item selected first, a clip or a subtitle.
  - When that item leaves the selection, the tab shows the next item selected, not the earliest on the timeline. Clips and subtitles share this order. Resolve Studio 21.1.2 shows this with three titles.
  - **Tracks** (pp. 1255–1256):
    - Each value that can have keyframes has a track, in a group for its section.
    - Volume is a track. Position is one track. Zoom is one track. The anchor point is one track.
    - Speed is one track, in a Speed group. It shows the speed keyframes of the clip (D48, [X94](deviations.md#x94-speed-keyframes-and-the-mark-menus-keyframe-items)).
    - The ⋯ menu has Display All Parameters and Display Parameters with Keyframes. All is the default.
    - A click on the name of a group opens or closes it. The ⋯ menu also has Expand All Parameters and Collapse All Parameters.
  - **Track controls** (p. 1257):
    - The curve button shows the curve of the value under its track.
    - The Keyframe button adds a keyframe at the playhead, also outside the clip (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2). On a keyframe, it removes that keyframe, as the Inspector's button does.
    - The Keyframe button is orange on a keyframe (p. 1250), in the Inspector too.
    - The arrows beside it go to the previous and the next keyframe of the value, also outside the clip (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
    - On the Speed row, the button adds a keyframe only inside the clip, after its first frame. The arrows go only to keyframes inside the clip.
    - Reset Keyframes removes all keyframes of the track.
  - **Time** (p. 1258):
    - The time ruler shows timecode. A click or a drag on it moves the playhead.
    - The tracks start at the range of the clip. Fit Clip goes back to that range.
    - Detail Zoom zooms in four times, around the playhead.
    - The zoom slider sets the zoom, around the middle of the range.
    - Ctrl and the mouse wheel zoom at the pointer. Shift and the mouse wheel move the range.
    - A scroll bar under the tracks shows the part of the clip in the range. A drag on it moves the range.
    - A click on the scroll bar outside that part moves the range to the click.
    - An arrow at the edge of a track marks its keyframes outside the range, for example after a trim.
  - **Edits:**
    - The rules of the clicks, the box, the steps and the paste with no page number are measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2.
    - A drag on a keyframe moves it in time. A drag on a curve point also changes the value.
    - A drag on a group track moves that group's keyframes at that time.
    - A drag on an In or Out handle changes that handle. A handle value moves the key's own value.
    - On Position, Zoom and the anchor point, Y keeps the handle frames and uses a value offset of 0.
    - One click selects one keyframe. A click on an empty part clears the selection.
    - Ctrl-click (Cmd-click on the Mac) adds a keyframe to the selection (p. 1257). Ctrl-click on a selected keyframe removes it from the selection.
    - Shift-click selects that keyframe alone, as a plain click does.
    - A drag from an empty part of the tracks draws a box (p. 1257). On release, the keyframes in the box are the selection.
      - The box replaces the selection, also with Shift or Ctrl.
      - One box takes the keyframes of all the values in it.
    - Delete removes the selected keyframes (p. 1258).
    - Mark > Move Selected Keyframes Left and Right move the selected keyframes by one frame (p. 1258). They send `keyframes.move`.
    - Mark > Move Selected Keyframes Up and Down change their values by one step. They send `keyframes.set` with `by`.
      - The manual says that a step is one unit (p. 4343). The step of each value is measured.
      - Position, the anchor point and each crop side: 0.001 of the picture. On a 1920 px wide picture, this is 1.92 px.
        - Position X counts in the timeline frame. The anchor point X and the crop count in the fitted picture.
      - Zoom: 0.2, that is 20%. Rotation: 0.02°. Pitch and Yaw: 0.001. Crop softness: 0.2. Opacity: 0.2%. Volume: 0.2 dB.
      - Resolve's Inspector shows Pitch and Yaw with three decimals. vidcut does the same, so one step shows.
      - On Position, Zoom and the anchor point, the step goes to X and to Y.
      - A value stops at its limit. The rest of that step is lost.
    - A click on the name of a value selects its row alone. Ctrl-click and Shift-click do the same.
      - A click on a keyframe also adds its row to the selected rows. A click on an empty part does not change the rows.
      - A selected row has a lighter background over its full width.
      - The tab and the tray share the selected rows. A new clip in the tab clears them.
    - Mark > Add Keyframe (Ctrl+[) adds a keyframe at the playhead on each selected row, at the value there. It sends `keyframes.add`.
      - A row with a keyframe at the playhead does not change.
      - The Volume row takes a keyframe also outside the clip (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2). A held key adds once.
      - The Speed row takes a keyframe only inside the clip, after its first frame.
      - The manual says that the context picks the value (pp. 1254–1255). In Resolve Studio 21.1.2, the selected row picks it.
      - The value changed last in the Inspector and the curve in view do not pick it.
    - Mark > Delete Keyframe (Alt+]) removes the selected keyframes. With none selected, it removes the keyframes of the selected rows at the playhead.
    - Mark > Delete All Keyframes removes the selected keyframes. With none selected, it asks first. Then it removes all the keyframes of the selected rows.
      - The question names the values, the number of keyframes and the clip ([X94](deviations.md#x94-speed-keyframes-and-the-mark-menus-keyframe-items)). Cancel has the focus.
    - These three items act on the clip that the tab shows.
    - With the tab and the tray closed, they act on the speed keyframes of the selected clips (D48, X94).
    - After a click in the Keyframes tab or the keyframe tray, Ctrl+C and Ctrl+X copy or cut the selected keyframes (p. 1258).
    - Ctrl+V there pastes them on the selected clips (p. 1258).
      - The first keyframe goes at the playhead. The others keep their distance from it.
      - With more than one clip selected, each clip takes the keyframes at the same frames from its start. The earliest selected clip sets these frames.
      - A pasted keyframe can be past the clip's ends (p. 1258).
      - `keyframes.copy`, `keyframes.cut` and `keyframes.paste` do this.
    - Keyframes and clips share one clipboard. A copy of keyframes replaces the copied clips, and a copy of clips replaces the copied keyframes.
      - Each place pastes only its own kind. Ctrl+V in the timeline pastes no keyframes, and Ctrl+V in the tab or the tray pastes no clips.
    - A right-click on a keyframe sets its ease (X87). The anchor point's keyframes take no ease (D92).
    - A double-click on an empty part of a track adds a keyframe there ([X108](deviations.md#x108-a-double-click-on-a-row-of-the-keyframes-tab-adds-a-keyframe)).
    - The bar under the tracks shows the value, time and ease of the selected keyframe (X90).
    - A keyframe that the Keyframe button or a double-click adds is selected. Thus the bar shows it at once.
  - **Keyframe tray** (p. 1259):
    - The Keyframes button of the lower timeline's toolbar shows a tray under the tracks of the lower timeline. The upper timeline does not change.
    - The tray has a track for each value of the item selected first that has keyframes. Its time is the time of the lower timeline.
    - Its tracks work as the tracks of the Keyframes tab, with Reset Keyframes and without the curve. They share the selection of the tab.
    - `gui.show keyframe_tray` opens the tray. `gui.close keyframe_tray` closes it.
    - Resolve's tray has more. vidcut does not have these parts yet ([todo](todo/keyframes.md)):
      - A divider that changes the height of the tray.
      - Group tracks for each effect, under a track with the clip's name.
      - A curve view, and a Parameters menu that chooses the shown values.
      - With no clip selected, the clip under the playhead. With more than one clip selected, a group for each clip.
  - The Sync Bin tab ([§7.4](#74-sync-bin-pp-708710-measured-in-resolve-studio-2112)).
  - vidcut's bar also has Import, Export and Jobs (deviation X3), and Help (X23).
  - Items that do not fit a narrow window go into a ⋯ menu at the right end of the bar (D72).
- **Below the Media Pool:** the edit-function buttons (§7), and Cut and Dissolve (§8.11).
- **Viewer:**
  - The mode buttons are at the top left. The Duration field is at the top right.
  - Along the bottom (p. 640): the jog control, Previous Edit, Stop, Play, Next Edit, Loop, Mark In, Mark Out and the timecode.
  - An audio meter is on the right (p. 642).
  - Items that do not fit a narrow viewer go into a ⋯ menu (D72).
- **Timeline menus** (pp. 645–646):
  - **Timeline Options:**
    - Ripple On, Snap, Fixed Playhead, Trim to Audio, Trim with Safe Edit.
    - Display Clip Names, Durations and Status.
    - Minimize Subtitle Track, Boring Detector.
  - **Timeline Actions:**
    - Add Video/Audio/Subtitle Track, Split Clips, Join Match Edit Clips, Add Marker.
    - The Add Tracks window of Resolve Studio 21.1.2 takes 0 to 99 video tracks and 0 to 999 audio tracks. `track.add` takes the same.
    - Detect Scene Cuts, Add Dissolve to All Edits, Clear Transition from All Edits.
    - vidcut also has Add Subtitle (deviation X44), Create Subtitles from Audio (X46), Re-time Subtitles to Speech (X49) and Ripple Delete Silence.
  - **Edit Actions:** Trim Start to Playhead, Trim End to Playhead.
- **Timeline buttons** (p. 649): Ripple On, Dynamic Trim, Split, Marker. After them is the Keyframes button, which shows the keyframe tray (p. 1259).
- **Upper timeline:**
  - The track-number buttons to its left select the target track.
  - The Audio Only and Video Only toggles are there too (pp. 643, 694).
    - The two cannot both be on.
    - `gui.only` sets them. The choice lasts for this session.

## 3. Media Pool (Ch. 34, pp. 653–675)

- **Import** (p. 654):
  - Ctrl+I, the Import Media button, or right-click > Import Media opens a file dialog. The dialog can select many files.
  - **Import Media Folder**, the button beside Import Media, opens a folder dialog (pp. 654, 658; D53):
    - It brings the folder in as a bin inside the open bin, with a bin for each folder in it.
    - Then it opens that bin.
    - Resolve Studio 21.1.1's Cut page has no File-menu or right-click item for it.
    - Keyboard Customization lists no command with that name.
    - vidcut adds both items (deviation X25).
  - **Drag and drop:**
    - Clips dragged from the file system go into the Media Pool, or into a bin in the Bin list (p. 364).
    - For the Cut page, the manual says macOS only (p. 372).
    - Folders dragged into the Bin list come in as bins (p. 365).
    - vidcut takes files and folders dropped anywhere on its window into the open bin (deviation X66).
    - It brings in the files as Import Media does, and each folder as Import Media Folder does.
  - In a project made without a format, the first video sets the format without a question (D37).
- **Stills and image sequences** (pp. 114, 366, 1194):
  - An image file, or a GIF or WebP of one frame, comes in as a still (D38).
  - Numbered image files come in as one clip (D39).
- **Remove** (p. 654):
  - Delete or Backspace removes the pool entry, after a confirmation.
  - Only the pool entry goes. The files on disk stay.
- **Views** (pp. 638, 661–665):
  - **Thumbnail:** a hover scrubs the clip, and I/O mark its source In/Out. A click on the lower-right corner of a thumbnail shows its metadata (deviation X60).
  - **Filmstrip:** each clip is a row of its pictures, as wide as the pool, under its name. Hover and I/O work as on a thumbnail.
  - **List:** a column for each field. A click on a header sorts by that field.
  - **Metadata** (pp. 661–663): a card for each clip, in as many columns as fit the pool.
    - A thumbnail is at the left of the card. A hover on it scrubs the clip, and I/O mark its source In/Out.
    - Beside the thumbnail, from the top:
      - The main description, which follows the sort (p. 662). Timecode gives the start timecode. Each other sort gives the clip name.
      - The start timecode and the camera name. A file with no timecode shows 00:00:00:00. A clip with no camera name shows `---`.
      - A subtitle file shows its cue count and its length on that line, and no camera name.
      - The clip name.
    - Sorted by Clip Name, the clips are in clusters by the first letter of the name, each under a heading (pp. 662–663).
      - Inside a cluster, the order is by timecode. The Source Tape takes this order.
    - Sorted by Date Modified, the clusters are the days of the file's modification time (p. 662).
    - Sorted by Timecode, Date Imported or Duration, there are no clusters. vidcut keeps no creation date and no date of import.
    - Descending reverses the whole order (p. 663).
    - vidcut keeps no Date Created, scene, shot, take or comment ([todo](todo/cut-page-gaps.md)). Thus the card and the main description leave them out.
    - `pool.set` with `view` `metadata` opens this view.
  - A sound file shows its waveform.
  - **Metadata Display** (p. 665): a tag icon beside the search field shows metadata of the selected clip at the top of the pool (deviation X60). The metadata is the camera name, scene, shot and take, frame rate and resolution.
- **Sort and search** (pp. 666–667):
  - Sort Media By has Clip Name, Date Imported, Date Modified and Timecode (D59), ascending or descending.
  - This order is also the Source Tape order.
  - The search field filters live, through the open bin and the bins inside it.
- **Bins** (pp. 655–656, 377):
  - The Master bin is the top level.
  - The pool shows the clips of the open bin, and its bins as folders.
  - A path at the top (Master › Interviews › Day 1), the Bins menu and a double-click open any bin.
  - New Bin is on the File menu (Shift+Ctrl+N), on the Bins menu and on the right-click menu of the pool.
  - The menu of a bin has Rename Bin and Remove Bin. Remove Bin keeps the clips of the bin (deviation X6).
  - Clips and bins dragged onto a bin, or onto a level of the path, move into it.
  - The menu of a clip has Move to Bin and Create Bin With Selected Clips (p. 656).
  - Imports go into the open bin (p. 377).
  - Source Tape strings together the clips of the open bin and of its bins, in the sort order (pp. 680, 696).
- **Clip Color** (pp. 667–668):
  - Right-click clips in the pool or the timeline. Then select one of 16 colors, or Clear Color.
  - A timeline clip has its own color. If it has none, it has the color of its pool item.
- **Camera name** (p. 706):
  - A camera can record its name in the file, and Resolve reads it. The Camera # field of the Metadata Editor sets it by hand.
  - A media item has a camera name (`camera`). It is text, such as a letter or a number.
  - The Camera row of Clip Info edits it. `media.set` sets it on one or more items. An empty name clears it.
  - `media.get` and the metadata strip show it.
  - vidcut does not read the name from a file. No measurement shows which file tags give it.
- **Multicam layout** (p. 1037):
  - `multicam.layout` tells where the files of a multicam clip lie. It changes nothing, and the project does not store the result.
  - The answer has rows. A named row holds all files of one camera name. Named rows come first, in camera order.
  - Rows with no name follow. A file with no name never joins a named row.
    - By `timecode`, files with no name go in timecode order. With one timecode, they go in the order of the request.
    - By `timecode`, each of these files goes on the first row with no name where it does not overlap. Touching is no overlap.
    - Example: tags 01:00:00:00, 01:00:05:00, 01:00:10:00 and 01:01:00:00 give three rows. Row 1 holds the first and the last file.
    - By `in`, `out` or `sound`, each file with no name has its own row, although two of them may not overlap.
    - A file with no name and no picture has its own row, after the other rows. It has sound only.
  - `sync` is `timecode`, `in`, `out` or `sound`.
  - By `timecode`, each file starts at its start timecode. A file with no timecode starts at 00:00:00:00.
    - The length is the union of the files, with any gaps. A gap of hours is not an error.
    - The start timecode of the whole is the earliest file.
  - By `in` or `out`, the marks of the items line up. The start timecode of the whole is 01:00:00:00.
    - One mark counts for each row. By `in`, it is the In of the file with the earliest timecode. By `out`, it is the Out of the file with the latest timecode.
    - The other files of the row keep their timecode distance from that file. The order of the request plays no part.
  - **The row rule:** files of one named row do not overlap.
    - Files go in timecode order. With one timecode, they go in the order of the request.
    - By `sound`, files go by their places from the sound. A tie goes by the order of the request ([deviation X125](deviations.md#x125-sync-by-sound-uses-time-order-on-a-row)).
    - A file keeps only the part after everything before it. A file with no such part is not on the row, and `dropped` names it.
    - A file does not move. It loses frames at its start, and `head` gives those frames.
    - Example: tags 01:00:05:00 and 01:00:10:00 on one row, 750 frames each. The first stays whole. The second keeps 125 frames from 01:00:35:00.
  - An item with marks uses only the frames between them. The shift stays. `head` and `tail` give the frames that it does not use.
  - `full_extents` uses each whole file. By `in` or `out`, its marks only line it up.
  - A request that names a file twice counts it once.
  - A still takes part. It starts at 00:00:00:00 and has the length of a new still.
  - An item with one mark uses the frames from In to its end, or from its start to Out.
  - By `in`, an item with no In lines up at its first frame. By `out`, an item with no Out lines up at its end.
- **Sync by sound** (pp. 706–707):
  - `sync` `sound` lines up files by their sound. `multicam.layout` and `multicam.create` take it.
  - A shift is in whole frames. Half a frame rounds up.
  - One shift holds for the whole file. vidcut does not follow drift. The shift falls between the error at the start and the error at the end.
  - The start timecode of the whole is 01:00:00:00.
  - A file that matches no other file stays at the start of the whole. `unmatched` names it. A file with no sound is such a file.
    - Thus with two files and no match, both are at the start and `unmatched` names both.
  - Two sets of files that do not match each other do not overlap. The later set starts one frame after the earlier one ends.
  - The whole files line up. An item with marks then uses only the frames between them, at the place of those frames.
    - The empty front stays. Thus marks at 100 to 400 on both files, 250 frames apart, give 650 frames.
  - vidcut compares the first sound stream of each file, with all its channels. No measurement shows which channel the Cut page uses.
  - Each file is placed by its own sound. The row rule then acts in the order of the places ([deviation X125](deviations.md#x125-sync-by-sound-uses-time-order-on-a-row)).
    - Thus files c1 and c2 keep c1 whole, and c2 loses its head. Files c2 and c1 give the same result.
    - Files c3 and c1 keep both files, because c3 starts after c1 ends.
    - Of two files with one place and one length, the first in the order of the request stays whole. The second file is not on the row.
  - `multicam.layout` also gives `pairs`: for each two files the shift, the score and whether they match.
  - While the GUI runs, `multicam.create` by sound is a job.
- **Synced group** (p. 708):
  - `multicam.create` stores files as a synced group, a pool item with the name "Media Sync", the date and the time. `name` gives another name.
  - It keeps the rows of `multicam.layout` with `full_extents`. A file's marks and its start timecode do not change.
  - The group stores its rows. A camera name that changes later does not move a file until Auto Sync reads the names again.
  - A stored file has its visible part: the frames lost at its head and at its tail. A file with no visible part is not in the group.
  - The group keeps `dropped`: the files that the row rule or a drag took off its rows.
  - The files stay in their bins. `bin` tells where the group goes, and the top level is the default.
  - A file can be in more than one group. The newest group that holds the file counts.
  - `multicam.list` gives each group with its layout. `media.get` and `media.list` give `group` for a file in a group.
  - `multicam.rename` and `multicam.move` act on the group. `multicam.remove` removes the group, and its files stay.
  - A group can have one file.
  - `media.remove` also takes the file out of its groups. A group with no file left goes too ([deviation X128](deviations.md#x128-removing-a-file-takes-it-out-of-its-groups)).
  - No command places a group on the timeline.
  - The pool does not show a group as an item. A file in a group has a blue sync badge.
  - `multicam.set` sets the rows and the sync locks of a group ([§7.5](#75-sync-clips-window-pp-706708-measured-in-resolve-studio-2112)). `multicam.create` takes them as `rows`, and the group then starts at 01:00:00:00.
    - They refuse files of one row that overlap, and a file with no visible part. A file that no row names is not in the group.
  - `multicam.set` and `multicam.create` with `rows` apply the rules of far gaps and of the earliest row. `at` plus `hidden` is the place of the first visible frame in real time.
  - `multicam.create` without `rows` keeps each gap. Its start is the first timecode, or 01:00:00:00 by sound, In or Out.
  - `multicam.place` moves one file as a drag does, by the rules of overlap in [§7.5](#75-sync-clips-window-pp-706708-measured-in-resolve-studio-2112). It refuses a file on a row with the sync lock.
- **Input Color Space** (pp. 228, 230):
  - The color tags of a file tell how to read the colors of its pictures.
  - For a file with wrong tags, right-click the media in the pool. Select BT.709, BT.601, BT.2100 PQ or BT.2100 HLG to read it that way.
  - As Tagged uses the tags again.
  - The viewer, the thumbnails and the exports follow this setting (D35).
- **Super Scale** (pp. 288, 432–433; deviation [X91](deviations.md#x91-super-scale-uses-the-upscaler-of-the-gpu)):
  - Super Scale enlarges the pictures of a media item with a better upscaler before they are fitted.
  - It is a setting of the media item. Thus it applies to every clip of that item (p. 433).
  - Right-click the media in the pool, or use the Super Scale section of the Inspector.
  - Super Scale is Off or On.
  - Its Upscaler is Project Settings, the default, AI or Classic. Project Settings has the upscaler of the project: Auto, AI or Classic.
    - AI is a neural network on the GPU. It has no settings.
    - Classic has a Sharpness of Low, Medium or High, which selects the upscaler.
  - The first time that a change needs an AI upscaler, vidcut asks to download its weights.
  - AI takes the picture in the timeline color space (§13). In an SDR timeline, HDR media is tone-mapped first.
  - In an HDR timeline, AI Slow refuses HDR media, and the Inspector shows a warning (X98).
  - A placed picture is made at most 4 times as large as its source, before it is placed.
  - A picture that fills the frame takes the better upscaler for all of its enlargement.
  - Super Scale works only where a picture is enlarged. A picture that is made smaller is scaled as before.
  - View > Show Super Scale shows it in the viewer (`viewer.super_scale`). It is off by default, as it costs playback speed.
  - With AI, the viewer then shows the result of the AI upscaler, as the export makes it.
  - `media.set` sets `super_scale`, `upscaler` and `sharpness`. With `download`, it downloads the weights first.
- **Relinking media** (pp. 668–669):
  - An item whose file is missing has the mark MISSING.
  - Its clips are red on the timeline (pp. 509, 530), and the viewer shows Media Offline.
  - Relink Media finds the files in a folder that you select, and in its subfolders.
  - Relink… on a clip selects one file.
  - vidcut checks each file that it finds (deviation X1).
- **Interaction:**
  - A click selects a clip.
  - In Source Clip mode, the Viewer shows the clip. In Source Tape mode, the playhead jumps to it (pp. 680, 704).
  - A double-click opens it as Source Clip (p. 680).
- **Selection** (p. 719):
  - Click, Shift-click range, Ctrl-click toggle, Ctrl+A.
  - A drag from an empty part of the pool draws a box. The box replaces the selection with the clips that it touches.
  - With Ctrl held, the box adds each clip that is not selected, and removes each clip that is selected.
  - vidcut: the choices below have no page and no measurement.
    - A drag on a clip stays a drag of that clip (§7.3). A drag on a bin stays a drag of that bin.
    - The selection changes on release. A box that touches nothing clears the selection.
    - The box takes clips, not bins.
    - Shift has no effect on the box.
    - The box does not move the Viewer, as Ctrl-click and Shift-click do not.
    - The wheel scrolls the pool during a drag, and the box keeps its start. The pool does not scroll by itself at its edge.
  - `selection.set` with `media` sets the same selection by request.

## 4. Viewer (pp. 639–640, 679–693, 713)

### 4.1 Modes
- **Source Clip** (p. 680):
  - The Viewer shows the selected pool clip.
  - A scroll area under the picture stands for the whole clip. It has the playhead, a zoomed-in waveform and In/Out handles that you can drag.
  - vidcut draws the waveform of the whole clip across the scroll area, from the bottom edge up.
    - With marks, the waveform is green between In and Out, and gray outside them.
    - Without marks, the waveform is green.
    - The waveform does not zoom ([gaps](todo/cut-page-gaps.md)).
  - Jog In and Jog Out controls at its ends move the marks frame by frame (p. 692).
    - Jog In is at the left of the scroll area. Jog Out is at the right.
    - A drag moves that mark. 8 pixels is one frame (D97).
    - A click moves that mark one frame later.
    - `media.marks.set` sets a clip mark. `tape.marks.set` sets a tape mark.
- **Source Tape** (pp. 680–681, 704–706):
  - All pool clips play end to end as one tape, in the pool sort order.
  - The clip under the playhead becomes selected in the pool.
- **Multi Source** (pp. 679, 682, measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2):
  - The Viewer shows the video files and stills of the current bin and its subfolders in a grid. All of them follow one playhead.
  - `viewer.set` with `multi_source` opens it. It needs no synced group and no timeline.
  - An audio file gets no cell and is not counted. The title is the count and the bin, for example "3 Clips - S1".
  - Files line up by file timecode. A saved synced group plays no part.
  - The scroll area stands for all the material, and it closes the gaps between files.
  - A grid cell:
    - Each camera name has one cell. Each file with no camera name has its own cell.
    - Named cameras come first, by name. The others follow in pool order.
    - The label is "Camera", then the name. A camera with no name counts from 1 among the unnamed cameras.
    - The number at the top left stays with the camera.
    - A camera with no frame at the playhead has no cell, and the grid closes up. The measurements show 24 cameras with 15 cells in a grid of 4 by 4.
    - Two files of one camera at one time show the first file.
  - The grid has the round-up of the square root of its cells as columns. It has no limit on cells.
  - One camera is active. It plays its sound, edits use it, and it has a red outline and a red corner.
    - The first camera is the default.
    - When the active camera has no frame at the playhead, the first cell becomes active. It does not come back by itself.
  - A click on a cell, or a number key, opens that camera alone (`viewer.set` with `angle` and `alone`).
    - The title is the file name, the pool selects the file, and the position stays.
    - A number key also changes the camera in that view.
    - The close button returns to the grid, with that camera active.
  - In and Out in the grid are marks of the viewer (`viewer.marks.set`, `viewer.marks.clear`). They are not on the files (p. 682).
    - A change of bin clears them, sets the position to 0 and makes the first camera active.
    - Opening a camera alone gives its file these marks, cut at the ends of the file. With no marks, the file loses its marks.
    - In and Out in the view of one camera are on that file only.
    - The Duration field takes a typed length (`viewer.marks.set` with `duration`). The Out moves to the In plus it. With no In, the In goes to the first frame. The marks hold exactly the typed length.
  - An edit in the grid takes the marks' part of the active camera. The marks of the files play no part, and the viewer's marks stay:
    - An In with no Out takes the file to its end.
    - An Out with no In counts position 0 as the In.
    - Marks outside the file: nothing happens.
    - With no marks, Append does nothing. Smart Insert adds nothing, and the playhead moves to the nearest edit. Ripple Overwrite, Place On Top and Source Overwrite take the whole file, with no empty part.
    - An In before the file: the part before the file stays empty on the timeline, before the clip (`lead`).
      - Append: after the last clip.
      - Smart Insert: at the nearest edit. Later clips move by the empty part plus the clip.
      - Ripple Overwrite: from the start of the clip at the playhead, or from the timeline In. Later clips move.
      - Place On Top: from the playhead, or from the timeline In.
    - Place On Top and Ripple Overwrite keep the clip's length between timeline marks.
    - Source Overwrite has no empty part, because it lines up by file timecode (`grid`):
      - The reference is the clip under the playhead if it shows part of the marks, or of the file with no marks. Else it is the first clip that does.
      - The playhead goes to the clip's end, also on a new track.
  - An edit in the view of one camera uses the marks of the file. With no marks, it takes the whole file. Then the grid comes back.
  - Escape leaves the view of one camera, as the close button does.
  - Multi Source keeps its own active camera and position. A clip open in Source Clip does not change them.
  - No measurement shows these cases:
    - F10 Overwrite in the grid places the marks' part at the playhead, with no empty part. With no marks, it places the whole file.
    - A bin with no video files opens an empty grid.
- **Timeline** (p. 681):
  - The Viewer shows the timeline at its playhead, with no scroll area.
  - Icons mark the first and the last frame of a clip.
- **Switching:**
  - Q toggles between Timeline and the last source mode (p. 696).
  - A click or a drag in the timeline area switches to Timeline (p. 681).

### 4.2 Transport (pp. 640, 687–688, 870–872)
- §11 gives the keys.
- J/K/L shuttle (pp. 696, 871; D54):
  - Each repeated J or L makes the speed higher, from 2× to 64×.
  - K held with J or L plays slowly.
  - K held with a tap of J or L steps a frame.
- Ctrl+/ loops playback (D51).
- A drag on the jog control scrubs finely (p. 640, D97).
  - The Jog control is on the viewer transport, before Previous.
  - A drag of 8 pixels moves one frame.
  - `player.seek` moves the playhead the same way.

### 4.3 Timecode (pp. 640, 688, 713–714)
- **Go to a time:**
  - Click the timecode field and type.
  - Or type `=` and a timecode, or `+` or `-` and a value to jump by.
  - Digits fill a timecode from the right: `100` is 1 second, and `10000` is 1 minute.
  - A value with a unit (`5s`, `450f`) is that duration.
- **Display:**
  - The source modes show source timecode. Timeline mode shows record timecode.
  - The timecode is drop-frame where the project counts drop-frame (D47).
  - That default is Auto (D91).
  - A right-click chooses another count (D91).
  - There is no KeyKode (X81).

### 4.4 In/Out (pp. 691–693, 700)
- **Source In/Out:** I/O in a source mode or over a pool thumbnail, or the handles of the scroll area.
- **Timeline In/Out:**
  - I/O in Timeline mode, or while you drag the ruler of either timeline.
  - The range shows on both rulers, and you can drag it there.
- **Clearing:** Alt+X clears In and Out.
- **Duration field:**
  - It shows the marked duration.
  - A change to it moves the Out mark (p. 693; D60).
  - It takes a number of frames, + or − and a number to change it by, or a timecode.

### 4.5 Other viewer features
- Full Screen (Shift+F) fills the display with the viewer.
  - Shift+F again returns to the page.
  - The Full Screen button on the viewer does the same thing (deviation X86).

- Enhanced viewer (Alt+F) fills the page with the viewer.

  - Alt+F again returns to the page.

- Esc returns to the page from full screen and from the enhanced viewer.
- Safe areas (pp. 682–683, 1177):
  - A Safe Area control at the right of the viewer's transport toggles framing guides.
  - Its menu, and View > Safe Area, pick the framing aspect and the safe-area lines.
  - Framing aspects:
    - Social Media: 1:1, 4:5, 9:16, 1.91:1 and 16:9.
    - Broadcast and Film: Default (the timeline's aspect), 1.33, 1.66, 1.77, 1.85 and 2.35.
  - Safe-area lines, each on its own: Extents, Action, Title and Center.

  - Extents is the boundary of the framing box.
  - The safe-area lines use that box, also while framing guides are off.
  - Framing guides shade outside that box when it does not fill the picture.
  - Action is 93% of the extents width and height, centered. Title is 90% (D90).

- Fast Review plays a long clip faster than a short clip (p. 640, D97).
  - The Fast Review button is on the viewer transport.
  - `player.fast_review` turns it on or off. It lasts for this session.
  - A clip of 10 seconds or less plays at 1×.
  - A longer clip plays at its length in seconds divided by 10, at most 8×.
  - Play and Space use that speed. J and L keep their shuttle speeds.
- Skimming with C held (p. 640).
  - Hold C and move the pointer across the picture.
  - The horizontal place of the pointer is the frame.
  - The playhead goes there with `player.seek`.
- **D97. Fast Review speed** (p. 640).
  - The manual says that a long clip plays faster than a short clip.
  - It does not give the speed.
  - A clip of 10 seconds or less plays at 1×.
  - A longer clip plays at its seconds divided by 10.
  - The speed is at most 8×.
  - 10 seconds is one short shot. 8× stays slow enough to see the picture.
  - The jog control and Jog In and Jog Out move one frame for each 8 pixels.
  - 8 pixels is a small drag, so the scrub stays fine.
- Audio scrubbing (Shift+S; on by default, p. 876):
  - A drag of the playhead, and frame steps, play the sound under it (D42).

## 5. Dual timeline (pp. 66, 643–646, 689–692, 716–718)

**Upper timeline:**
- It shows the whole program at full width, with no zoom.
- Its playhead moves freely. A drag on its ruler, or a sideways two-finger swipe, scrubs the program.
- Most edits work there.
- You can drag clips between the two timelines.
- It shows the timeline In/Out and the markers.

**Lower timeline:**
- It is a detail view around the playhead (p. 644).
- Ctrl+wheel (deviation X21), a trackpad pinch and the − and + buttons zoom it.
- The manual calls its zoom fixed, but Resolve zooms it too.
- A sideways swipe scrolls it (p. 66).
- **Fixed Playhead on (default, D1):**
  - The playhead stays at the center, and the clips move under it.
  - This is also true when you drag or swipe the ruler.
  - Clips do not snap to the playhead (p. 717).
- **Fixed Playhead off:**
  - The clips stay in place, and the playhead moves.
  - The view pages when the playhead reaches an edge.
  - A click on the ruler moves the playhead, and you can drag the playhead.

**Track display:**
- The combined video+audio tracks have numbers (1, 2, …). The audio-only tracks (A1, A2, …) are below them.
- A video clip shows a filmstrip. Each picture is the frame under its left edge, and the first picture is the first frame of the clip.
  - Thus a picture that starts before a cut in the source shows the shot before the cut.
- Its audio shows as a waveform band along the bottom. The band becomes larger with the track (p. 647).
- Its duration shows as seconds:frames, at the bottom left.
- The clips of a locked track are stippled. The clips of a disabled track are dimmed.
- A disabled clip is dull. A muted clip shows a mute icon.

**Track headers** (pp. 647, 678, 717): Enlarge (one track at a time), Lock, Mute (audio) and Video Enable icons (D43).

**Automatic tools** (p. 690):
- The **Boring Detector** (Timeline Options) highlights clips on the upper timeline, live as you edit:
  - clips longer than "Boring Clips", in light gray
  - clips shorter than "Jump Cuts", in red
- Analyze Timeline Edits sets both values (D31).
- **Detect Scene Cuts** (Timeline Actions) splits the selected clips where their shots change (D30).

## 6. Track model (pp. 647, 649, 716, 721, 727)

- **Track 1 is the main story (A-roll):**
  - Insert, delete, move and trim on it ripple. Thus it never has gaps.
  - With **Ripple On** off, it behaves like the other tracks.
  - Alt held during a trim turns rippling off for that trim.
- **Tracks 2+ are B-roll:**
  - Each clip is attached to the Track 1 clip under its In point. It follows that clip when Track 1 ripples (p. 649; D21).
  - A trim that moves the Out of the anchor clip to before the In of the attached clip deletes the attached clip (p. 727).
  - These tracks ripple only where there is no Track 1 video under them (p. 721; D22).
  - Clips on these tracks sit freely, with gaps.
- **Audio tracks:**
  - A1, A2, … are below the video tracks. They hold sound alone, for example music.
  - Media without video go there by default (D26).
- **Track header** (p. 647):
  - A click makes the track the target (D3).
  - A right-click adds a video or audio track, or removes one (D27).
- **Overlapping video:**
  - The highest enabled track is visible (p. 647).
  - A disabled clip, or a track with its video off, shows what is under it.
- **Linking:**
  - The video and audio of a clip are one combined clip.
  - Trims move both, but a J/L cut moves only the audio edit (§8.7).
  - The Cut page has no link/unlink.

## 7. Edit functions (pp. 696–701)

**Workflow** (p. 696):
1. Select a source, and mark its In/Out.
2. Optionally, click the header or number of a track to make it the target.
3. Push an edit button, or press an edit key.
4. Press Q to review the result, and Q again to go back.

Video Only and Audio Only limit what an edit takes (p. 694).

The edit buttons are one row of icons at the bottom of the Media Pool (p. 696). Cut, Dissolve and Smooth Cut are at the right of this row (p. 758). A tooltip gives the name of each icon.

### 7.1 Smart Indicator (p. 696)
- A marker on the ruler shows the edit point nearest the playhead.
- Some functions use this edit point instead of the exact playhead frame.
- After a time without input, the marker bobs.
- Then the edit that it selected flashes green. It flashes red if the clip there has no media past its edge.
- vidcut shows the colors without the motion (deviation X15).

### 7.2 Edit functions
- **Smart Insert** (F9 in the Edit page; p. 697):
  - It inserts at the edit point of the Smart Indicator on the target track, never at any other frame.
  - On Track 1, everything after it moves right by the incoming duration.
- **Append** (Shift+F12; p. 697): it ignores the playhead, and puts the clip after the last clip.
- **Ripple Overwrite** (Shift+F10; p. 698):
  - With no timeline marks, it replaces the whole clip under the playhead.
  - With a timeline In/Out, it replaces exactly that range, possibly across clips.
  - In both cases, everything after it moves by (incoming − replaced) duration. The source duration always wins.
  - The button, the key and a drop treat the other tracks in the same way (D21).
- **Overwrite** (F10; p. 701):
  - It writes over the target track at the playhead, at the timeline In, or backtimed to a lone timeline Out.
  - Other clips do not move.
- **Place On Top** (F12; pp. 699–700):
  - It goes to the first track above all clips at that position, whatever the target track.
  - It makes this track if necessary (D24).
  - It never ripples.
  - It aligns by the first of these rules that applies:
    1. a timeline In with the source In;
    2. a lone timeline Out with the source Out (backtimed);
    3. with no marks, within 5 frames of an edit, with that edit;
    4. otherwise with the playhead.
- **Source Overwrite** (pp. 700–701; measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2):
  - It puts a file in sync by timecode with a reference clip on the timeline.
    - The clip's position is the reference's start, plus the file's timecode, minus the timecode of the reference at its start.
    - The timeline's own start timecode plays no part.
    - A file with no timecode tag has timecode 0.
  - A clip counts when the timecode range that it shows overlaps the file. The rest of its media does not count.
    - Audio clips count. Generators do not count.
  - **With timeline marks,** the source marks play no part.
    - The reference is the first of these: a clip at In, a clip in the marked range, any clip.
    - With In alone, no marked range counts: the reference is a clip at In, else any clip.
    - In each, the lowest track comes first, then the earliest clip.
    - The new clip goes from In to Out. With no Out, it goes to the file's end.
    - It stops at the file's end. It starts at the file's start ([deviation X113](deviations.md#x113-source-overwrite-starts-where-the-file-starts)).
    - With Out alone, the reference is the first clip before Out. The new clip goes in sync from the file's start to Out ([deviation X126](deviations.md#x126-source-overwrite-keeps-sync-with-a-timeline-out-alone)).
  - **With source marks and no timeline marks,** the playhead plays no part, except past the timeline's end.
    - The reference is the first clip that overlaps the marked range: lowest track first, then the earliest clip.
    - The marked range goes in. A source Out with no In goes from the file's start to Out.
    - With no such clip and the playhead at or past the timeline's end, the first clip that overlaps the whole file is the reference ([§7.4](#74-sync-bin-pp-708710-measured-in-resolve-studio-2112)).
  - **With no marks,** the whole file goes in.
    - Take the lowest track with a clip under the playhead that overlaps the file.
    - T is the source timecode of that clip at the playhead.
    - The reference is the first clip on that track that overlaps the file and starts at or before T.
    - With no such clip under the playhead, the reference is the first clip that overlaps: lowest track first, then the earliest clip.
    - When a video clip and an audio clip under the playhead disagree, the video clip counts.
  - With source marks or no marks, the clip is cut at the timeline start, and it can pass the timeline end.
  - **Track:** the first video track above the highest track with a clip in the new clip's range.
    - With no clip in the range, the track is V1, even into a gap.
    - It makes the track if necessary. It never fills a free lower track.
    - A source with no picture is an audio-only edit. An audio-only edit always makes a new top audio track.
  - **Another frame rate:** timecode lines up in real time, for a file and for a reference clip.
  - **A still** has timecode 00:00:00:00, so it goes in only with a clip at that timecode.
  - **After the edit,** the timeline marks clear, and the source marks stay.
    - The playhead goes to the clip's end.
    - When the edit made a new video track, the playhead stops at the old timeline end at the latest.
  - With no overlap, or on an empty timeline, nothing changes and vidcut tells why ([deviation X114](deviations.md#x114-source-overwrite-tells-when-it-writes-nothing)).
  - **By the synced group:** `group` lines up the file by its synced group, as the Sync Bin does. In the other views, the edit does not use the group.
    - The rules above do not change. The place in the group takes the place of the timecode.
    - Only clips of that group count as the reference.
    - A file in no group is refused.
  - `track` is refused, because Source Overwrite picks its own track.
  - These cases are not measured, and vidcut lets them through:
    - **A reference clip with a changed speed:** its first frame lines up, and the file goes in at normal speed.
    - **A range with left-out frames:** each kept part goes in at its own place.
    - **`tape` across files:** each file lines up by itself, and the edit is one step.
    - **Several selected pool items:** each file lines up by itself, the timeline marks hold for all of them (`keep_marks`), and the edit is one step.
- **Close Up (later).**

### 7.3 Drag and drop from the pool or Viewer (pp. 648, 679, 694–695)
- **Onto an empty timeline, or left of the first clip:** the clip becomes the first clip.
- **Past either end of all clips:** the clip is appended or prepended.
- **Onto a clip, released immediately** (the whole clip highlights): Ripple Overwrite replaces that clip. It ignores the timeline marks, and they stay (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
- **Onto a clip after a short hover** (the highlight narrows to the incoming footprint): a plain Overwrite with no ripple. It can split the target.
- **Onto an edit point:** insert at that edit.
- **Into the gray area above the top track:** this makes a new video+audio track.
- **Below the bottom track:** an audio clip makes an audio-only track.
- **Onto another track:** an overwrite there, never a ripple. Only Track 1 pushes clips (D23).

### 7.4 Sync Bin (pp. 708–710; measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2)
- The Sync Bin tab takes the Media Pool's place. `gui.show sync_bin` opens it. The viewer goes to the timeline.
- **The rows:** one row and one cell for each row of the group of the current bin and its subfolders, or of its files.
  - The rows have the numbers 1, 2, 3 and so on. The measurements show 24 rows, in a grid of 5 by 5. The number keys reach row 9.
  - Each row shows its files on the timeline's time. All rows move with the playhead.
  - A drag to the right moves the playhead earlier. A click on the rows or the ruler does not move it.
  - `multicam.rows` gives the rows at a time.
- **The group:** a saved group gives its stored rows. Other files of the bin do not show.
  - With two groups or more in the bin, the group of the clip at the playhead counts.
  - Example: a group with 2 rows and a group with 1 row of 2 files. The rows change when the playhead moves to a clip of the other group.
  - A file in more than one group counts for its newest group. Thus a newer group of all files gives its rows at each playhead.
- **No saved group:** the rows are the rows of [§3](#3-media-pool-ch-34-pp-653675) by timecode, so the rows are those of the Sync Clips window. A row with no name has the label Camera and its number among those rows.
- **The file of a row** at a time is the file whose visible part holds that time.
  - Example: tags 01:00:05:00 and 01:00:10:00 on one camera. At source 01:00:15:00 the row shows the first file. At source 01:00:38:00 it shows the second file, 5 seconds long.
- **The time of a file:** by its synced group when it is in one, and by its timecode otherwise.
  - A file with no tag, or with the tag 00:00:00:00, and no saved group has a row at 00:00:00:00.
  - The rows follow the source timecode of the clip at the playhead, not the record time.
  - Example: a timeline of three files in a row, with the second file at the playhead. The rows read that file's source timecode.
  - With several clips at the playhead, the clip on the lowest track that overlaps the file counts. A video clip counts before an audio clip.
  - With no clip at the playhead, the first clip that overlaps the whole file counts.
  - A file with no time in common with the timeline has an empty row.
- **The grid:** the viewer shows the cameras at the playhead, with the title "Sync Bin". The sound is the timeline's sound.
  - A cell has its number at the top left and "Camera" with its name at the bottom.
  - The camera with the file on top at the playhead is the active camera. Its cell has a red outline and a red corner, and its row number is red.
  - A camera with no file at the playhead shows a black cell with its number and label.
- **Choosing a camera:** a click on its cell, a click on its row number, or its number key (p. 709).
  - The viewer shows the camera's file at the playhead alone. Its name is the title, and a close button is at the top left.
  - `multicam.pick` sets the source marks of that file only: In at the playhead, five seconds long, stopping at the file's end. Marks on other files stay.
  - A click on a black cell only shows a white outline. It sets no marks and opens no view.
  - The close button or Escape returns to the grid and writes nothing (p. 709).
- **Source Overwrite** writes the marked range by the rules of [§7.2](#72-edit-functions), with `group` for a file in a group.
  - The playhead moves to the clip's end, and the grid returns. The source marks stay.
  - With no camera chosen, an edit tells the user to select one.
- **Past the timeline's end:** a drag can pass the end, up to the end of the last file.
  - In is the file's frame at the timeline's end. Out is its frame at the playhead (p. 710).
  - The edit fills the timeline from its end to the playhead, and the playhead stays.
  - The reference is then the first clip that overlaps the whole file, as the marked range overlaps no clip.
- These cases are not measured, and vidcut lets them through:
  - **A still:** it has no row, as in the Sync Clips window.
  - **A file with no picture:** it has its own row and cell, as in the Sync Clips window.
  - **Two groups in the bin, and the clip at the playhead in neither:** the newest group counts.
  - **A camera at another frame rate:** a click chooses it like any other camera.

### 7.5 Sync Clips window (pp. 706–708; measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2)
- **Opening:** the Sync Clips icon in the Media Pool acts on the files of the current bin. `gui.show sync_clips` does the same.
  - With no file selected, it opens all files of the bin and its subfolders.
  - With two files or more selected, it opens only those files.
  - With one file selected, no window opens.
  - With fewer than two files, no window opens. The row rule counts too: two files of one camera with one start and one length open no window.
  - Two files of one camera open one row and one cell when they do not overlap or overlap only in part. Two files with no name and no overlap do the same.
  - Files that are one saved group open that group ([deviation X123](deviations.md#x123-the-sync-clips-icon-opens-a-saved-group)).
  - A file that the group's `dropped` lists is not a new file, so the icon opens the group ([deviation X123](deviations.md#x123-the-sync-clips-icon-opens-a-saved-group)).
  - A saved group plus a new file opens as a new sync, by the rows of [§3](#3-media-pool-ch-34-pp-653675). Files of two saved groups or more do the same.
    - Cancel adds nothing. Save Sync makes a new group, and the earlier groups stay.
  - "Open Sync Group…" in the menu of a file in a group opens its newest group.
  - The window is inside vidcut's main window ([deviation X121](deviations.md#x121-the-sync-clips-window-is-inside-the-main-window)).
- **Which files it takes:**
  - A file with no camera name opens the window. Its camera name stays empty after Save Sync.
  - The window has no limit of cameras. The measurements open 24 cameras.
  - A still is left out.
  - A file with no picture has a row with a waveform, and its cell shows a waveform symbol.
  - Two files with one camera name share one row.
- **The window:**
  - Left: "Sync View", a grid of all cameras. Right: the viewer of the chosen cell, with the name of its file.
  - The measured grids: 2 cameras side by side, 3 or 4 in 2 by 2, 6 in 3 by 2, 12 in 4 by 3.
  - More measured grids: 13 cameras in 4 by 4, 24 in 5 by 5.
  - One row and one cell for each row of [§3](#3-media-pool-ch-34-pp-653675). A named row holds all files of that camera. Named rows come first, in camera order, with 10 after 9. Rows with no name follow.
  - The numbers of the rows and the cells are positions. The label of a cell is the camera name, for example cells 1 to 6 with Camera 7 to Camera 12.
  - A row with no name has the label Camera and its number among those rows. Example: Camera A, Camera B, then Camera 1.
  - A cell whose camera has no file at the playhead is black, with its number and label.
- **Selection:**
  - A click on a clip selects that clip only, with a red outline. The viewer and the red cell number do not change.
  - A click on the empty part of a row selects the row. Its cell turns red, the viewer shows that camera, and no clip is selected. The other clips have blue outlines.
  - A click on the row header selects the row number, which turns red.
  - A click on a cell selects its row. The viewer shows that camera, the red number follows the cell, and no clip is selected.
  - The sync icon acts on the row of the last click.
- **Play:**
  - A click or a drag on the ruler moves the playhead of the window.
  - Play and Space play the pictures and the sound. Space also stops.
  - Cancel and Save Sync are dim during play. Escape closes the window like Cancel.
- **Sound:**
  - The headphones icon at the right end of the bar is "Solo Audio". It is green when on. It stays on at the next open.
  - With Solo off, all rows sound. They add up with no scaling: two equal rows measure peak 0.687 alone and 1.000 together.
  - With Solo on, only the row of the chosen cell sounds. A new cell during play changes what is heard.
  - A file with no sound stays silent.
- **Places at open:** each file lies at its timecode, and no row has a sync icon.
  - Tags 01:00:00:00, 01:00:05:00 and 01:00:10:00 give rows at 0, 125 and 250.
  - A file with no tag reads 00:00:00:00. Thus with no tags, all files start at one point.
  - The rows of [§3](#3-media-pool-ch-34-pp-653675) by timecode act on all files.
- **The ruler** shows the timecodes of the files.
  - It starts at the earliest timecode of the files. Where all files read 00:00:00:00, it starts at 01:00:00:00.
  - Its start does not change when rows move.
- **Far gaps:** the rule is for an empty stretch where no row has a picture. It is measured at 25 fps and 30 fps.
  - A stretch of exactly 10:00 minutes stays. One frame more makes it 1 second: 25 frames at 25 fps, 30 frames at 30 fps.
  - The length of the clips plays no part.
  - The rule acts at open, at Auto Sync and during a drag.
  - A shift of more than 10:00 by Sound or In stays, because the rows overlap and no stretch is empty.
  - The saved group has the short places. The ruler shows a dark bar at each short gap, and it jumps to the real timecode after the bar.
- **Earliest row:** when no row stays at the start after a drag or a nudge, the earliest row goes to the start.
  - The empty front goes away. The frames of the group fall, and its start stays.
  - One measurement gave 936 frames from 1005.
- **Auto Sync:** it runs the method under "Sync By" and replaces the places: Timecode, Sound, In or Out.
  - It reads the camera names again, so the rows can change. It gives whole files at their places, and the rows of [§3](#3-media-pool-ch-34-pp-653675) act again.
  - On an opened group it uses only the group's files, so a file in `dropped` does not come back.
  - Each row that it lines up gets one sync icon, the sync lock.
  - With In or Out, one mark counts for each row ([§3](#3-media-pool-ch-34-pp-653675)). A file stays whole. A file with no mark lines up at its first frame by In, and at its end by Out.
  - By sound, a file that matches no other file stays at the start, with no icon. "Auto Sync Failed" names such files.
  - By sound, files of a row go by their places, and a tie goes by bin order. Thus the result does not depend on bin order when the places differ ([deviation X125](deviations.md#x125-sync-by-sound-uses-time-order-on-a-row)).
  - Auto Sync reads the marks at that moment.
  - On a saved group, the result waits for Save Sync ([deviation X119](deviations.md#x119-auto-sync-on-a-saved-group-waits-for-save-sync)).
- **By hand:**
  - Keys and drags act on the selected or dragged clip only. The other clips of the row stay.
  - A drag moves a clip on a row with no sync icon. On a row with one, nothing moves.
  - The sync icon in the bar turns the sync lock of the selected row on and off.
  - Period moves the selected clip 1 frame later, and Shift+Period 5 frames later. Comma moves it 1 frame earlier. With no clip selected, these keys move nothing.
  - Shift+Comma moves it 5 frames earlier, by the manual (p. 707). No measurement on a Finnish keyboard shows that move.
  - These keys work by the key's place too, so Shift+Comma and Shift+Period work on a layout that makes them other signs.
  - Comma on a clip at the start changes nothing. A drag before the start stops the clip at the start, and the other clips stay.
  - A drag on an opened group changes the group at Save Sync ([deviation X120](deviations.md#x120-a-drag-in-an-opened-group-changes-the-group-at-save-sync)).
- **Overlap by a drag:** the moved clip stays as it is. The other clip of the row gives way when the drag ends.
  - The moved clip's start is inside the other: the other ends at the moved clip's start. Its tail is gone, also past the moved clip's end.
  - The moved clip covers the other's start and ends inside it: the other starts at the moved clip's end. Its tail stays in place.
  - The moved clip covers all of the other: the other leaves the row and the group, and `dropped` lists it.
  - A cut stays when the clip is dragged away again. A part keeps its length when dragged.
  - `multicam.place` and a nudge follow these rules too.
- **Save Sync** stores the places, the rows and the sync locks, and closes the window.
  - The first Save Sync makes a group. It is `multicam.create` with `rows`. A file that no row holds goes on the group's `dropped`.
  - The start of that group is always 01:00:00:00: tags from 01:00:00:00, tags from 01:02:00:00, no tags, mixed, and after Auto Sync.
  - After the rule of far gaps, the frames of a group run from the first row's start to the last row's end.
  - A Save Sync on an opened group keeps the group, its name and its start. It is `multicam.set`.
  - A clip on the timeline does not follow ([deviation X122](deviations.md#x122-a-clip-on-the-timeline-does-not-follow-save-sync)).
- **Cancel,** Escape or the close button changes nothing.

## 8. Timeline editing (Ch. 36, pp. 715–732)

### 8.1 Selection (pp. 719–720)
- **Clips:**
  - A click selects. Ctrl+click toggles. Shift+click selects a range.
  - A marquee from empty space selects what it touches.
  - A click on empty space, or Ctrl+Shift+A, deselects.
  - Alt+Shift+V selects all clips under the playhead.
- **Edit points:**
  - The pointer at the center of an edit selects both sides (a roll).
  - The pointer just left or right of the edit selects one side (a resize).
  - Ctrl+click on an edit adds it to the selection. Ctrl+click on a selected edit takes it out.
  - V selects the edit nearest the playhead. U cycles through both, incoming and outgoing.
  - In both timelines, each selected edit is a solid line on the cut. A glow is on its selected side, or on both sides.
  - The glow pulses about once a second.
  - The glow is green where that side has media to trim into, and red where it has none.
- **Several selected edits** (Resolve Studio 21.1.1; Edit page, pp. 1079–1080):
  - The Trim Editor shows the edit clicked last.
  - When Ctrl+click takes that edit out, the Trim Editor closes. The other edits stay selected.
  - `,` and `.` trim every selected edit in one step. Each roll and each side moves by the full amount.
  - Two Out sides on Track 1 each get the full amount. The later clips move by the sum.
  - A drag on a selected edit trims every selected edit. Each roll moves by the full drag.
  - Ripple trims on one track share the drag. The frames go to the edits one at a time, from left to right (p. 1079).
  - After the drag, no edit is selected.
  - A drag on an edit that is not selected trims only that edit. After it, no edit is selected.
  - A roll and a side cannot move together (p. 1079). With both kinds selected, a trim does nothing.
  - U changes the side of every selected edit (p. 1080).
  - Dynamic Trim (J, K, L) trims every selected edit by the same amount (p. 1080).
  - Out sides and In sides move in opposite directions (p. 1080).
  - vidcut: the side of the edit clicked last, or of the edit dragged, follows the key or the pointer.
  - vidcut: the handles and buttons of the Trim Editor trim only the edit that it shows.
  - Not checked: which side follows the key in Resolve, and what the Trim Editor trims with several edits.

### 8.2 Moving clips (pp. 720–723)
- **Onto another Track 1 clip, released quickly:** the clip replaces that clip and ripples.
- **After a hover:** it overwrites the middle of the target and splits it. On Track 1, the gap that it leaves closes.
- **Over the edge of a neighbor, with the pointer not on the neighbor** (p. 722):
  - The overlapped part of the neighbor is overwritten.
  - The gap left behind closes.
- **Onto an edit point** (it highlights): insert-at-edit.
  - The gap left behind closes.
  - Later clips move with their attached clips.
- **A Track 1 clip dropped onto another track:** it can only overwrite (p. 721). Track 1 closes up behind it.
- **Higher-track clips:** they move freely, and leave gaps.
- Moves between tracks: D25.

### 8.3 Copy, cut, paste (p. 723)
- Ctrl+C copies.
- Ctrl+X cuts, and closes the gap with a ripple.
- Ctrl+V pastes at the playhead on the original track, and overwrites.

### 8.4 Delete (pp. 721, 724–725)
- Delete removes the selected clips.
- On Track 1 with Ripple On, it ripples, and the attached clips follow.
- With a timeline In/Out set, it removes that range (D68).
- D disables or enables the video of a clip.
- Right-click > Mute silences the audio of a clip.

### 8.5 Split (pp. 646, 723, 950)
- The Split button, Split Clips, or Ctrl+\ splits at the playhead.
- Join Match Edit Clips (Timeline Actions) joins the parts of a split clip back into one clip (D49).

### 8.6 Trimming (pp. 693, 716, 718, 726–732)
- **The pointer position selects the operation:**
  - **Clip edge: resize.**
    - A tooltip shows the frame delta and the new duration.
    - On Track 1 with Ripple On, it ripples. On other tracks, only that clip changes.
  - **Edit center: roll.**
    - Trim > Roll > Start to Playhead next to a gap moves only the picture edge. The sound stays.
    - `edit.roll` and `edit.roll_to_playhead` do this.
    - The Trim menu has Roll > Start to Playhead and End to Playhead (p. 1087). These commands choose clips as D4 does.
    - The total duration stays the same.
    - A white overlay shows the available handle media.
  - **Slip handle at the clip center: slip.** Several selected clips slip together.
  - **Lower audio band at an edit: audio-only roll**, a J/L cut (§8.7).
- **Safe Edit** (option, p. 732):
  - A trim stops at the neighbor clip, with a small timer on the cursor.
  - Continue the drag to overwrite the neighbor.
- **Trim to Audio** (option, p. 718; Resolve Studio 21.1.1):
  - It is off at first.
  - During an edge trim, a roll or a slip, the clips that change show only their sound.
  - The sound is a waveform the full height of the track, about the middle of the track.
  - A roll changes both clips. A slip changes each slipped clip.
  - The track height does not change. The view goes back at the end of the drag.
  - The audio-only roll (§8.7) and Dynamic Trim do not change the view.
- **Trim Editor** (pp. 729–730; D52):
  - It shows while an edit is selected and the player is stopped.
  - The Viewer shows the last frame of the outgoing clip beside the first frame of the incoming clip.
  - Under these two frames are two filmstrips: the outgoing filmstrip on top, and the incoming filmstrip below it.
  - Each frame has a number, counted from the position of the edit when the editor opened.
  - A bar in the color of the clip is under the frames of the clip. A grey bar is under the frames past its edge.
  - The handles stay at the centre, and the filmstrips slide under them, as far as the media allow.
  - The top handle trims the outgoing Out. The bottom handle trims the incoming In. The white handle between them rolls.
  - While you drag a handle, the timeline shows the trim as it will be (Resolve Studio 21.1.1).
  - The release of the handle applies the trim.
  - A handle turns red where its side has no more media.
  - A pair of ±1-frame buttons is on each side of the transport.
  - The left pair nudges the Out, and the right pair nudges the In. With both sides selected, they roll.
  - An edit with a dissolve shows the dissolve between the filmstrips, several frames to a cell.
  - A drag on either end of the dissolve makes it longer or shorter about the edit.
  - Esc or Close deselects the edit.
- **Trim Start/End to Playhead** (Edit Actions, p. 729): moves the head or the tail of a clip to the playhead.
- **Change Clip Speed** (R†): a speed, reversed or not, or a freeze frame, for each selected clip (D48).
  - The Speed Change controls of the Inspector set the same (pp. 736–737).
  - vidcut: the Speed section of the Inspector tab (X70).
  - vidcut: the window also sets Retime Process (X93).
- **Change Clip Duration** (Ctrl+D, p. 693; Resolve Studio 21.1.1):
  - It takes frames or a timecode, or 1, 5 or 15 seconds, for each selected clip.
  - End runs to the end of the media.
  - A duration longer than the media stops at the end of the media. No error shows.
  - On Track 1 it ripples, also with Ripple On off.
  - **Extend Beyond Clip Length** (checkbox):
    - It is off at first. The dialog keeps its state, also after Cancel.
    - On Track 1, the rest of the duration becomes a gap after the clip. The later clips move by the full amount.
    - On other tracks, the clip runs to the end of its media and overwrites the start of the next clip. No gap is added.
- **Dynamic Trim** (W, pp. 730–732):
  - W toggles the mode, and shows the timeline.
  - The playhead turns yellow.
  - It goes to the selected edit (Out, In or both by U), else to the edit nearest the playhead. The Trim Editor shows that edit.
  - Or it goes to the edge of the selected clip that is nearer the playhead.
  - Then J/K/L trim the edit, or slip or slide the selected clips, instead of playing.
  - Right-click the button to select slip or slide.
  - With nothing selected, J/K/L play.
  - A slide keeps the media of a clip. It makes the clip before it longer as it makes the clip after it shorter (p. 731).
    - On a side with a gap, the gap changes in place of a clip. These are measurements in Resolve Studio 21.1.2.
    - The slide stops one frame short of an earlier clip that does not touch it. It stops at the timeline's start.
    - A slide right stops in the same way at a later clip. The measurements show this only with a gap of one frame.
    - A clip with a gap on both sides also slides. No measurement shows this case.
  - L slides the clip right. It also slips its content right, to earlier frames, as a drag does.
  - Space plays around the playhead, and Up/Down go to another edit (D32, deviations X11, X28, X29).
  - W again deselects the edit.

### 8.7 J/L cuts (p. 728)
- Hover over the lower audio part of a clip at an edit until the cursor shows a note. Then drag.
- Only the audio edit moves, as an audio roll.
- A click selects that audio edit, and `,` / `.` move it (D20).
- The edit needs sound on one side only.
  - With sound on the outgoing side only, the sound goes on past the picture. It goes into a gap, or under a clip with no sound.
  - With sound on the incoming side only, the sound starts before the picture.
  - vidcut stops the edit at the nearest sound on the track, at the timeline's start and where the media runs out.

### 8.8 Snapping (pp. 716–717)
- N toggles snapping.
- Clip edges, markers, the playhead and the timeline In/Out snap to each other.
- A white line shows the snap.
- N pressed during a drag applies to that drag only. N pressed between drags stays.

### 8.9 Markers (pp. 718–719)
- M adds a marker at the playhead, or edits the marker there.
- A double-click on a marker opens the marker dialog. The dialog has a button for each marker color (p. 1016).
- Shift+Up/Down jumps between markers.
- You can drag markers.
- Alt+M deletes the marker at the playhead. Delete removes the selected markers.
- In the Source Clip viewer, M, Alt+M and Shift+Up/Down act on the markers of the clip in the viewer (p. 1013).
  - A second M on the same frame opens the marker dialog.
  - `markers.add`, `markers.list` and `markers.remove` with `media` do this. `player.seek` moves the playhead.

### 8.10 Undo (pp. 651–652)
- Ctrl+Z undoes, and Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes. The number of steps has no limit.
- Edit > History lists the last twenty steps:
  - The newest step is on top.
  - The current step has a check mark.
  - The steps that can be redone are also in the list.
  - Select a step to undo or redo to it.
- Open History Window, at the bottom of that menu, lists all the steps:
  - The oldest step is on top, from Original. Original is the project before all the steps.
  - The current step has a dot.
  - The steps that can be redone are grey.
  - A click on a step goes to it immediately.
- A new change discards the steps that could be redone.
- Details: D55.

### 8.11 Dissolves (pp. 645, 747, 758–761, 895, 1114, 1159, 1173)
- **Dissolve and Ctrl+T** (p. 758):
  - Dissolve puts a one-second Cross Dissolve centered on the edit nearest the playhead.
  - Ctrl+T puts it on the selected edit (V). Else it puts it on the selected clips (p. 895), else on the edit nearest the playhead.
  - U selects the position: ending on the edit (outgoing side), starting on it (incoming side), or centered (both).
  - Where the sounds of the clips meet at that edit, a Cross Fade goes on the sound too (p. 1114).
  - Details: D50.
- **Cut** (p. 759): the edit nearest the playhead becomes a cut again.
- **Smooth Cut** (p. 759):
  - The button puts a 0.2-second Smooth Cut centered on the edit nearest the playhead (deviation X100).
  - Its Mode is Better, its Process is AI and its Ease is None.
  - Where the sounds of the clips meet at that edit, a Cross Fade 0 dB of the same length goes on the sound too.
- **A selected dissolve** (pp. 759, 761):
  - A click selects it.
  - Its right-click menu has Change Transition Duration, then Start on Edit, Center on Edit and End on Edit. The current one is greyed.
  - Change Transition Duration takes a timecode or frames (Format, remembered), or a 1, 5 or 15-second preset.
  - The duration is shortened to the media (deviation X27).
  - Ctrl+D also opens Change Transition Duration.
  - Delete removes the dissolve.
  - A change of its length or its alignment keeps its type: a wipe stays a wipe.
  - A drag on an edge of a dissolve, in either timeline, makes it longer or shorter evenly about the edit.
  - The drag works at the scale of that timeline. The length is shortened to the media (X27).
  - A tooltip shows the move of the edge and the new length. The drag also selects the dissolve.
  - Each of these also acts on the Cross Fade at the edit.
- **How it plays** (pp. 747, 760, 1159, 1173):
  - Each clip shows its media past its edge. Thus a dissolve is as long as the clips have frames there.
  - Pictures cross in a straight line. At a gap, they cross from or to black.
  - The level of a Cross Fade at its middle is +3, 0 or −3 dB.
- **A Dip To Color Dissolve** (p. 1165):
  - In the first half of the transition, the outgoing picture fades to its color. In the second half, the incoming picture fades in from it.
  - Its color is black unless it is set. Resolve's Start Ratio and End Ratio come later.
  - A request makes it: `{"type": "dip_to_color", "color": "#rrggbb"}`.
  - An OTIO file holds it as a Dip To Color Dissolve, with the color as its `color` value ([gotchas](gotchas.md#resolves-otio-files)).
- **A wipe:**
  - A wipe is another transition on an edit.
  - It uses the same length rules as a dissolve.
  - It has one direction: left, right, up or down.
  - A left wipe reveals the incoming picture from the left edge toward the right.
  - The other directions start at their own edge.
  - The edge moves in fractions of the duration (deviation X89).
  - A request can make a wipe.
  - A request can change a dissolve into a wipe, and a wipe into a dissolve.
- **A Push and a Slide** (pp. 1166–1167):
  - They use the same length rules as a dissolve.
  - In a Push, the incoming picture pushes the outgoing picture out of the frame.
  - The Preset of a Push is Push Left, Push Right, Push Up or Push Down. Push Left moves the pictures right, so the incoming picture comes in from the left.
  - In a Slide with the Direction In, the incoming picture slides in over the outgoing picture. With Out, the outgoing picture slides out over the incoming picture.
  - The Preset of a Slide is the edge or the corner that it comes in from: Left-Right, Right-Left, Bottom-Up, Top-Down, Top-Left or Bottom-Right.
  - Border puts a band of Color on the moving edge. Its width is a part of the shorter side of the frame.
  - Feather fades one picture into the other across the band. The band then shows no color.
  - Motion Blur blurs along the move. A Push blurs the whole frame. A Slide blurs the moving picture only.
  - Ease is None, In, Out, In & Out or Custom. A Custom ease plays the keys of its curve. Without keys, it stays at its fixed value, from 0 to 1.
  - A change to Custom keeps the curve of the ease before it. A change to another ease gives the curve of that ease.
  - A change of the length scales the curve, as in Resolve. A key at frame k goes to k × new / old, rounded down. The handles scale by new / old. The values stay.
  - An import from an OTIO file at another frame rate converts the curve to the project's rate. Each key goes to the nearest frame at that rate. The handles scale by the ratio of the rates.
  - If the scale or the conversion puts two keys on one frame, vidcut keeps the first of them. Resolve keeps the first key when it scales.
  - At a gap, the missing side shows the tracks below. Where it covers the other picture, that picture does not show.
  - The pictures move and blur first. Then the composite mode of the outgoing clip mixes them with the tracks below. The tracks below do not move.
    - The composite mode of the incoming clip has no effect during the transition.
  - Resolve's measured geometry, blur and ease are in [gotchas](gotchas.md#resolves-otio-files).
  - A request makes one: `{"type": "push", "preset": "up", "border": 0.02}`, or `{"type": "slide", "preset": "top_left", "out": true}`.
  - An OTIO file holds them as Resolve's Push and Slide, with their values.
- **A Smooth Cut** (pp. 759, 1166):
  - It morphs the outgoing picture into the incoming picture. It hides a jump cut, for example in an interview.
  - It uses the same length rules as a dissolve.
  - With the Mode Better, both clips play on, as in a dissolve. Each frame morphs the frame of one clip into the frame of the other.
  - With the Mode Faster, it morphs two still frames. They are the outgoing clip at the first frame of the transition and the incoming clip at its last frame.
  - The Process makes the frames between the two pictures: AI Fast, AI Slow or Frame Blend (deviation X99). Frame Blend gives a Cross Dissolve.
  - Ease and Transition Curve are as for a Push. Frame f of n frames shows the morph at curve(f) + 0.5 / n, at most 1.
  - The morph takes the nearest source frame of each clip. A clip with a speed change shows no frames between its source frames.
  - A Solid Color side acts as a still side. Measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2: two Solid Colors give the same picture as two stills of those colors.
  - It morphs two pictures that each cover the frame alone, with the usual Inspector values. It plays as a Cross Dissolve in these cases:
    - A side is a gap, a clip in a compound clip, or a clip over other tracks that does not fill the frame.
    - A side has other Inspector values, or plays backward.
  - Resolve's measured values are in [gotchas](gotchas.md#resolves-otio-files).
  - A request makes one: `{"type": "smooth_cut", "mode": "faster", "process": "frame_blend"}`.
  - An OTIO file holds it as Resolve's Smooth Cut, with its values.
- **The Transitions tab** (pp. 759–761):
  - It has cards for Cross Dissolve, Dip To Color Dissolve and Smooth Cut under Dissolve.
  - It has a card for Edge Wipe under Wipe, and cards for Push and Slide under Motion.
  - The Edge Wipe of the card comes in from the bottom, as Resolve's Edge Wipe at 0 degrees (X89).
  - The Push of the card is Push Left. The Slide of the card is Left-Right, In. The Smooth Cut of the card is the Smooth Cut of the button.
  - A drag of a card onto an edit of a video track adds that transition there, one second long. A Smooth Cut is 0.2 seconds long (X100).
    - Left of the edit, it ends on the edit. Right of the edit, it starts on it. On the edit, it is centered.
    - The length is shortened to the media (X27).
  - A drag of a card onto a transition changes its type. The length stays.
  - A double-click on a card adds it on the edit nearest the playhead, as the Dissolve button does.
  - The right-click menu of a card has Add to Selected Edit Points and Clips.
    - It acts on the selected edit. Else it acts on the edges of the selected clips.
  - The cards add a picture transition only. They add no Cross Fade.
  - `gui.show transitions` opens the tab.
- **The Transition Inspector** (pp. 747–748):
  - A double-click on a transition selects it and opens the Inspector.
  - The Inspector shows the selected transition: Transition Type, Duration and Alignment.
  - Duration is in frames, with the time beside it. A change keeps the alignment. The length is shortened to the media (X27).
  - Alignment is Start on Edit, Center on Edit or End on Edit.
  - An Edge Wipe also has Edge: Left, Right, Top or Bottom (X89).
  - A Dip To Color Dissolve also has Color. A change goes to the project when the color picker closes.
  - A Push and a Slide also have Preset, Border, Feather, Color, Motion Blur and Ease. A Slide also has Direction: In or Out.
  - Border shows the band in pixels of the timeline width, as Resolve shows it. A change of Border or Motion Blur goes to the project when the drag ends.
  - A Smooth Cut also has Mode, Process and Ease. Mode is Faster or Better. Process is AI Fast, AI Slow or Frame Blend (X99).
  - A Process with an AI model that is not on the computer asks for its download first, as Retime Process does.
  - A Push, a Slide and a Smooth Cut also have Transition Curve: the curve at the playhead, from 0 to 1. The row has the value, the previous-key and next-key arrows, a keyframe button and Reset.
    - The frame of a key counts from the first frame of the transition. A key can be outside the transition, as in Resolve.
    - A change of the value with keys puts a key at the playhead. With no keys, it changes the fixed value.
    - The keyframe button removes the key at the playhead. Else it adds a key with the value at the playhead.
    - A new key between two keys keeps the shape of the curve. On a curved part, the key and its neighbors get the handles that cut the curve there.
    - A change of the curve with an ease other than Custom changes the ease to Custom first.
    - The arrows move the playhead to the previous or next key.
    - Reset with Custom gives the straight curve from 0 to 1, and the fixed value 0.
  - Each change is a request: `edit.transition` for the type and its values, `edit.dissolve` for the length and the alignment.
- **Later:**
  - Hover Scrub Preview of a transition, and favorites
  - the other values of each type, such as the Style of a dissolve and the border of a wipe
  - moving a transition to another edit
  - Change Transition Type, Set as Default Transition

### 8.12 Subtitles (pp. 645–646, 702, 1227–1248)
- **Subtitle tracks** (pp. 646, 702, 1228, 1232, 1245):
  - Add Subtitle Track adds a subtitle track above the video tracks. It is in Timeline Actions, and in the right-click menu of a track header.
  - The names of the tracks are Subtitle 1, Subtitle 2, and so on.
  - A double-click on the name renames the track. Exports use the name.
  - A subtitle track locks like any track.
  - One subtitle track shows at a time. When one shows, the others are hidden (p. 1228).
  - Minimize Subtitle Track (Timeline Options, p. 645) makes them shorter.
  - Details: D56; deviation X44.
- **Cues** (pp. 1231–1233, 1236–1237):
  - Add Subtitle, in the right-click menu of a subtitle track, puts a cue at the playhead.
  - You type its text in the Inspector. The text shows on the cue as you type it.
  - The Captions list, at the bottom of the Inspector, shows every cue of the track (pp. 1231, 1233).
  - Each row of the list has its number, its In above its Out, its text and its characters per second (CPS).
  - A click on a cue in the list loads it into the Inspector to edit (p. 1236).
  - Add New, above the list, adds a cue at the playhead on the track of the list (p. 1233).
  - Prev and Next in the Inspector move to the cue before or after on the track (pp. 1231, 1233).
  - The In and Out fields of the Inspector (pp. 1231, 1233) move one edge of the cue. A value inside the cue next to it changes nothing (D79). Else the new range overwrites the other cues of the track.
  - Cues move, trim, split and delete like clips.
  - Details: D56, D57, D78, D79; deviations X43, X44.
- **Keyframes** ([measured](todo/keyframes.md#subtitles)):
  - A cue acts as a picture as large as the frame. It takes the Transform, Crop and Composite keyframes of a clip (§8.15, D75), without an anchor point, flips, Dynamic Zoom, composite mode or effects.
  - The Inspector of a cue has no Keyframe buttons. The Keyframes tab and the keyframe tray show the item selected first, a cue or a clip.
  - Each cue has its own keyframes. They count frames from its start, and can be before or after the cue.
  - The Keyframe button of a row adds a keyframe at the playhead, also outside the cue. The arrows of a row go to keyframes outside the cue too.
  - A head trim and a split keep each keyframe on its timeline frame. A split gives both parts every keyframe. A move takes the keyframes along.
  - A ripple delete inside a cue gives two cues, with or without keyframes. Each cue moves all its keyframes by the same amount as its frames.
  - A join needs a shared edit point: each cue starts where the cue before it ends. Otherwise the join is refused.
  - A join keeps only the text, the words and the keyframes of the first cue.
  - The viewer and a burn-in show the keyframes. A subtitle stream and a subtitle file hold no keyframes.
- **Import** (pp. 1230–1231):
  - `.srt`, `.srtx`, `.vtt`, `.xml` and `.ttml` files come into the Media Pool as subtitle clips, with a badge.
  - Any media import, or Import Subtitle, brings them in.
  - They relink as video clips do.
  - Insert Selected Subtitles to Timeline Using Timecode puts each cue at its timecode.
  - Drag a subtitle clip onto a subtitle track, or above the video tracks for a new track. Its first cue goes where you drop it.
  - Details: D58; deviations X40, X41.
- **Export** (pp. 1247–1248):
  - File > Export Subtitle, or Export Subtitle on a subtitle track header, writes a track as SRT or WebVTT.
  - On the Deliver page, a render can write each selected track as a file beside it (IMSC1, DFXP, SRT, WebVTT).
  - A render can also burn in the shown track, or embed it.
  - Details: D58, D61, D64; deviations X42, X45.
- **Create Subtitles from Audio** (pp. 1234–1235):
  - It transcribes the speech of the timeline's In–Out range, or of the whole timeline.
  - The cues go onto the highest subtitle track. If there is no subtitle track, it makes one.
  - Its options:
    - the language (Auto finds it)
    - a caption preset
    - the characters per line (42), and one or two lines
    - the frames between subtitles (0)
  - A muted track is not heard.
  - Details: D62; deviations X46 and X56.
- **Re-timing to speech:** the manual does not have it. vidcut moves the cues of a subtitle track to the speech of the timeline: deviation X49.
- **Style** (p. 1237):
  - A track has a style: its font, size, color, outline, background and margin.
  - The style also has the bold face, the letters as capitals or small letters (Font Case), and a drop shadow.
  - Alignment is `left`, `center` or `right`. The usual alignment is center.
  - Position is `[x, y]`, two fractions of the picture from the top left.
  - Each fraction is from 0 to 1. Left out, the block stays in the cue's place.
  - With a position, that point is where the alignment of the block sits.
  - Details: D61; deviation X51.
- **Style of one cue** (p. 1238):
  - A cue stores its own style when you set one.
  - A field left out of that style keeps the track's style.
  - The cue leaves the whole style out when it keeps the track's style.
  - The viewer and a burn-in draw that one result.
  - WebVTT writes the cue's alignment and its position.
  - SRT writes the alignment together with the cue's place.
  - An IMSC1 paragraph takes a font, size, color, bold face, or alignment that the cue sets.
  - `subtitles.set` takes `style`. An empty style drops the cue's own style.
  - Other cues on the track stay as they were.
- **Style of one word** (p. 1238):
  - A word stores its own style when you set one.
  - That style is the same style a cue stores.
  - A field left out of the word style keeps the cue's style.
  - The cue's style keeps the track's style where the cue leaves a field out.
  - `SubtitleStyle::for_cue` does both merges.
  - The word leaves the whole style out when it keeps the cue's style.
  - A word with no start frame can still store a style.
  - The viewer, a burn-in and an export draw that word with this merged style.
  - That drawing uses its font, size, color, bold face and letter case.
  - Outline, background, shadow, alignment, position and margin stay with the cue.
  - The other words of the cue stay as they were.
  - `subtitles.set_word` sets the style. An empty style drops it.
  - The word counts from 0, in the order of the words of the cue's text.
- **Animated subtitles** (pp. 1240–1241, Studio only):
  - Drag Fusion templates from the Animated section of the Titles tab onto the header of a subtitle track.
  - Then each word shows as it is spoken, from the word times of the transcription.
  - Word Highlight colors the word.
  - Update Word Timings times the words of a cue to the speech after a correction.
  - Synchronize Word Timings does this for a track, also for captions not made from the speech.
  - Details: D69, D70; deviation X58.
- **Style presets** (pp. 1239–1240):
  - The option menu of the Subtitle Inspector saves the style of a track under a name.
  - It loads a preset onto a track as one change that you can undo.
  - It replaces a preset and deletes a preset, each after a confirmation.
  - It exports or imports a preset as a `.preset` file.
  - vidcut: looks, for titles too. Some looks are built in (X59). Details: D71.
- **Later:**
  - the faces of a track style other than regular and bold, line spacing, kerning, zoom, opacity and text anchoring beyond alignment (p. 1237)
  - caption presets (p. 1235)
  - fades (p. 1240)
  - subtitle regions (pp. 1243–1244)
  - linking subtitles to clips (pp. 1242–1243)
  - Fusion templates that style a subtitle track (p. 1242)
  - find and replace (p. 1236)
  - QC thresholds (pp. 1228–1229)
  - hiding subtitle tracks from the timeline (p. 1232)
  - The rest: [todo/subtitles.md](todo/subtitles.md).

### 8.13 Text-based editing (pp. 714, 994–1009)

- **vidcut:**
  - Transcribe Audio, in the right-click menu of a pool item (`media.transcribe`), keeps its words.
  - Transcribed media show a speech mark, in the pool and on their clips with sound.
  - Its window tells when the file already has a transcript made with the same settings. Then Transcribe Again makes that transcript again (`again`).
  - The Transcription window (Transcription in the top bar) shows the words of the source clip, or the words of the timeline from its clips.
  - The Transcription window has:
    - a click on a word, a drag over words, X, Delete and Undelete
    - Remove Silent Portions and Delete Filler Words
    - Place on Top, Insert, Append, Create Subclip and Add Marker on a source clip
    - Cut, Paste and Add Marker on the timeline
    - search: First, Previous, Next, Last and a count of the matches
    - Edit, Copy and Follow
    - the Transcript menu and the differences between transcripts (X50)
  - On a source clip, Delete leaves the words out of every edit of the media (`media.leave_out`). This includes the edits of the viewer, of the pool and of scripts.
  - On a source clip, Create Subclip opens a window with a Name and Create (p. 1005).
    - The subclip holds the selected words (p. 998). It goes in the bin that the pool shows (p. 864).
    - `media.subclip` does this. It takes a `range`, a `name` and a `bin`.
    - A subclip works like any other pool item (pp. 460–461, 885–886).
    - The words of the subclip come from the transcript of its file.
  - The Source Clip viewer also makes a subclip, from the In and Out of its clip (pp. 460–461, 885–886).
    - Mark > Create Subclip and Alt+B open the New Subclip window.
    - Create Subclip in the menu of the scroll area also opens it. A drag from the viewer to the pool also opens it.
    - A drop on a bin of the pool moves the clip to that bin. The window does not open.
    - The window has a Name and Create. The subclip goes in the bin that the pool shows (p. 864).
    - Without an In, the subclip starts at the first frame of the clip. Without an Out, it ends at the last frame.
    - `media.subclip` does this.
  - Add Marker puts a marker at one selected word, or a marker with a duration over more words (p. 998).
    - On a source clip, the marker is on that clip, at its source frame (`markers.add` with `media`).
    - The scroll area of the Source Clip viewer shows it. A marker with a duration also shows as a bar (p. 1018).
    - A double-click on it there opens the marker dialog.
    - `markers.list` with `media` lists them. `markers.move`, `markers.set` and `markers.remove` take their ids.
  - On the timeline, Delete takes the words out of every unlocked track (`edit.ripple_delete_words`, D63).
    - With Ripple On, the gap closes. With it off, a gap is left (`lift`).
    - The edges move to the quiet points around the words (`quiet`, X92).
  - On the timeline, Cut takes the selected words out of every unlocked track and leaves a gap (`edit.cut` with `range`).
  - Copy on the timeline also keeps the selected words for Paste (`edit.copy` with `range`).
  - Paste puts them in at the first selected word, else at the playhead (`edit.paste`).

    - With words selected, only as many frames as the words are pasted (`duration`).

  - `analyze.words`, `media.leave_out`, `media.words.set`, `edit.ripple_delete_words`, `edit.cut`, `edit.copy` and `edit.paste` do the same by request.
  - Details: D63, D73; deviations X50, X61, X92.

### 8.15 Transform and Crop (pp. 766–768)

- **Tools** (pp. 766–767):

  - Each effect has a Reset of all its values (p. 767).

- **Transform** (p. 767):
  - A drag inside the box of the clip moves it.
  - A drag on a corner resizes it in proportion. A drag on a side stretches or squeezes it in one direction.
  - The center handle turns it.
  - Shift keeps a move on one axis.
  - Its values are Zoom (width and height, linked or not), Position X and Y, and Rotation Angle about the anchor point.
  - Its other values are Pitch, Yaw, and Flip Horizontal and Vertical.
  - vidcut: the shape and snapping of the title handles (X52), and the values of a picture in fractions of the frame (X65).
  - vidcut: the Inspector shows lengths in pixels, zoom in percent and rotation in degrees (X70).
  - vidcut: the zoom and the rotation work about the anchor point, and a cross marks it in the viewer (D92, X82).

- **Crop** (p. 768):
  - A handle on each side crops that side.
  - Crop Left, Right, Top and Bottom are in pixels.
  - The cropped area is transparent, so what is under it shows through.
  - Softness goes from −100 to 100. It softens the edges of the crop (p. 768).
  - Details: D75.
- **Dynamic Zoom** (p. 768): a slow push or pull and pan from a start box to an end box.
  - The green box is the start and the red box is the end.
  - Each box is moved and resized in the viewer, and a path shows the motion.
  - Swap reverses the boxes. The ease is Linear, Ease In, Ease Out or Ease In and Out.
  - Shift keeps a drag on one axis.
  - vidcut: Alt+T shows the boxes, and the Tools row has Dynamic Zoom (X65). The eases are curves of their own (X67).
  - Details: D75.
- **Composite** (p. 769):
  - Opacity goes from 0 to 100. The tracks below show through the rest.
  - The composite modes mix the clip with the tracks below in other ways (pp. 1199–1203).
  - vidcut: Foreground and the mask modes come later ([todo](todo/composite-masks.md)).
  - A clip with an alpha channel composites over the tracks below it (p. 769).
  - vidcut: a clip's alpha channel is straight alpha. Alpha Mode comes later ([todo](todo/clip-alpha.md)).
  - Details: D75.
- **How they play:** a clip covers the tracks below it only where its picture covers the frame. Its composite mode mixes it with them. Its opacity and its soft edges let them show through. Details: D75.
- **Keyframes** (D75):
  - Position is one keyframe for X and Y. Zoom is one keyframe for X and Y. The anchor point is one keyframe for X and Y.
  - Rotation, Pitch and Yaw are values of their own. Each crop side, Softness and Opacity are values of their own.
  - The flips have no keyframes.
  - Dynamic Zoom has no Keyframe button. Its two boxes stay at the ends of the clip.
  - A Keyframe button sits between Previous and Next arrows on each keyed value (X70).
  - A right-click on the button sets the ease. The ease is the volume curve (X74).
  - After the first keyframe, a change at another frame adds a keyframe.
  - This applies to the Inspector and to the viewer handles.
  - The Reset of a value removes its keyframes. The Reset of a section removes that section's keyframes.
  - A clip with keyframes shows a mark on the timeline.
  - The Volume of the Audio section has the same button.
- **Inspector** (pp. 734–744):
  - The Inspector button at the top right of the UI toolbar opens it.
  - Its panels are Video, Audio, Effects, Transition, Image and File. A panel that does not apply is gray.
  - Each group has an on/off toggle and a Reset.
  - The groups of the Video panel are Transform, Cropping, Dynamic Zoom, Composite, Speed Change, and Retime and Scaling.
  - Transform has Zoom X and Y, linked or not, Position X and Y, Rotation Angle, Anchor Point, Pitch, Yaw and Flip.
  - Cropping has Left, Right, Top and Bottom in pixels, Softness, and Retain Image Position.
  - Dynamic Zoom has Ease and Swap.
  - Retime and Scaling has Retime Process, Motion Estimation, Scaling and Resize Filter (pp. 739–740).
  - vidcut: Retime Process is in its Speed section, with AI models in the place of Optical Flow (X93).
  - vidcut: the project's Retime Process is in Project Settings. Its default is Auto, not Nearest (X93).
  - The Audio panel opens in Clip mode when the user selects an audio clip (p. 742).
  - The Audio panel has Volume, Pan, Pitch, Equalizer, Voice Isolation, Dialogue Leveler and Music Remixer (p. 743).
  - vidcut: an Inspector tab in the place of the Media Pool (X70).
  - vidcut: its Audio section has Volume, Mute and Voice Isolation for the selected clips with sound (X70).
  - vidcut: its Crop section has Softness, and its Composite section has Composite Mode and Opacity.
- **The units of the Inspector,** measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.1:
  - The timeline was 1920×1080. A 1280×720 chart and a 960×720 chart were fitted into it.
  - OTIO exports on 1080 and 2160 timelines gave the values in the file.
  - Position X and Y are timeline pixels from the middle, with Y up.
  - The OTIO `Pan` is Position X divided by the timeline width. `Tilt` is Position Y divided by the timeline height.
  - Thus a picture narrower than the frame moves less than its number. On the 4:3 chart in the 16:9 timeline, Position X 200 moves it 150 pixels.
  - Crop is in pixels of the picture as it is fitted in the timeline. Crop Left 100 on the 4:3 chart is a `cropLeft` of 100/1440 in the OTIO file.
  - Zoom is a factor (1.000). Rotation is in degrees.
- **Later:** Foreground and the mask modes of Composite (pp. 769, 1202).

  - vidcut: the other effects come later ([todo](todo/effects-library.md)).
  - vidcut: a double-click adds the effect to the first selected clip with a picture.
  - vidcut: the right-click menu of an effect has Add to Selected Clips. It adds the effect to each selected clip with a picture.
  - Measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2: a generator takes no effect. A drag on the Cut page, a drag on the Edit page and the scripting API all leave it with none.
  - vidcut: `effects.add` refuses a generator. A drag of a card onto a generator does nothing, and the double-click and Add to Selected Clips skip it.
- **Inspector** (vidcut, X70):
  - The Inspector shows the effects of the first selected clip, after its Super Scale section. A change applies to that clip only.
  - Each effect is a section with an on/off box, Move Up, Move Down, Reset and Delete.
  - An effect that is off stays on the clip and does nothing.
  - On an effect that is on, Reset puts back the usual values and leaves it on.
  - On an effect that is off, Reset does nothing, as measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2.
  - The effects of a clip work in their order, from the top.
- **Blend** (p. 3526):
  - Each effect has a Blend value under Global Blend, at the bottom of its values. It mixes the effect with the picture that comes in.
  - Measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2: Blend goes from 0 to 1 and is 1.000 by default.
  - The output is (1 − Blend) × the picture that comes in + Blend × the effect. The picture on p. 3526 shows 0.000.
  - Use Alpha is under Global Blend and is on by default.
  - Measured: with Use Alpha off, the clip keeps its own shape. Inside it, the effect counts the clear area around the picture as black.
- **Gaussian Blur** (p. 3536). Its values and their defaults, measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2:
  - Split Horizontal and Vertical is off. Off, Strength sets the blur. On, Horizontal Strength and Vertical Strength set it.
  - Strength, Horizontal Strength and Vertical Strength go from 0 to 1. Each is 0.400.
  - Horizontal Channel Adjustment and Vertical Channel Adjustment have Red, Green, Blue and Alpha. Each goes from 0 to 2 and is 1.000.
  - The channel values also apply with Split Horizontal and Vertical off.
  - Advanced Controls has Border Type: Black, Replicate, Reflect or Wrap-Around. Reflect is the default.
  - The manual names a Blur Alpha box. Resolve Studio 21.1.2 does not show it.
- **How the blur works,** measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2 with 16-bit renders:
  - It is a Gaussian curve, sampled at each pixel, across the frame and then down it. A Strength of 0 gives the picture as it comes in.
  - Its sigma is 0.05625 × Strength² × the timeline width in pixels.
  - A channel value multiplies the sigma of that channel in that direction. A value of 0 gives no blur.
  - The resolution of the source does not change the sigma.
  - It works on the code values of the timeline, with no conversion to linear light.
  - It comes after Transform and Crop. A Zoom of 2 doubles the sigma.
  - Zoom X and Zoom Y multiply the sigma by √(Zoom X × Zoom Y), in both directions.
  - Across and down are the directions of the frame. A rotated clip thus blurs across the frame, not across the clip.
  - It also blurs the alpha. Over a lower track, the edge of a smaller picture fades into that track.
  - Reflect and Wrap-Around match a blur of one pass. Black and Replicate do not ([X101](deviations.md#x101-black-and-replicate-borders-follow-their-plain-definitions)).
  - A portrait timeline gives a sigma 3% smaller than the rule ([X102](deviations.md#x102-a-portrait-timeline-blurs-by-the-width-rule)).
- **Mosaic Blur** (p. 3538). Its values and their defaults, measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2:
  - Pixel Frequency goes from 0.1 to 4000 and is 100.
  - Smooth Strength goes from 0 to 1 and is 0.
  - Cell Shape is Square, Hexagon or Triangle. Square is the default.
  - Aliasing goes from 0 to 2 and is 1.
  - Advanced Controls has these values:
    - X Offset and Y Offset go from −1 to 1. Each is 0.
    - Aspect goes from 0.001 to 1000 and is 1.
    - Position goes from −1000000 to 1000000. It is the middle of the picture.
    - Border Type has Black, Replicate, Reflect and Wrap-Around. Reflect is the default.
    - Antialias Result is off.
    - Blur Alpha is on, and Whole Cells Only is off.
- **How the mosaic works,** measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2 with 16-bit renders:
  - The mosaic works after Zoom and Position, on the pixels of the frame. A cell keeps sharp edges at each Zoom.
  - A cell is S high and S × Aspect wide, with S = 2 × √(width × height) / Pixel Frequency.
    - The width and the height are those of the picture on the frame, after its fit and its Zoom.
    - A 1920×1080 picture that fills its frame thus has S = 1.5 × 1920 / Pixel Frequency.
    - The size of the source does not change S. A 3840×2160 source on a 1920×1080 timeline has the cells of a 1920×1080 source.
    - Zoom X 2 alone gives square cells, √2 times as large.
  - The grid lines up on the middle of the picture on the frame. Thus a Pan moves the grid with the picture.
  - Position sets the place of the grid. Position Y goes up.
  - X Offset moves the grid right, and Y Offset moves it up. An offset of 1 is half a cell.
  - Hexagon: the cells have a flat top and bottom. Their columns are √3/2 of a cell width apart, and each odd column is half a cell lower.
  - Triangle: the columns are 1/√3 of a cell width apart. The triangles point down and up in turn.
  - A pixel of the frame takes the color of the cell around it.
  - The color of a cell is the mean of a box around the middle of the cell, with equal weights.
    - The box has the same form for each Cell Shape.
    - Aliasing sets its size. At 0 it is one pixel, at 1 it is one cell, and at 2 it is two cells.
    - Beyond the frame, the box takes the pixels of the edge. At the edge, the edge pixel thus weighs more.
    - The clear area around a smaller picture counts as clear pixels in the box.
  - The box takes the mean of the premultiplied colors and of the alpha.
  - Blur Alpha off keeps these colors, but the output has the alpha that came in.
    - Color can thus be more than alpha. Over a lower track it adds to that track and stops at 1.
    - A clear part of the picture thus adds the color of its cell to the lower track.
  - Whole Cells Only, with Blur Alpha on:
    - A cell with no clear part in its box stays, with an alpha of 1.
    - The other cells have an alpha of 0. There the premultiplied colors that came in add to the lower track.
  - Whole Cells Only, with Blur Alpha off, changes nothing.
  - Antialias Result makes all of this at twice the size, from a linearly enlarged picture. Then each pixel is the mean of its four samples.
  - Border Type and Smooth Strength do not change the renders. The manual does not name Smooth Strength for this effect.
- **Mosaic in vidcut:**
  - vidcut keeps Smooth Strength and Border Type on the effect. They do not change its picture.
  - A cell is whole when the alpha of its box is at least 0.999. The renders put that limit above 0.674 and at 1 or below.
  - Where a cell edge falls exactly on a pixel, Resolve gives that line of pixels to either cell, with no rule that the renders show. vidcut gives it to the cell on the right or below.
  - A Triangle has corners at the sides of the frame, from triangles with a middle off the frame. The distance of that middle from the frame counts in column steps.
    - At 0.585 of a step or less, each corner has the box at its own middle, clamped into the frame. At 0.691 or more, Resolve changes the corners. No render falls between these distances.
    - Most of a corner of a triangle that points up then has the color of the next triangle in its row. Single rows have other colors.
    - These rows change between renders. Two renders of Aspect 2 at Pixel Frequency 25 differ on 1211 pixels, by up to 9.22/255. Renders at 0.132 and 0.566 of a step repeat exactly.
    - vidcut takes the box at the middle of the pixel's own triangle, clamped into the frame, at each distance.
  - Only the Fusion page gave renders of Blur Alpha off and Whole Cells Only. Only the default Pixel Frequency has Edit page renders on other sizes and Zooms ([todo](todo/effects-library.md)).
  - vidcut's own choices, not measured ([todo](todo/effects-library.md)):
    - A Position away from the middle, on a picture with a Zoom or a Pan, counts in fractions of the picture on the frame.
    - On a rotated clip, the cells keep the directions of the frame, as Gaussian Blur does.
    - A crop and a Dynamic Zoom box do not change the size or the place of the cells.
    - The Zoom of each compound clip around the picture multiplies the size on the frame.
    - With Use Alpha off, Blur Alpha and Whole Cells Only change nothing.
    - Whole Cells Only tests each sample's cell, with each Cell Shape and each Aliasing.
    - A made frame of a speed change, and a frame from the render cache, keep no color at an alpha of 0.
- **Vignette.** Its values and their defaults, measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2:
  - Operating Mode is Basic or Advanced. Basic is the default.
  - Border Shape is Oval, Triangle, Diamond, Rectangle, Hexagon or Octagon. Oval is the default.
  - Size goes from 0.01 to 2 and is 0.5.
  - Rotation goes from −360 to 360 and is 0.
  - Anamorphism goes from 0.2 to 5 and is 1.78.
  - Center goes from −1000000 to 1000000. It is the middle of the picture.
  - Softness goes from 0 to 1 and is 0.5.
  - Transparency goes from 0 to 1 and is 0.
  - Color is black.
  - Composite Type has the 27 modes of the Composite section. Multiply is the default.
- **How the vignette works,** measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2 with 16-bit renders:
  - The output is b + (m − b) × (1 − v) × (1 − Transparency), held between 0 and 1. Here b is the picture that comes in, m is the Composite Type of Color and b, and v is the shape picture.
  - Basic uses Size, Softness, Anamorphism and Color. It always has an Oval, the Normal mode, no Rotation, no Transparency and the Center in the middle.
  - The size of the picture is its fit in the frame, before Zoom and Position. The shape does not follow them.
  - The radius of the shape is 0.7 × (Size + 0.01) × the height of the picture.
  - The mask has one cell for each 2 pixels, around the middle of the shape. Each cell is 1 inside the shape and 0 outside it.
  - The cells go from −W to W − 2 across and from −H to H − 2 down, in pixels from the middle of the shape.
  - Anamorphism divides x. Then Rotation turns the shape. A rotated rectangle is thus a parallelogram.
  - Three box blurs go across and then three go down, the widest first. Beyond the cells they take 0.
  - The sigma of the blurs is (966 / 1080) × the height × √Size × (0.05 + Softness²), in pixels.
  - Then v = min(1, B / (B0 − L) − L). Here B is the blurred cell, B0 is the cell at the middle of the shape, and L is the smallest cell.
  - The value of a pixel is the linear mix of the four cells around it.
  - The alpha does not change.
  - Composite Type differs from the Composite section in these ways:
    - Color Dodge, Color Burn, Divide and Vivid Light do not stop at 0 or 1 before the mix.
    - Divide is b / a, with an a of at least 0.000001. This is measured at one Color with a channel of 0.
    - Linear Burn stops at 0.
    - Hue with a gray Color gives one fixed hue. This is measured at one gray.
- **Vignette in vidcut:**
  - A source that fits with a half pixel takes the next whole pixel as its size. Only 607.5 and 608 were tried.
  - The smallest cell is the smallest of the four corners. A blurred shape with no holes has its smallest value at a corner.
  - The GUI shows only the values that work in the Operating Mode.
  - vidcut's own choices, not measured ([todo](todo/effects-library.md)):
    - The rule works on the premultiplied colors. A clear part of the picture thus adds a Color that is not black to the lower track.
    - Beyond the cells, a pixel takes the value of the nearest cell.
    - Color takes any number. The GUI gives 0 to 1.
    - On a compound clip, the size of the picture is the size of the frame.
- **Sharpen.** Its values and their defaults, measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2:
  - Sharpen Amount goes from 1 to 5 and is 1.8.
  - The Detail Levels group has Fine Detail Size, Fine Details, Medium Details and Large Details.
  - Fine Detail Size goes from 0.01 to 0.1 and is 0.05.
  - Fine Details, Medium Details and Large Details go from 1 to 5 and are 1.
  - The Chroma group has Sharpen Chroma. It goes from 1 to 5 and is 1.
- **How the sharpen works,** measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2 with 16-bit renders:
  - It works after the Transform, in pixels of the frame. A black gap in the frame counts as picture.
  - No value grows with the size of the frame.
  - One solve makes a smooth plane u from a plane p of the picture, with a strength λ and a number n of half sweeps.
    - The link between two neighbor pixels q and r is λ / (|p(q) − p(r)|^1.2 + 0.0001).
    - A neighbor outside the frame is the pixel itself, with the link λ / 0.0001.
    - u starts as p.
    - The first half sweep changes the pixels where x + y is odd. Then the half sweeps alternate.
    - A half sweep finds t = (p + the sum of link × u of the neighbor) / (1 + the sum of the links). Then u becomes u + 1.8 × (t − u).
  - Three solves start from p, each one apart from the others:
    - u1 has λ = Size² and 15 half sweeps.
    - u2 has λ = 4 × Size² and 15 half sweeps.
    - u3 has λ = 32 × Size² and 20 half sweeps.
    - The bands are B1 = p − u1, B2 = u1 − u2 and B3 = u2 − u3.
  - The plane of the brightness is y = 0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B.
    - y′ = y + the sum of (Sharpen Amount × g − 1) × B. Here g is the Details value of the band.
  - The planes of the colors are Pr = 0.65 (R − y) and Pb = 0.552 (B − y).
    - Pr′ = Pr + the sum of (Sharpen Chroma × g − 1) × B, with the bands of Pr. Pb′ is the same.
  - The output is R′ = y′ + Pr′ / 0.65, B′ = y′ + Pb′ / 0.552 and G′ = (y′ − 0.2126 R′ − 0.0722 B′) / 0.7152.
  - Nothing stops the colors at 0 or 1 before the output.
  - It works on the premultiplied colors. The alpha does not change.
- **Sharpen in vidcut:** vidcut's own choices, not measured ([todo](todo/effects-library.md)):
  - The medium band has λ = 4 × Size² at each Size. It is measured only at Size 0.05.
  - The color bands use the same rule at a Sharpen Chroma of 1. They are measured only at a Sharpen Chroma of 5.
  - A viewer that draws fewer pixels than the frame uses the same numbers on the pixels that it draws.
  - A source of another size goes through the scaler of the placed picture first.
- **Compound clips:** vidcut: the effects of a compound clip work on each picture inside it ([todo](todo/compound-opacity.md)).
- **In OTIO files:** Resolve writes no effects to them. vidcut keeps the effects in the picture of its own metadata of the clip.

### 8.17 Generators (p. 766)

A generator is a clip that makes its own picture. It has no media and no sound.

- **The Generators page:**
  - Measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2: the Effects panel has the tabs Video, Audio, Generators and Favorites.
  - vidcut: the Effects tab has the pages Video and Generators. `gui.show generators` opens the Generators page.
  - Measured: the Generators section has these kinds, in this order:
    - 10 Step and 100mV Steps
    - BT.2111 Color Bar HLG Narrow, BT.2111 Color Bar PQ Full and BT.2111 Color Bar PQ Narrow
    - EBU Color Bar, Four Color Gradient, Grey Scale and SMPTE Color Bar
    - Solid Color, Window and YCbCr Ramp
  - vidcut has these kinds, on cards in this order.
  - The tab also has a Fusion Generators section. It is part of Fusion (§1).
  - The pointer on a card previews the generator (p. 766). vidcut does not have this preview yet ([todo](todo/effects-library.md)).
- **Adding a generator:**
  - Drag a card onto the upper timeline or the lower timeline (p. 766).
  - vidcut: a drop puts the generator where the pointer is, on the video track under it, as `generators.add` with that track and frame does.
  - Not measured: the track and the frame of a drop in Resolve.
  - Measured: a double-click on a card loads the generator in the viewer as a source of 5 s. The timeline does not change. The Inspector shows nothing to inspect, so it has no Color row.
  - Measured: a drag from the viewer onto a clip on V1 overwrites from the frame under the pointer. The clip then has its Color row.
  - vidcut: a double-click on a card adds the generator at the playhead. The source viewer takes only media ([todo](todo/effects-library.md)).
  - `generators.add` without a track uses the track that D66 gives a title.
  - A new generator is 5 s long, and it adds no sound clip (measured).
- **Inspector:**
  - Measured: the Generator tab of Solid Color has one row, Color. Color has no keyframes.
  - Measured: the Generator tab of Four Color Gradient has Center X and Center Y, as sliders with keyframes.
  - Measured: this tab also has Upper Left Color, Upper Right Color, Lower Left Color and Lower Right Color. The colors have no keyframes.
  - Measured: the Generator tab of EBU Color Bar has a Level menu, with 75% (SMPTE) and 100%.
  - Measured: Window has no Generator tab. 10 Step, 100mV Steps, Grey Scale, YCbCr Ramp, SMPTE Color Bar and the BT.2111 Color Bars have its OTIO shape.
  - The tabs of those eight kinds were not opened. vidcut shows no Generator section for them.
  - Measured: the Settings tab has Transform, Cropping, Dynamic Zoom, Composite, Speed Change and Stabilization.
  - vidcut: the Inspector shows a Generator section with these rows, over the picture sections (X70).
- **Picture sections:**
  - Measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2: a Solid Color gives the same picture as a still of its color. The largest difference is 0.1 of 255. The renders have Rotation, Pitch, Yaw, Flip Vertical, Crop, Zoom, Overlay and Hue.
  - Measured: vidcut's frames and renders of vidcut's OTIO file differ by at most 0.6 of 255. The cases are Rotation, Anchor, Flip Vertical, Flip Horizontal with Crop Left, Zoom, Opacity and the 26 composite modes.
  - Measured: Pitch and Yaw differ at the edge pixels, by at most 78.2 of 255. These pixels are 0.3% of the values. A still in Resolve gives the same picture.
  - Not measured on a generator: Dynamic Zoom, Stabilization, keyframes in a render, Push, Slide, Wipe and Dip To Color.
  - vidcut: the sections and these transitions act as on a still. On a Solid Color and on a still, the same requests give the same frames (0.0 of 255).
- **Speed:**
  - Measured: Speed Change on a generator has a dim dot and does not open. A still acts the same.
  - Measured: the right-click menu has Change Speed and Add Speed Point grey, and Change Clip Duration on.
  - Measured: the scripting API refuses a speed. An import of a time warp on a Solid Color keeps the length and drops the warp.
  - vidcut: the speed requests refuse a generator. The right-click menu has Change Speed and Add Speed Keyframe grey, and Change Duration on.
  - vidcut: an import of a time warp on a Solid Color keeps the length and drops the warp.
- **Solid Color:**
  - It has one value, Color. Black is the default (measured).
  - `generators.set` and the Color row change it.
  - Measured: these are its code values in the timeline. `c` is the color, each channel from 0 to 1.
    - SDR timeline: `c`
    - PQ timeline: P(0.01) × P(M · P⁻¹(c))
    - HLG timeline: H(0.1^(1/1.2)) × H(S(M · D(H⁻¹(c))))
  - The parts of these sums:
    - P is the PQ curve, with 1.0 at 10000 nits.
    - H is the HLG curve.
    - M is the matrix from BT.709 to BT.2020.
    - D changes HLG scene light to display light, with a system gamma of 1.2. S is its inverse. Both use the BT.2020 luma weights.
  - Thus white is 100 nits in a PQ timeline. In an HLG timeline, the code value of white is 0.6296.
- **Four Color Gradient:**
  - The defaults are Center X 0, Center Y 0, Upper Left `#ff0000`, Upper Right `#ffff00`, Lower Left `#0000ff` and Lower Right `#00ffff` (measured).
  - `generators.set` and the rows change them. A typed number past an end takes that end (measured: 5 in Center Y becomes 1).
  - Measured: this is the picture, at a timeline of W × H. All values are fractions.
    - u = x / (W − 1), and v = y / (H − 1).
    - bend(t, p) = 0.5 t / p for t ≤ p, else 0.5 + 0.5 (t − p) / (1 − p).
    - The mix of each channel is (1 − V) ((1 − U) UL + U UR) + V ((1 − U) LL + U LR).
    - U = bend(u, (Center X + 1) / 2), and V = bend(v, (Center Y + 1) / 2).
  - Thus a positive Center Y moves the center down.
  - Measured: in an HDR timeline, the corners mix in code values first. Then each pixel has the conversion of a Solid Color.
  - Measured: the keyframes of a center count on clip frames, and they are linear. At frame 10 of −1 at frame 0 and 1 at frame 29, Center X is −0.310.
  - `keyframes.add` with `center_x` or `center_y` adds a keyframe. With keyframes, `generators.set` puts the value on a keyframe at `at`.
- **Ramps and bars:**
  - Measured: these are the pictures, at a timeline of W × H. All values are fractions, and x and y count from 0.
  - 10 Step: a band is ⌈W / 11⌉ wide. The grey is min(⌊x / band⌋, 10) / 10.
  - 100mV Steps: a band is ⌈W / 10⌉ wide. The grey is clamp(⌊x / band⌋ − 1, 0, 7) / 7.
  - Grey Scale: the grey is x / W.
  - YCbCr Ramp: t = x / W, and each channel is clamp(0.5 + s (t − 0.5), 0, 1).
    - s is 2.539648 for red, 0.359182 for green and 2.814179 for blue.
  - EBU Color Bar has eight bars. A bar is ⌈W / 8⌉ wide.
    - The bars are white at 1, then yellow, cyan, green, magenta, red and blue at the Level, and then black.
    - The Level is 75% or 100%. 75% is the default (measured).
  - The rows of the bars end at ⌊k / 12 × H⌋, with 32-bit floats.
  - SMPTE Color Bar has four rows, with these sizes:
    - B = 1920 when W is less than 1920. Else B = max(1920, min(2 ⌊8 H / 9⌋, W × 4 / 3)).
    - c = ⌈B × 3 / 28⌉, and U = 2 ⌈c / 6⌉
    - F = max(W, 1920), and d = max(0, ⌊(F − 7 c) / 2⌋)
  - The picture is F wide. It is cut off at the right when W is less than F.
  - Row 1 ends at k = 7. The sides are 0.4. From d, seven bars of c are white, yellow, cyan, green, magenta, red and blue at 0.75.
  - Row 2 ends at k = 8. The left side is cyan at 1. The first bar is white at 1, and the other bars are 0.75. The right side is blue at 1.
  - Row 3 ends at k = 9. The left side is yellow at 1, and the right side is red at 1. Between them is the grey (x − (d + c)) / (min(d + 7 c, F) − (d + c)), held from 0 to 1.
  - Row 4 goes to the bottom. The sides are 0.15.
    - From d, it has black for 4.5 U, white for 6 U and black for 4.5 U.
    - Then it has 0.02 for U, black for U, 0.04 for U, and black to d + 7 c.
  - Window is white where ⌊W / 4⌋ < x < ⌊3 W / 4⌋ and ⌊H / 4⌋ < y < ⌊3 H / 4⌋. Elsewhere it is black and opaque.
  - The three BT.2111 Color Bars have five rows. W sets the columns, and H sets the rows.
    - d = ⌊W / 8⌋, and c = ⌊W × 206 / 1920⌋
    - The bars start at d, d + c, d + 2 c, d + 3 c, W − d − 3 c, W − d − 2 c and W − d − c. The last bar ends at W − d.
  - A code value is a fraction: (code − 64) / 876 in the Narrow kinds, and code / 1023 in PQ Full.

    | Kind | Grey 40% | Mid | 2% | 4% |
    |---|---|---|---|---|
    | HLG Narrow | 414 | 721 | 80 | 99 |
    | PQ Narrow | 414 | 572 | 80 | 99 |
    | PQ Full | 409 | 593 | 20 | 41 |

  - Row 1 ends at k = 1. The sides are Grey 40%. The bars are white, yellow, cyan, green, magenta, red and blue at 1.
  - Row 2 ends at k = 7. The sides are Grey 40%. The bars have the same colors at Mid.
  - Row 3 ends at k = 8. The sides are Mid.
    - It is black from d to the middle of the second bar.
    - Then it has nine steps from 0.1 to 0.9, each half a bar, to the end of the sixth bar.
    - The last bar is 1.
  - Row 4 ends at k = 9. It is a grey ramp, held from 0 to 1.
    - In the Narrow kinds, the ramp is (x − ⌊W × 858 / 1920⌋) / (W × 876 / 1920).
    - In PQ Full, the ramp is (x − ⌊W × 791 / 1920⌋) / (W × 1022 / 1920).
    - Measured: at widths that do not divide evenly, this rule has an error of up to 0.001.
  - Row 5 goes to the bottom, and it is black under these parts:
    - Three tiles fill the left side, from ⌊d n / 3⌋ to ⌊d (n + 1) / 3⌋. Three tiles fill the right side in the same way, from W − d.
    - gap = ⌊(c − W × 70 / 1920) / 2⌋, and u = c − 2 gap.
    - The 2% patch is u wide, from the second bar plus gap. The 4% patch is u wide, from the third bar.
    - Mid goes from ⌊W / 2⌋, for ⌊W × 438 / 1920⌋.

    | Kind | Left tiles | Right tiles |
    |---|---|---|
    | HLG Narrow | 713, 719, 316; 538, 709, 718; 512, 706, 296 | 651, 286, 705; 639, 269, 164; 227, 147, 702 |
    | PQ Narrow | 568, 571, 381; 484, 566, 571; 474, 564, 368 | 536, 361, 564; 530, 350, 256; 317, 236, 562 |
    | PQ Full | 589, 592, 370; 491, 586, 592; 478, 584, 355 | 551, 347, 584; 544, 334, 225; 296, 201, 582 |

  - Measured: in an HDR timeline, these kinds keep their SDR code values. The BT.2111 Color Bars are the same in SDR, PQ and HLG.
  - vidcut does not copy two faults in these pictures ([X129](deviations.md#x129-generators-have-the-correct-picture-where-resolve-has-a-fault)).
- **Length and dissolves:**
  - Measured: a generator can have any length.
  - Measured: Resolve keeps the source start of a generator at 0. A left trim moves the keyframes, and the picture does not move.
  - Measured: a dissolve between two generators runs past the ends of both clips. Thus it needs no extra frames.
- **In OTIO files:**
  - vidcut writes its kinds in the shape of Resolve's own file.
    - Each one is a `Clip.2`. Its media is a `GeneratorReference` with the `generator_kind` of the kind.
    - The values are in the group named `Generator`, in the `Resolve_OTIO` parameters.
    - A placed generator also has the effects of D75.
    - Its keyframes count from the first frame of the clip. Measured: Resolve reads them so, and its import sets the source start to 0.
  - Measured: Four Color Gradient has the `Effect Name` `Four Color Gradient`, `Type` 66 and `Display Type` 0. Its parameters are `centerX`, `centerY`, `upperLeft`, `upperRight`, `lowerLeft` and `lowerRight`.
  - Measured: EBU Color Bar has the `Effect Name` `EBU Color Bar`, `Type` 65 and `Display Type` 0.
    - Its parameter `level` is a `UInt`: 0 is 75% and 1 is 100%. The values 2 to 4 play as 0.
  - Measured: the other kinds have the `Effect Name` `Generator`, `Type` 42 and `Display Type` 3. They have only the `Display Name` parameter.
  - Measured: the `generator_kind` of a BT.2111 Color Bar has a lower-case word: `BT.2111 Color bar PQ Full`. Its clip name has `Color Bar`.
  - vidcut reads its kinds back from that shape.
  - vidcut keeps a generator of any other section as data. It plays as a gap, and an import warns about it (X9).

## 9. Audio (pp. 642, 649–650, 743–744, 771, 773, 778–779)

- **Meter:** green is safe, yellow is near the limit, and red can cause clipping.
- **Mixer** (pp. 649–650):
  - It has a channel strip for each track with sound: name, meter, fader in dB, mute and solo.
  - D27 has the law and the ticks of the fader.
  - It has a meter for the mix.
  - Solo silences the tracks that are not soloed.
  - Mute and solo apply to the export too (p. 650).
  - Track pan, and FX other than the Ducker: out.
- **Voice Isolation** (p. 774; measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2, on a clip):
  - It has a switch and an Amount from 0 to 100.
  - A fresh clip turned on gives Amount 100.
  - Amount typed 0 stays on, with the value 0.
  - A clip's stored Amount comes back when the switch goes on again.
  - vidcut: `clip.set` and `track.set` take the switch (`voice_isolation_on`) and the Amount (`voice_isolation`) ([X64](deviations.md#x64-voice-isolation-is-free-on-a-clip-or-a-track)).
  - vidcut: at Amount 0 the sound equals the source, and the model does not run.
- **Ducking** (pp. 744, 778–779):
  - The Ducker Track FX lowers a target track, such as music, where one or more source tracks, such as dialogue, are active.
  - It changes the level only. It compresses nothing.
  - Its controls and their defaults:
    - Duck Amount: 2.7 dB. The Inspector calls it Duck Level. The manual says that 2 to 5 dB works best most of the time.
    - Lookahead: 15 ms.
    - Rise Time: 10 ms.
    - Hold: 150 ms.
    - Recovery Time: 750 ms.
  - A graph shows the level of the source against the ducking.
  - vidcut: `track.set` with `duck`, and the strip of the track in the Mixer. Alt+D turns it on or off (X69).
  - vidcut gives every track the timing of a mono track (X68).
  - vidcut adds Detect: Speech, the default, or Sound level (X69).
  - The curve, the timing and the limits are in D76.
  - vidcut: the Ducking window draws the graph above its controls. `analyze.ducking` gives its numbers.
    - The graph shows the seconds around the playhead, with the playhead at the center. It needs no playback.
    - The graph follows the values in the window before Apply.
    - The filled shape is the level of the loudest source, after its clip gain and its fader.
    - The line is what the ducking takes from the track.
    - Both use one dB scale, from 0 dB at the top.
    - With Detect at Sound level, a second line shows the threshold.
- **Viewer meter** (p. 642):
  - It shows what the viewer plays.
  - Its speaker button mutes only the playback, not the export.
- **Clip volume** (pp. 743, 1109–1110, 1112):
  - Each clip has a gain, and a mute.
  - The Inspector has the Volume of the clip, in its Audio panel. This is the value of the volume overlay of the clip (p. 743).
  - On the Edit page, each clip with sound has a volume overlay. A drag up or down on it sets the level of the clip (p. 1109).
  - Shift gives finer steps (p. 1109). The overlay is flat at first (p. 1112).
  - The volume commands of the Edit page change several selected clips together. The clips keep the differences between their levels (p. 1110).
  - vidcut: Volume and Mute in the Audio section of the Inspector (X70). `clip.set` sets `gain_db` and `muted`. D12 gives the range.
  - vidcut: a line on each clip with sound that is tall enough. A drag on it sets `gain_db` with `clip.set` (X71).
  - The scale of the line and the step of Shift are in D77.
- **Volume keyframes** (pp. 1109, 1111–1113, 1251, 1255):
  - An Alt-click on the overlay makes a keyframe (pp. 1109, 1112). Two keyframes or more change the level over time (p. 1112).
  - Keyframes of the Inspector's Volume also show on the overlay (p. 1111).
  - A drag on the line between two keyframes raises or lowers that part (p. 1112).
  - A drag on a keyframe changes its level and its time. Shift keeps one direction (p. 1113).
  - A right-click on keyframes sets Linear, Ease In, Ease Out or Ease In and Out (p. 1113).
  - Cut, copy and paste work on keyframes. Backspace deletes them (p. 1113).
  - Audio keyframes can be between frames (p. 1113).
  - The Cut page has a Keyframe Editor (p. 1255). Its timeline has no volume overlay.
  - vidcut: the volume line takes the keyframe controls of the Edit page (X73). Its eases are Bezier handles that drag (X74).
  - vidcut: `keyframes.list`, `keyframes.add`, `keyframes.set`, `keyframes.move`, `keyframes.remove`, `keyframes.copy`, `keyframes.cut` and `keyframes.paste` (D81).
  - A keyframe is on a whole source frame (D81). The Keyframes tab copies and pastes keyframes alone (§2). On the volume line, Ctrl+C copies the clips.
  - A volume change on a clip with keyframes is in D82. Normalize Audio Levels with keyframes is in D83.
  - Ducking, Ripple Delete Silence and the meters follow the keyframes (D84). The keys are in D85.
- **Ripple Delete Silence** (p. 773):
  - Clip > Audio Operations > Ripple Delete Silence works on the selected clips.
  - vidcut: it is also in the right-click menu of the clip and in Timeline Actions.
  - Settings: threshold (dB), pre-head, post-tail, minimum to trip.
  - vidcut adds Detect: Sound level or Speech (deviation X47).
  - It shows the parts to remove as red stripes.
  - vidcut lets you keep, trim and play them first (deviation X48).
  - Remove cuts them and closes the gaps, as one undo step (D29).
  - Cross Fade Audio puts a short cross fade where two parts meet (D29).
- **Normalize Audio Levels** (pp. 1110–1111):
  - The Edit page and the Fairlight page have it. The Cut chapters do not name it.
  - It sets the volume of the selected clips so that their level reaches a target. It applies no dynamics.
  - Normalization Mode: several loudness standards, Sample Peak or True Peak.
  - Set Level: Relative treats the clips as one clip. Independent gives each clip its own change.
  - vidcut: `clip.normalize`, in Audio Operations of the right-click menu and in the Audio section of the Inspector (deviation X72).
  - The defaults, the range and the measured part of a clip are in D80.

## 10. Quick Export (Ch. 39, pp. 788–790, 4095–4096, 4104–4109)

- **Flow:** File > Quick Export or the toolbar button. Then a preset from the icon row, Export, a file name and a progress dialog.
- **Range:** the timeline In–Out range if it is set, else the whole timeline.
- **Output:** one file. Individual clips are for the Deliver page, and for our Export dialog.
- **Preset models** (Deliver page):
  - H.264 Master (QuickTime, AAC 320 kb/s)
  - H.265 Master (Main10)
  - ProRes 422 HQ (LPCM audio)
  - YouTube 720p–2160p (MP4, H.264 High)
  - Vimeo
  - TikTok (vertical 1080×1920)
- **User presets:** you can add them to the Quick Export row.
- **In vidcut:**
  - The Quick Export row, the Export dialog and jobs are deviation X3.
  - The suggested file name is the name of the project.
  - AAC takes 96 to 320 kb/s, and 320 by default.
  - The sample rate of AAC is the project's by default. The other choices are 44100 Hz and 48000 Hz.
  - These are the values of the Deliver page of Resolve Studio 21.1.2. An export refuses another bitrate or chosen rate.
  - A project at another rate, for example 96000 Hz, with AAC was not measured.
  - The TikTok preset has AAC at 256 kb/s. That number was not measured.
  - H.264 and H.265 round an odd side up to even. The side is of the timeline or of a custom size.
    - A size of 1281 x 721 gives a file of 1282 x 722. A size of 1281 x 720 gives a file of 1282 x 720.
    - With a timeline of an odd side, one factor scales the whole picture on both axes, and the picture stays centered. Thus part of a pixel can stay black at two edges.
    - With a custom size, the picture fits into the rounded size with one draw.
    - Resolve Studio 21.1.2 writes these sizes at 1281 x 721 with H.264 and H.265, on a timeline and as a custom size. It writes 258 x 258 for 257 x 257 with H.264. Other odd sizes were not measured.
    - The Export dialog and the report of the export show both sizes.
  - DNxHR LB, SQ, HQ and HQX round an odd width up to even. They keep an odd height.
    - A size of 1281 x 721 gives a file of 1282 x 721. A size of 1283 x 723 gives a file of 1284 x 723.
    - Resolve Studio 21.1.2 does this with these four. Its DNxHR 444 keeps 1281 x 721.
  - ProRes, DNxHR 444, VP9 and AV1 on the CPU keep an odd side.
  - AV1 on NVENC writes an even size by itself. The report shows that size after the export.
  - A side of a custom size is from 256 to 32768.
    - Measured with the render settings of the scripting API in Resolve Studio 21.1.2. The Deliver page was not seen.
    - Resolve makes a side under 256 into 256 with H.264 and ProRes. It makes a side over 32768 into 32768 with ProRes.
    - Resolve writes H.264 at 8192 x 4320. A job of H.264 at 8193 x 4321 stops its scripting API. It writes ProRes at 8193 x 4321.
    - vidcut refuses a side outside these limits. It does not change a setting without a word.

## 12. Decisions on gaps and contradictions

Each decision applies until the manual or a test shows that Resolve behaves differently. Then the decision and the code change.

- **D1. Fixed playhead.**
  - The playhead of the lower timeline is fixed by default.
  - A Timeline Options toggle changes it.
  - Reason: the manual calls it fixed (p. 689), but it also describes a toggle (pp. 645, 716).
- **D2. What Split cuts.**
  - Split cuts the selected clips under the playhead, if there are any.
  - Else it cuts every clip under the playhead on every unlocked track.
  - This is a simplification of Auto Select on the Edit page (p. 950).
  - The left halves stay selected.
- **D3. Target track.**
  - Smart Insert, Ripple Overwrite and Overwrite use the target track.
  - The target track is V1 by default, and A1 for sound alone (D26).
  - The manual does not say which track Append uses. Append always uses Track 1.
  - Place On Top selects its own track (D24).
  - Source Overwrite selects its own track ([§7.2](#72-edit-functions)).
- **D4. Trim Start/End to Playhead.**
  - It applies to the selected clips.
  - With no clip selected, it applies to the Track 1 clip under the playhead.
- **D5. Missing source marks.**
  - With no source In, the In is the first frame of the clip. With no Out, the Out is its last frame.
  - In Source Tape mode with no marks, an edit takes the whole clip under the playhead.
- **D6. Marks persist after edits.**
  - Source marks stay with each clip.
  - Timeline marks stay until you clear them (Alt+X).
  - This is the least surprising behavior for scripts.
- **D7. Overwrite with timeline In and Out set.**
  - It is a three-point edit: the range sets the duration, from the source In.
  - Too little source media is an error.
- **D8. Ripple Overwrite with timeline In and Out set.**
  - The source duration wins.
  - The program ripples by the difference (p. 698).
- **D9. Lift on Track 1.**
  - Alt+Delete lifts, and leaves the gap, as Alt stops the ripple during a trim.
  - With Ripple On off, Delete also lifts.
- **D10. Overwrite past the end of Track 1.**
  - With Ripple On, it starts at the end of Track 1 instead.
  - Thus Track 1 stays without gaps.
- **D11. Hover time.**
  - After 0.8 s of hover, a drop or move onto a clip changes from replace to overwrite.
  - The manual gives no value.
- **D12. Clip gain range.**
  - The range is −100 dB to +30 dB, plus mute (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
  - The manual gives no range.
  - The volume line of a clip shows this range on its own scale (D77).
- **D13. Keys.**
  - Keys that the Cut chapters do not give come from the Edit page.
  - §11 marks them with †.
- **D14. J/L cuts at an edit point.**
  - Between two touching clips with sound, a J/L cut (§8.7) is the `tail` of the outgoing clip that matches the `head` of the incoming clip.
  - An edit that gives such a clip another neighbor with sound there resets the J/L cut to a straight cut.
  - The edit also resets the other cut of the clip, if the clip would have no sound of its own.
  - With a gap or a clip with no sound there, the `head` or `tail` stays. It gets shorter when it would overlap other sound.
  - Reason: sound does not overlap on a track.
- **D15. Playhead after an edit function.**
  - After Smart Insert, Append, Overwrite, Ripple Overwrite or Paste, the playhead moves to the frame after the new clips.
  - The Viewer keeps its mode, so Q reviews the result (p. 696).
  - A drop does not move the playhead.
  - This is the behavior of the Edit page.
- **D16. Mark Out includes the frame under the playhead,** as in Resolve.
  - Ranges are stored half-open: O at frame `f` stores `out = f + 1`.
- **D17. Source Tape In/Out** (p. 692).
  - In Source Tape mode, I/O mark the tape.
  - The marks are kept as tape frames, with the pool order of that moment.
  - Thus an edit takes the same span, whatever the pool shows later.
  - Within one clip, the marks are also the source marks of that clip (D5).
  - An edit puts the span as one block, in one undo step.
  - When one of its clips is removed or relinked, the marks are cleared.
- **D18. Drop and move targets.**
  - Within 6 px of an edit point, a drop inserts there.
  - Several clips always overwrite.
  - Up to 24 px below the bottom row, a drop makes an audio track.
  - Further away from the tracks, or with Esc, the drop or move is cancelled.
- **D19. Trimming with the pointer.**
  - Edges follow the movement of the pointer from the press, snapped (§8.8).
  - A trim, roll, slip or J/L cut stops where the media runs out. It does not fail.
  - Safe Edit (p. 732) holds an edge at the neighbor for 0.6 s. Continue to push to override it.
  - A drag on empty space scrubs in the upper timeline, and draws a marquee in the lower timeline.
- **D20. Keyboard trimming.**
  - With an edit selected (V, U or a click), `,` and `.` trim it by a frame, or with Shift by a second.
  - They trim the Out, the In or both.
  - They are the nudge keys of the Edit page. The keyboard trimming of the Cut page is Dynamic Trim (D32).
  - On a J/L handle, they move the audio edit, and leave the picture cut (D36).
- **D21. Following Track 1** (pp. 649, 727).
  - A clip on another track is attached to the Track 1 picture under its first frame.
  - It moves with that picture, split parts included.
  - When that Track 1 clip is deleted, the attached clip goes where Track 1 closed up.
  - A trim of the tail of that Track 1 clip past the attached clip deletes the attached clip (p. 727).
  - A trim of its head moves the attached clip to the new start.
  - A Ripple Overwrite has its own rule. The new end is where the new clip ends. The old end is where the replaced part ended. These are measurements in Resolve Studio 21.1.2:
    - A clip that starts before the new end stays where it is, and it stays whole.
    - A clip from the old end on moves by the change in length, also past the last Track 1 clip.
    - A clip on a video track that starts from the new end to the old end goes away.
    - A clip on an audio track that starts there stays where it is.
    - The first clip that moves back on a track ends the clips that stayed on that track. The clip under its first frame stops there. Clips that stayed after that frame go away.
    - A timeline marker in the replaced part stays where it is, also past the new end.
    - A timeline marker from the old end on moves by the change in length. It replaces a marker on the frame where it lands.
  - Over a gap, the clip keeps its distance to the Track 1 clips around it.
  - Clips on locked tracks do not follow.
  - In other edits, a clip that would land on another clip moves to the next free track of its kind.
- **D22. Rippling other tracks** (p. 721).
  - With Ripple On, a delete or a trim of a clip on another track can ripple that track.
  - It ripples only when Track 1 has no clips from the start of that clip on. Otherwise it leaves a gap.
  - A delete of a timeline In–Out range takes the range out of every unlocked track, and closes them together (p. 725).
- **D23. Edit functions on other tracks.**
  - Only Track 1 pushes clips (p. 697).
  - On other tracks, Smart Insert and insert overwrite.
  - On other tracks, Ripple Overwrite replaces, and does not move other clips.
  - Paste puts each clip back on its own track (p. 723). It makes the missing tracks.
  - Split follows D2.
- **D24. Where Place On Top goes** (pp. 699–700).
  - It goes onto the video track above the highest track with clips in the incoming span. It makes that track if necessary.
  - It goes onto Track 1 only if no track has clips there (D10 applies).
  - Sound alone goes to the first audio track with room.
- **D25. Moving clips between tracks.**
  - A move gives positions as they are before the move.
  - A Track 1 clip moved to another track lifts out. With Ripple On, Track 1 closes up.
  - Clips moved onto Track 1 insert or overwrite (§8.2).
  - Video cannot go onto an audio track.
  - A selection across tracks moves only along them.
- **D26. Sound alone.**
  - An edit without picture goes to A1, unless a target track is given.
  - An edit without picture is media without video, or Audio Only (p. 694).
  - If you drag it onto a video track, it goes onto the first audio track.
  - A clip on an audio track keeps only its audio.
- **D27. Track numbers are positions.**
  - V1, V2, … count from the bottom. A1, A2, … count from the top.
  - When a track is removed, the tracks after it get new numbers.
  - The removal of a track with clips needs a confirmation.
  - An edit or a move can name the next track number. This makes that track.
  - Track 1 cannot be removed.
  - **The track fader** (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2: one drag on A1, 66 px of travel, read from the number above the fader):
    - The top is +10 dB. The bottom is minus infinity.
    - The table gives the place in px from the top, and the dB there.

      | px | dB | px | dB | px | dB |
      |---|---|---|---|---|---|
      | 0 | +10 | 24 | −3.8 | 48 | −31 |
      | 3 | +8.5 | 27 | −5.6 | 51 | −38 |
      | 6 | +6.6 | 30 | −7.4 | 54 | −45 |
      | 9 | +4.8 | 33 | −9.1 | 57 | −54 |
      | 12 | +3.1 | 36 | −12 | 60 | −68 |
      | 15 | +1.3 | 39 | −16 | 63 | −95 |
      | 18 | −0.3 | 42 | −19 | 66 | −∞ |
      | 21 | −2.1 | 45 | −24 | | |

    - The ticks have no numbers. They are at these places in px from the top: 0, 17 (white, 0 dB), 35, 42.7, 47.7 and 51.7.
    - Three or more ticks are close together from 57 to 63 px.
    - By the table, the ticks at 35, 42.7, 47.7 and 51.7 px are about −10, −20, −30 and −40 dB. The lower ticks are not read.
  - **The track fader in vidcut:**
    - It uses straight lines between the rows of the table, from a place to dB and from dB to a place.
    - The ticks are in the same share of the travel. The close ticks are at 57, 60 and 63 px.
    - `volume_db` goes from −100 to +10 dB, because JSON has no infinity. −100 stands for the bottom.
    - At −100, the number above the fader reads −∞, and the track is silent in playback and export.
    - A track at −100 counts as not heard, as a muted track does. This applies to the meters, ducking sources and transcription.
- **D28. Meters.**
  - There is a bar for each channel of the project's channel count (p. 649).
  - The bars show linear peaks from −60 dBFS. A peak stays for 1.5 s.
  - During playback, they show the sound as it is heard: the mix, and each track after its fader.
  - When paused or during a scrub, they show the waveforms of the media at the playhead, with the gains.
  - Channels mix, and mute and solo apply, as in the export (p. 650).
  - The monitoring mute silences only the viewer meter.
- **D29. Ripple Delete Silence** (p. 773).
  - The manual gives no defaults and no rule.
  - Silence is where the peak of every channel, after clip gain, stays below the threshold.
  - The check uses 10 ms steps.
  - Silence includes a clip past the end of its audio.
  - Silence also includes a click (under 0.05 s between 0.1 s of silence).
  - With Sound Level, the values are the ones that the window of Resolve Studio 21.1.2 showed, at 30 fps:
    - Threshold: −100 to +10 dB, −25 by default.
    - Pre head and post tail: 0 to 60 frames, 0 by default.
    - Minimum: 2 to 60 frames, 30 by default.
    - That window keeps the last values used. Thus it is not known whether −25 and 30 are factory values or kept values.
    - `edit.ripple_delete_silence` refuses a value outside these ranges.
  - With Speech, the pre head, the post tail and the minimum have their own defaults (deviation X47).
  - `analyze.silence` starts from the same Threshold and minimum, and has no limits. It is vidcut's own.
  - Each silence of at least the minimum goes, less the post-tail and the pre-head.
  - At the ends of a clip, all of the silence goes.
  - The cuts are in whole frames.
  - Remove takes the stripes as they are left (deviation X48).
  - Remove takes those stretches of the timeline out of every unlocked track, and closes them up, whatever Ripple On is.
    - Thus the other tracks lose the same frames, and stay in sync.
    - The track of each clip must be unlocked. Clips on locked tracks stay where they are.
    - The window warns when other unlocked tracks lose frames.
  - Cross Fade Audio (`cross_fade`) is off by default.
    - With it, a Cross Fade 0 dB goes on the sound where two parts of the same clip meet after the cut.
    - The cuts on other tracks get no cross fade.
    - The cross fade is 2 frames long, centered on the cut. A side with no frames past the cut gets less.
    - Without it, the cuts get the 3 ms de-click fades that every cut gets.
  - **Resolve Studio 21.1.1** (its Edit page):
    - Cross fade audio made a Cross Fade 0 dB, 2 frames long and centered.
    - It did so with a pre head and a post tail of 6 frames, and of 24 frames.
    - Its window keeps the last values used. Thus its factory defaults are not known.
    - On the install that was checked, it opened at −25 dB, no pre head or post tail, and a minimum of 24 frames.
    - Cross fade audio was off.
    - With a clip on V2 over a full V1, V1 and A1 lost the same frames. A later V2 clip moved left.
    - With V1 and A1 locked, they stayed, and V2 and A2 closed up.
    - With the clip on V1, a V2 clip over the silence lost the same frames.
    - Only the clip's own sound got the cross fade.
- **D30. Finding shots and black.**
  - Detect Scene Cuts (p. 690) gives no algorithm.
  - A frame starts a shot when the FFmpeg scdet score on its luma is 10 or more.
  - The score is from 0 to 100. It is the mean difference from the frame before, or its change if that is smaller.
  - Thus steady motion is not a cut.
  - A frame is black when 98% of its pixels are within 10% of black.
  - Black of 0.5 s or more is reported.
- **D31. Boring Detector defaults** (p. 690).
  - The manual gives none.
  - Clips longer than 30 s are boring. Clips shorter than 10 frames are jump cuts.
  - It looks at video tracks.
  - Its settings are view options, kept between runs. They are not part of the project.
- **D32. Dynamic Trim** (pp. 730–732).
  - L or J runs the trim at shuttle speed. Repeat the key for 2× up to 64×.
  - The run continues until the media or a neighbor runs out.
  - During the run, it plays with sound, at that speed, what the trim changes under the playhead:
    - A roll, or the In side, plays the incoming clip on from the cut.
    - The Out side plays the outgoing clip.
    - A slide plays the clip beside the edge that the playhead is on.
    - A slip plays the clip alone, as the timeline plays it. It plays backward when L moves the clip right.
  - Loop does not wrap the run.
  - When the mode starts and when a run starts, the playhead goes to the edit. Or it goes to the edge of the first selected clip nearer the playhead.
  - During the run, the playhead moves with the edit, or with the edge of a sliding clip.
  - On the In side (Ripple On) and in a slip, the playhead stays.
  - During the run, the viewer shows the exact frames on both sides of the edit.
  - For a slip or a slide, it shows four frames: the first and last frames of the clip, over the frames before and after it.
  - During the run, the timeline shows the clips as the trim leaves them.
  - While no run occurs, the Trim Editor shows for an edit, and the viewer shows for clips.
  - K, Space, W, Up/Down and the end of the media stop a run.
  - They apply the run as one request, and one undo step (deviation X11).
  - Where the media or a neighbor ran out, the cut turns red, and the viewer tells why (deviation X29).
  - K held with a tap of J or L trims one frame.
  - K held with J or L held trims at half speed, until one key is released (D54).
  - `,`/`.` trim as usual (D20).
  - While no run occurs, Space plays from 2 s before the playhead to 2 s after it. Then it puts the playhead back.
- **D33. Rotation metadata.**
  - The manual does not say.
  - A display-matrix rotation is applied everywhere, as in players. Phones store portrait video sideways with one.
  - Thus a portrait clip that sets the format makes a portrait project.
  - Mirrored matrices count only as rotations.
- **D34. Interlaced sources** (p. 741).
  - A stream is interlaced if its field order says so, or if its first frame has the interlaced flag.
  - Frames with the flag are deinterlaced everywhere by the method of `bwdif`, one frame for each frame (deviation X13).
  - The deinterlacer keeps the first field.
  - Output is never interlaced, and never at the field rate.
- **D35. HDR and color** (pp. 230–239).
  - A clip is HDR when its transfer is PQ or HLG.
  - SDR output tone-maps it to BT.709.
  - The tone-map keeps up to 80% of the 203-nit reference white, and rolls highlights off up to 1000 nits.
  - HDR output (D44) is BT.2100 in 10 bits, with SDR white at 203 nits (BT.2408).
  - This is the same as Resolve's "203 nits" option (p. 238).
  - Untagged media are BT.709 when they are 1280 wide or have more than 576 lines. Else they are BT.601, as players guess.
  - The Input Color Space of a media (§3) stands in for its tags, and drops its HDR metadata.
- **D36. Which track an edit point is on** (pp. 696, 720, 729–732).
  - The Smart Indicator, V, Smart Insert, the alignment of Place On Top, and Up/Down use the edits of Track 1, the main story.
  - An edit selected by a click stays on its track for `,`/`.`, U, the Trim Editor and Dynamic Trim.
- **D37. The format: chosen at the start, or set by the first video without asking** (§3; Resolve asks, p. 654).
  - New Project takes a picture size from 256 to 32768 px on each side, odd sizes too, and any positive frame rate.
  - An export in H.264 or H.265 rounds an odd side up (§10).
  - A side under 256 sets both sides to 256, whichever side it is. 16 x 720, 99999 x 16 and 16 x 99999 give 256 x 256.
  - Else a side over 32768 sets both sides to 32768. 99999 x 720 gives 32768 x 32768.
  - This rule is measured in the Project Settings dialog of Resolve Studio 21.1.2. `project.set` and `project.create` do the same.
  - A `project.set` of one side checks the pair as it stands then, as a script in Resolve Studio 21.1.2 does.
  - Without them, the first media with video sets both, with no prompt. A prompt would stop scripts and agents.
  - The size can change at any time.
  - The rate can change only while the timeline is empty, because positions are whole frames.
- **D38. Stills** (§3; pp. 114, 1194).
  - The manual gives two standard still durations: 5 s (p. 114) and 10 s (p. 1194).
  - vidcut takes 5 s, as a project setting (deviation X16).
  - A still edited without a range has that duration.
  - A range or a trim makes it up to 17 h 40 min (p. 1194).
  - Every source frame of a still shows its picture. Thus only that length limits its clips, the frames of a dissolve too.
  - A head trim or a dissolve can go before the source start of the still.
  - A clip keeps its source position, which can be less than 0.
  - A GIF or WebP of one frame is a still too. A GIF or WebP of more frames is a clip.
  - Resolve Studio 21.1.1 imports a WebP of one frame as a still.
  - It imports a GIF of one frame as a still only when its frame has no delay (deviation X39).
  - FFmpeg 8 cannot decode an animated WebP. With FFmpeg 8, vidcut refuses such a file on import. The message says that it needs FFmpeg 9.0 ([gotchas](gotchas.md#ffmpeg-reading-and-decoding)).
- **D39. Image sequences** (§3; p. 366).
  - `media.add` with `sequence` brings numbered files (`frame_000001.png`, …) in as one clip.
  - The clip is the unbroken run around each file given.
  - It is at the project's rate, unless `fps` gives another rate.
  - `media.add_folder` with `sequence` does this for each run of two or more files in its folders.
  - The pool names the clip as Resolve does: `frame_[000001-000120].png`.
  - Without `sequence`, a numbered image is a still (deviation X17).
- **D40. Timeline interchange with Resolve** (deviation X9; pp. 527–530, 4160).
  - The manual describes Resolve's OTIO import and export, not the files.
  - vidcut's OTIO uses the shapes that Resolve writes and needs: source ranges in media timecode, plain paths, link groups, marker notes.
  - Thus a cut goes to Resolve and back with its media linked, and with its audio together with its video.
  - **A file with tracks and no clips.** Resolve Studio 21.1.2 imports it as an empty timeline, with V1, A1 and the start timecode of the file (measured).
    - `project create --from` and `timeline.import` with `replace` make an empty timeline. It takes the rate and the start timecode of the file.
    - `timeline.import` with `append`, `compound` or `media` refuses such a file. Resolve was not measured for these modes.
    - A file whose clips cannot be read also refuses, and the error names them.
  - Mapping: [deviation X9](deviations.md#x9-opentimelineio-interchange), [model](design/model.md#opentimelineio).
  - **A multicam clip in a file of Resolve** (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2):
    - It is a `Stack` with the `Sequence Type` "Multicam Clip".
    - The `Stack` has one video track and one audio track for each angle.
    - The shift of an angle is its leading `Gap` and the source start of its clip. No field holds a shift.
    - Only the name of the `Stack` tells the video angle. The file does not hold the audio angle.
    - With `importSourceClips` off, Resolve imports each `Stack` as one plain clip, with the name and the source range of the `Stack`.
    - The clip has no pool item, and the multicam is lost. Its export is a `Clip` with a `MissingReference`.
    - A cut multicam clip has one `Stack` for each part. It gives one clip for each part, on the video track and on the audio track.
    - The audio mode All has one audio `Stack` on each audio track. It gives one clip on each audio track.
    - A `Sequence Fps` that is not the rate of the timeline does not change the result. The clip has the frame count of the file.
    - With `importSourceClips` on, the import returned nothing for one file. The reason is not known.
  - **A clip with no media in `timeline.import`.** This is a multicam `Stack`, or a `Clip` with a `MissingReference` that has no `Media Url`:
    - Each one makes one clip on its track, with its name and its source range.
    - The pool gets no media. The import does not read the files of the angles.
    - The clip plays as a gap, with no sound.
    - The timeline shows it red, and the viewer shows Media Offline for a clip on a video track.
    - An import warning names the clip.
    - An OTIO export writes a `Clip` with a `MissingReference` on its track.
    - Clips with the same place and the same link group in the file stay in one link group.
    - A sound transition at such a clip stays in the export. vidcut plays no fade on the clip with no media, and no fade on the sound of a compound clip.
  - **The linked parts of a clip with no media or of a compound clip.** Each part is a clip on its track. The parts act together, as linked clips do in Resolve:
    - Clips with no media are parts of one clip when they have the same link group, start and length.
    - A picture and a sound of a compound clip are one clip when they have the same sequence, start, length, source start and speed.
    - A request that names one part acts on each part: `edit.delete`, `edit.cut`, `edit.copy`, `edit.move`, `edit.replace`, `edit.trim`, `edit.roll`, `edit.slip`, `edit.slide`, `edit.split`, `edit.join`, `edit.change_duration` and `edit.change_speed`.
    - The part on V1 leads. The rules of V1 apply to it, and the other parts take its new place.
    - An insert into such a clip on V1 splits each part at that frame.
    - A move to another video track keeps the sound on its audio track, at the new place. When that track has a clip at the new place, the sound goes on the next free audio track.
    - A move of a sound part to another audio track moves its picture to the new place on the picture's own track.
    - A part on a locked track refuses the edit.
    - An edit that writes on one track does not write on the other parts: `edit.overwrite`, `edit.ripple_overwrite`, `edit.place_on_top`, `edit.source_overwrite` and `edit.paste`.
    - After such an edit the parts can differ in place or length. Then they are separate clips, and an export gives them no link group.
    - A timeline drag shows only the part under the pointer. The other parts move when the drag ends.
    - Resolve was not measured for a locked part, or for a move to another track.
- **D41. Proxies** (deviation X18; the proxy chapters, pp. 207–218, are outside the Cut page).
  - Videos and image sequences get proxies on demand (Generate Proxy).
  - A running GUI also makes proxies for media that are hard to play (preferences `proxies.*`):
    - image sequences
    - pictures larger than 3840×2160
    - files over 150 Mb/s
  - The GUI checks for these when media are added, when `use_proxies` turns on, and when the file of a proxy is gone.
  - A proxy whose file is gone counts as no proxy:
    - The pool marks it LOST PROXY.
    - The original plays.
    - These rules make it again as the project names it: its size, and its codec where this computer makes that codec.
  - The longest side of a proxy is 960 px, unless set.
  - `auto` selects a codec that the GPU plays ([export](design/export.md#proxies)).
  - A project names a proxy by its file. Each computer keeps the file in its own proxy folder.
  - The proxy folder is `proxies` in the folder of playback files (deviation X26).
  - That folder is the project's folder, unless `playback_files.folder` sets another. A relative folder is in the project's folder. An absolute folder is shared by every project.
  - With `use_proxies` on, the preview plays fresh proxies, frame for frame as the originals. Thus heavy media play in real time.
  - Sound, exports and analyses use the originals.
- **D42. Audio scrubbing** (§4.5; p. 876).
  - The manual says only that a drag of the playhead plays the sound.
  - A frame step, and every move of a paused playhead, plays that frame of the mix.
  - The frame is faded over 3 ms, against clicks.
  - It plays forward, also when stepping back: a reversed frame is not heard as backward.
  - A newer frame replaces any frame that waits. Thus a held key does not pile up frames.
  - Backward play plays the sound reversed.
- **D43. Enlarging a track** (pp. 647, 678).
  - The enlarged track is three times its height, and at least 180 px.
  - In a short timeline, the other tracks shrink first, to a line each.
  - It is a view, like the zoom. It is not in the project or in the undo history.
- **D44. An export's dynamic range follows the timeline, and its bit depth follows its footage** (§10; deviation X14).
  - `video.hdr` `auto` (the default) follows the timeline color space (D50).
  - The export is HDR when both of these are true:
    - The timeline color space is HDR.
    - The codec carries HDR (HEVC, AV1, VP9) with a 10-bit encoder on this computer.
  - An HDR export uses the transfer of the timeline: PQ or HLG.
  - Otherwise the export is SDR, and HDR is tone-mapped (D35).
  - An unset depth is 10 for HDR.
  - It is also 10 for SDR when both of these are true:
    - Most of the picture has 10 bits or more.
    - The 10 bits of the codec play widely (not H.264).
  - The plan tells why.
  - When an HDR timeline is tone-mapped and the user did not ask for it, the Export dialog tells so, with an Export HDR button.
  - An HDR export of an SDR timeline has only SDR pictures. Thus its HDR metadata is the metadata of SDR pictures (D46).
- **D45. Passes** (§10; Resolve's multi-pass option is on the Deliver page).
  - Two passes bring an export closer to a target bitrate, and spread its bits better.
  - At constant quality, they gain nothing worth the time.
  - Thus an unset `video.passes` is two with a bitrate, where the encoder makes two. Otherwise it is one.
  - A request for two passes from an encoder that makes one is an error.
- **D46. HDR metadata follows the footage** (§10; deviation X19).
  - With no grade, an export carries what its sources give (`video.hdr_metadata` `auto`).
  - PQ output gets the widest mastering display of the pictures shown.
  - It also gets their highest MaxCLL and MaxFALL, measured where a source gives none.
  - SDR pictures count as BT.709 at 203 cd/m². HDR pictures without a display count as BT.2020 at 1000 cd/m².
  - HLG and SDR output get none.
  - Each frame keeps its HDR10+ and Dolby Vision where its transfer stays:
    - HDR10+ (PQ only) when any picture has it. For the other pictures, it is made from measured light.
    - Dolby Vision when every picture has it, in one profile.
- **D47. Drop-frame timecode** (§4.3).
  - At 29.97 and 59.94 fps, record timecode can count drop-frame, as Resolve's project settings offer.
  - Each minute, drop-frame skips the frame numbers 00 and 01 (00–03 at 59.94), except each tenth minute.
  - New Project and Project Settings offer it.
  - It turns on when the media's timecode counts drop-frame, in two cases:
    - The first video sets the format (D37).
    - An OTIO import goes into an empty timeline.
  - It shows as `;` before the frames.
  - When you type a timecode, `;` means drop-frame. So does `:` in a drop-frame project.
  - A skipped label is refused. Digits that fill one land on the first frame of the minute.
- **D48. Clips at another speed** (§1, §8.6; deviations X9, X95, X96, X97).
  - A clip's speed comes from Change Clip Speed, the Speed section of the Inspector, speed keyframes, or an OTIO file.
  - The speed applies wherever its pictures and sound go.
  - Change Clip Speed takes 0.01% to 100000%, forward or backward (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
  - It also takes 0, which freezes the frame under the playhead.
  - It gives the whole clip one speed. The speed keyframes of the clip go.
  - Without ripple, the clip keeps its length. With ripple, it keeps its source, and later clips follow.
  - Each frame shows the source frame in which its stretch of the source starts.
  - Backward, it shows the bottom of the stretch, as Resolve shows it.
  - This is Retime Process at Nearest. The other processes make the frames between the source frames (deviation X93).
  - Sound keeps its pitch, and plays reversed when backward. A freeze is silent.
  - Edits count frames through the speed.
  - **A speed under 0.01%, or 0.** These facts come from Resolve Studio 21.1.2, from `SetSpeed` in a script, on a clip with no speed keyframes. `speed.set` does the same:
    - The speed gives a freeze frame. 0.009% and 0 give the same result.
    - The clip keeps its length, and the next clip does not move. Ripple on or off gives the same result.
    - With the playhead inside the clip, the clip splits at the playhead.
      - The left part keeps its speed.
      - The right part is a freeze frame of the source frame at the playhead, up to the clip's old end.
      - The linked audio splits at the same frame and takes the same speeds.
    - With the playhead on the clip's first frame, the whole clip is a freeze frame of its first source frame.
    - With the playhead on the clip's last frame, the right part is a freeze frame of 1 frame.
    - With the playhead after the clip, the whole clip is a freeze frame of its last source frame.
    - A clip with speed keyframes and a playhead before the clip are not measured. In vidcut, `speed.set` refuses both.
    - A value typed in the Inspector is not measured.
  - **Speed keyframes.** These facts come from Resolve Studio 21.1.2, from its renders and its OTIO files:
    - A speed keyframe cuts a clip into two sections. Each section has a speed of its own. The sound of the clip gets the same sections.
    - A new speed keyframe gives both sections the speed of the section that it cuts.
    - Each timeline frame shows `floor` of its source position plus 0.002 frames. The source position is on the line of its section.
      - Thus a position that is 0.002 frames or less below a whole number shows that number.
      - The amount is in frames, at 25 fps and at 30 fps.
    - An eased speed keyframe changes the speed on a curve. Let k be its frame and s its source position.
      - v0 and v1 are the speeds before and after it. a and c are the frames of its handles, a before k and c after k.
      - From k + a to k + c, the curve is a cubic Bezier curve. Its points are (k + a, s + a × v0), (k, s), (k, s) and (k + c, s + c × v1).
      - Outside that range, the curve stays on the lines of the sections.
      - A handle on one side alone gives the straight line again.
    - Resolve shortens a handle that reaches past the speed keyframe next to it. An OTIO import does this, and the export has the short handles.
      - The clip's first frame and its end count as speed keyframes.
      - Where the handles of two eased speed keyframes overlap, the later speed keyframe's handle keeps its length, up to the section. The earlier handle gets the rest of the section.
      - The value of a handle changes with its length.
    - A section that plays backward uses the same `floor`. Thus where the clip turns, the speed keyframe's own source frame shows once.
    - An eased speed keyframe where the clip turns gives a smooth turn, on the same curve. The picture slows down, stops and goes back.
    - A change of a section's speed moves the later speed keyframes and the clip's end. Their sections keep their speeds.
    - The freeze button holds the frame under the playhead for 2 seconds of the timeline. It adds a section at 0% and a speed keyframe after it.
    - Delete Keyframe removes the speed keyframe at the playhead, or else the nearest one before the playhead.
    - The section that is left goes in a straight line between the speed keyframes on its two sides. The clip keeps its length.
    - Trims and splits keep all the speed keyframes, also those outside the clip. They stay on the retimed source.
    - A keyframe of the clip stays on its frame of the clip when a section's speed changes.
    - The OTIO export writes the speed keyframes as the `Key Frames` of a `TimeEffect`, in seconds from the media's first frame.
      - An eased key is `[output, source, true, a, a × v0, c, c × v1]`, and `Retime Mode` is `SPEED`. The import uses only a and c.
  - **Timeline:** a clip with speed keyframes has a strip at its top. These facts come from Resolve Studio 21.1.2:
    - The strip shows the speed of each section, and a line at each speed keyframe.
    - A click on a speed opens a list: 20%, 30%, 40%, 50%, 60%, 70%, 100% and Set.
    - Set opens the Set Speed Change window, with a Speed field.
    - The clip's right-click menu adds a speed keyframe ([X94](deviations.md#x94-speed-keyframes-and-the-mark-menus-keyframe-items)).
    - With the Keyframes tab and the keyframe tray closed, the keyframe items of the Mark menu act on speed keyframes.
  - **Inspector:** these facts come from Resolve Studio 21.1.2:
    - The header of the Speed Change group has a keyframe diamond between < and > buttons. The diamond is red with the playhead on a speed keyframe.
    - Change Speed and Frames per Second show and change the speed of the section under the playhead.
    - The Direction buttons act on that section. In one check, Reverse played it backward to the media's first frame ([X96](deviations.md#x96-reverse-keeps-the-speeds-of-the-other-sections)).
    - The Duration field shows the section's length in one check and the clip's length in two others. The reason is not measured.
    - The Ease list under Direction has Linear, Ease In, Ease Out and Ease In and Out.
      - In one check, each of the three eases set only the speed keyframe at the start of the section ([X97](deviations.md#x97-ease-in-and-ease-out-ease-their-own-side-of-a-section)).
      - Its handles are 1/6 s before and after the speed keyframe at 30 fps.
  - **In vidcut:**
    - The Inspector's Speed section shows the section under the playhead. With the playhead outside the clip, it shows the nearest section.
      - Speed and Frames per Second send `speed.set`. They are off when the section is frozen or plays backward.
      - Freeze sends `speed.freeze`. Forward and Reverse send `speed.reverse`. They are off when the section is frozen.
      - Reverse turns the section at the speed keyframe where it starts, and the first section at the one where it ends.
      - The sections after it go on from where it stops, at their own speeds. Thus the picture does not jump.
      - Ease sends `speed.ease` ([X97](deviations.md#x97-ease-in-and-ease-out-ease-their-own-side-of-a-section)). Ease In eases the speed keyframe at the section's start, and Ease Out the one at its end.
        - The list shows the eases of the section's two speed keyframes. It offers only the eases that they can take.
      - An ease reaches 1/6 s before and after its speed keyframe, at each frame rate. Resolve's length at other rates than 30 fps is not measured.
      - An ease stops at the speed keyframes next to it and at the clip's edges, as in Resolve. Where two eases overlap, the later one keeps its length.
      - vidcut applies this rule again after each edit that moves a speed keyframe.
      - Duration is off on a clip with speed keyframes.
      - Reset gives the whole clip 100%. Its speed keyframes go.
      - Speed takes a typed value under 0.01%. On clips with no speed keyframes, it sends `speed.set` at the playhead.
      - On a clip with speed keyframes, that value becomes 0.01%. A drag of Speed stops at 0.01%.
    - The diamond adds or removes a speed keyframe at the playhead. The arrows move the playhead to the previous or next speed keyframe.
    - The diamond is orange on a speed keyframe ([X107](deviations.md#x107-the-diamond-of-speed-change-is-orange)).
    - The list opens only on a section that plays forward. `speed.set` changes only such a section.
    - Add Speed Keyframe in the right-click menu adds a speed keyframe at the playhead.
    - The Speed row of the Keyframes tab and the keyframe tray shows the speed keyframes ([X94](deviations.md#x94-speed-keyframes-and-the-mark-menus-keyframe-items)).
      - A speed keyframe does not move or copy. Its value is the speed of the section after it.
      - Its ease menu has Linear and Ease In and Out ([X97](deviations.md#x97-ease-in-and-ease-out-ease-their-own-side-of-a-section)).
      - Ctrl+X leaves the selected speed keyframes in place, and they stay selected.
      - A change of its value in the tab sends `speed.set`. The field takes 0.01% to 100000%.
      - The field is off when the section is frozen or plays backward.
    - With the tab and the tray closed, Mark > Add Keyframe (Ctrl+[) adds a speed keyframe at the playhead to each selected clip under it.
    - Then Mark > Delete Keyframe (Alt+]) removes the speed keyframe at the playhead, or else the nearest one before it.
    - Then Mark > Delete All Keyframes asks first. Then it removes all the speed keyframes of the selected clips.
  - **Requests:** `keyframes.add` and `keyframes.remove` with the `param` `speed` add and remove speed keyframes.
    - `speed.set`, `speed.freeze`, `speed.reverse` and `speed.ease` change the sections. All six act at a time, the playhead by default.
    - `keyframes.add` skips a clip that cannot take a speed keyframe at `at`. It fails only when no clip can.
    - `speed.reverse` turns a section at its speed keyframe ([X96](deviations.md#x96-reverse-keeps-the-speeds-of-the-other-sections)).
    - `speed.set` takes 0.01% to 100000% forward, in a section that plays forward.
    - On a clip with no speed keyframes, `speed.set` also takes the speeds of `edit.change_speed`, and a speed under 0.01%.
    - `speed.ease` eases the speed keyframes of a section. `keyframes.set` gives one speed keyframe `linear` or `ease_in_out`.
    - `keyframes.list` gives `ease` `ease_in_out` for an eased speed keyframe.
    - `keyframes.remove` without `frames` and `at` removes all the speed keyframes of the clips.
    - `keyframes.list` gives each speed keyframe with the speed of the section after it.
    - With ripple, the clip's length follows a change. Without ripple, the clip keeps its length.
  - **Model:** a clip's speed is a list of sections on its retimed source (`Speed`, `Section`).
    - The retimed source has one frame for each timeline frame. A trim or a split moves only the clip's place on it (`skip`).
    - The keyframes of a clip with a speed are on its retimed source. Thus they stay on their frames of the clip.
    - The sound of a clip plays through all its sections in one stretch, with its pitch kept. Thus a speed keyframe makes no click and no dip.
    - Over an ease, the sound follows the curve one frame at a time.
    - Where the clip turns, the sound of each side plays in one stretch, also backward.
- **D49. What Join Match Edit Clips joins** (pp. 646, 950).
  - The manual says only that it rejoins a split clip.
  - Two clips join when the second plays on from where the first stops. Thus nothing seen or heard changes.
  - That is, both clips are adjacent on one track, with the same media, speed and settings.
  - No frames are missing, and no fade or transition is between them.
  - With two or more clips selected, the selected clips that meet join.
  - Otherwise the clips at the playhead join. They are selected as for Split (D2).
  - The first part keeps its id.
- **D50. Dissolves** (§8.11; deviations X9, X24, X27).
  - The manual says only that one picture fades as the next comes in.
  - These rules match Resolve's renders without color management. Deviation X14 tells how they differ with it.
  - Frame i of an n-frame dissolve shows the incoming picture at (i + ½)/n, and the outgoing picture at the rest.
  - The mix is in the code values of the timeline color space, not in linear light.
  - The timeline color space is a setting of the project: Auto, SDR (BT.709), HDR PQ or HDR HLG.
  - Auto is HDR when more than half of the timeline's pictures are HDR, by duration. It is HLG when every HDR picture is HLG, else PQ.
  - Each picture is converted into the timeline color space first, with the conversions of D35.
  - Dissolves, wipes, placed pictures, titles and subtitles mix there.
  - Then each export, the viewer and each render of a timeline frame convert the mixed frame to their own colors.
  - Pictures of a single media file do not use the timeline color space: `frame.render` of a media, the tiles and the source tape. They show the file directly.
  - An SDR picture in an HDR timeline goes through two conversions in an SDR export. The tone map lowers its white to about 96% of the code range.
  - At a gap, the mix is with what is under the track.
  - A Cross Fade gives the incoming sound √x (+3 dB), x (0 dB) or x^1.5 (−3 dB) at x of the way.
  - The outgoing sound gets the same of 1 − x.
  - The Cross Fade has no de-click at its edges.
  - The manual names no track for the edit nearest the playhead. Thus it is the edit of Track 1 (D36).
  - Ctrl+T on selected clips puts a dissolve on both edges of each clip, on its track.
  - An edit between two selected clips gets one dissolve.
  - A new dissolve is one second long.
  - It is centered. For an odd length, its middle frame is before the edit.
  - It is shortened to the media that each side has (deviation X27).
  - It adds a Cross Fade of +3 dB where the sounds meet.
  - If it is longer than both sides allow, it fills both sides, as in Resolve.
  - Example: 999 frames on a cut between two 300-frame clips with long handles make 300 + 300.
  - At the edge of a clip with no clip beside it, it lies inside the clip. This applies beside an empty track and at the timeline start.
  - There, it ends on the last frame of the clip, or starts on its first frame.
  - This is true when U selects the side of the clip, and when U selects both sides.
  - The empty side alone takes no dissolve.
  - A fade past the last clip, as an OTIO file can have, makes the program longer.
- **D51. Loop** (§4.2; p. 640).
  - The manual says only that Loop Playback loops.
  - With Loop on, play that reaches the end continues from the start.
  - With both In and Out set in the viewer, play that reaches the Out continues from the In.
  - Play from past the Out runs to the end, then continues from the In.
  - Backward play loops in the opposite direction.
  - The sound continues without a break.
  - Loop is off at the start, and it is not in the project.
- **D52. When the Trim Editor shows** (§8.6; pp. 729–731).
  - The manual opens it with a double-click.
  - Resolve Studio 21.1.1 shows it whenever an edit is selected and the player is stopped.
  - A click on the edit, V or a double-click selects an edit.
  - A click on a dissolve selects its edit and the dissolve.
  - In both cases, an edit with a dissolve shows the dissolve (§8.6).
  - Space plays the timeline as usual, through the edit, with the editor hidden.
  - When play stops, the editor shows again, with the edit still selected.
  - A drag on its handles trims at the scale of the lower timeline.
  - A drag on the ends of a dissolve trims at the scale of the filmstrips.
- **D53. Import Media Folder** (§3; pp. 654, 658).
  - The manual says that each imported folder is a bin.
  - Its bin sync expects bins named as the folders and as the folders in them.
  - Resolve Studio 21.1.1 makes a bin for each folder in the folder, with the name of that folder.
  - It does this also for an empty folder, and for a folder that holds only documents. vidcut does the same.
  - Files and folders with a name that starts with a dot stay out.
  - Files and folders that Windows marks hidden come in, as in Resolve's Media Storage and its import.
  - Resolve's order of the clips in the new bin changes from one import of the same folder to the next.
  - The video whose frame rate Resolve offers a project with an empty pool also changes: it is the first in that order.
  - vidcut's media come in by name, ignoring case, with the files of a folder before the folders in it.
  - Thus the first video in that order sets the format (D37).
  - A file is media by its extension.
  - The other files, and media that do not open, are skipped, as in Resolve.
  - But the result and the notice of the GUI name them, and a folder without media is an error (deviation X32).
  - A bin that exists takes the media of the folder.
  - Media already in the pool keep their ids, and move into the bin of their folder (deviation X31).
  - Thus a second import of a folder brings in only its new files.
  - In Resolve, an import into the bin that holds a file adds nothing.
  - Numbered images follow D39: `sequence`, or a question in the GUI.
  - This is checked on the Cut page of Resolve Studio 21.1.1, and through its scripting API.
  - The scripting API has no call that makes the bins.
- **D54. Slow play with K** (§4.2; pp. 731, 871).
  - The manual gives no speed, and no duration for a tap.
  - Resolve Studio 21.1.1 plays at half speed with the sound slowed, in playback and in Dynamic Trim (checked by hand).
  - Not checked: whether it keeps the pitch, whether the playhead steps as J or L goes down, and how a held trim undoes.
  - With K held, a J or L released within 0.25 s is a tap.
  - On release, a tap steps a frame. In Dynamic Trim, it trims one frame.
  - Held longer, J or L plays in that direction at half speed until J, L or K is released.
  - In Dynamic Trim, it runs the trim at half speed.
  - The run then applies as K applies it, as one undo step (D32).
  - The sound plays slowed, with its pitch kept, as at any speed other than 1× (deviation X30).
  - The repeats of a held key are not presses. Thus L held alone plays at 1×.
- **D55. Undo steps** (§8.10; pp. 651–652; deviations X4, X33–X38).
  - The manual does not say what a step is, or how a step gets its name.
  - Every request that changes the project is one step, an `apply` batch too.
  - Undo and redo are not steps.
  - Views are not steps: zoom, enlarged tracks, the selection.
  - Original is the oldest state that the history keeps (X4).
  - Resolve Studio 21.1.1 names a step by its kind ("Split Clips", "Remove clips", "Add Transition to Edit Points").
  - vidcut's names tell more (X33).
  - The steps that a new change replaces stay as a version (X36).
  - A step can have a name (X37).
- **D56. Subtitle tracks and cues** (§8.12).
  - The manual does not say which track shows after a new track is added, or how long a new cue lasts.
  - Subtitle tracks are `S1`, `S2`, … from the video up.
  - In Resolve Studio 21.1.2, a script added 380 subtitle tracks to one timeline, and none was refused.
  - `track.add` takes a `count` of at least 1 subtitle track, with no top limit.
  - A new track is the shown track.
  - A new cue lasts 3 s from the playhead, measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2. It does not stop at the next cue.
  - Add New and Add Subtitle overwrite what the new cue covers, measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2:
    - A cue that the new cue fully covers is removed.
    - A cue that the new cue partly covers is trimmed to the new edge.
    - A cue that covers all of the 3 s and more becomes two cues with the same text, before and after the new cue.
  - These two cues keep their words and keyframes as a split does (D70, §8.12).
  - The new cue has the text "Subtitle", selected in the text box, measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2.
  - `subtitles.add` alone does not overwrite. A script that wants the overwrite sends its own `apply`.
  - Cues of one track can overlap, and show together.
  - On the timeline, cues that overlap share the height of their track, each in its own lane.
  - Split leaves its text in both parts.
  - Join needs a shared edit point (§8.12). It keeps the first cue, to the end of the last cue, with the text of the first cue only.
  - The viewer draws the cues of the shown track as a burn-in draws them (D61), in the style of the track, its font too.
  - While the Subtitle Track section is open for the shown track, the viewer draws the style of its fields, where they are valid.
- **D57. Cues follow Track 1** (§6, §8.12; p. 1242).
  - The manual says only that subtitles linked to clips move with them.
  - A cue keeps the frames of Track 1 under it:
    - A ripple delete or trim takes out the part of a cue over the frames taken out.
    - A ripple delete inside a cue leaves two cues. Both keep the text. The test: a cue from 27 s to 30 s, cut from 28 s to 29 s, gives 27–28 and 28–29.
    - It drops a cue that has no frames left.
    - An insert inside a cue splits it. Both parts keep the text.
    - Overwrite and lift leave cues where they are.
    - A Ripple Overwrite takes the part of a cue over the frames that it takes out. A longer clip splits a cue across the end of the replaced part.
    - A timeline In–Out delete takes its range out of cues too.
  - A locked subtitle track does not move.
  - Resolve Studio 21.1.1 does the same in a ripple delete of a Track 1 clip (Edit page, through its scripting API).
  - There, the cues inside the clip go, and a cue across its end keeps its part after the clip.
- **D58. Subtitle file times** (§8.12; pp. 1230, 1247).
  - Each time goes to the nearest frame.
  - A file in timecode is timed in seconds from 00:00:00:00.
  - The start timecode of the timeline counts as frames at the project's rate.
  - Resolve Studio 21.1.1 does this (checked through its scripting API):
    - It reads `01:00:00,000` on a 23.976 timeline as 00:59:56:10.
    - Its renders write a cue at 01:00:00:00 as `01:00:03.599`, at 23.976 and at 29.97 non-drop.
    - Not checked: the files that File > Export Subtitle writes. The measurement used the scripting API only.
  - vidcut reads a file in real time, as Resolve writes and reads it. There are two exceptions:
    - `time_base` on `subtitles.import` says labels: timecode read as a clock, 01:00:00:00 as `01:00:00,000`.
    - The times of the file with fractions of a second fall on timecode frames, and clearly not on the frames of real time.
  - Round times (whole seconds, tenths) count for neither.
  - Thus a round `01:00:00,000` is 3600 s: 00:59:56:10 at 23.976, as Resolve reads it.
  - At whole rates, the two are the same.
  - A subtitle file beside an export starts at zero at the start of the output.
  - A subtitle file beside an OpenTimelineIO file is timed in the timecode of that timeline.
  - Insert Selected Subtitles to Timeline Using Timecode needs this (checked by hand):
    - From an SRT in timecode, it puts each cue where it was.
    - From an SRT timed from zero, it inserts nothing.
  - An SRT beside a timeline file has no `{\an8}` position tags. Resolve shows these tags as text.
- **D59. Sorting by timecode** (§3; pp. 666–667).
  - The manual names the order, not how it sorts.
  - Timecode sorts by the label of the start timecode of each file.
  - The start timecode is the recorded timecode of a video, or the time reference of a broadcast WAV.
  - It sorts by hours, minutes, seconds, and then frames as a part of a second.
  - Thus files at different rates sort as their labels read.
  - Example: a 29.97 fps non-drop-frame clip at 01:00:00:00 comes before a 25 fps clip at 01:00:02:00.
  - The 29.97 fps clip is 3.6 s past the hour in real time.
  - A file without a start timecode counts as 00:00:00:00.
  - This is the Start TC that Resolve Studio 21.1.1 gives such a clip (checked through its scripting API).
  - Thus it comes first in ascending order, and last in descending order.
  - Equal labels keep their import order, reversed in descending order, as in the other orders.
- **D60. The Duration field** (§4.4; p. 693).
  - The manual lists what the field takes. It does not say how it reads it, or what it counts from.
  - A number alone is frames (`48`). It is not digits that fill a timecode, as in the timecode field (§4.3).
  - A timecode, or a time with a unit (`00:00:02:00`, `2s`), is that duration.
  - `+` or `−` before either changes the duration shown by that amount (`+12`, `-5`).
  - The Out moves to the In plus the new duration.
  - With no In, the duration counts from the first frame of the clip or the timeline. That frame becomes the In.
  - With no Out, the duration shown runs to the end of the clip or the timeline.
  - A duration of less than one frame is refused.
  - A source Out past the end of the media is refused.
  - `marks.set` and `media.marks.set` take the same values as `duration`.
- **D61. Burned-in subtitles** (§8.12; p. 1247; deviations X45, X51).
  - The manual does not say how a track looks burned in before it has a style.
  - The usual look is Resolve's default, measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2:
    - White Open Sans Regular at Size 58: 58/1080 of the picture height.
    - Lines 1.362 times the text height apart. This scales with the picture height.
    - A white stroke of Size 1, centered on the edge of the letters.
    - A shadow with an offset of 0, which draws nothing.
    - One black box, 50% opaque, behind the whole block, with no box for each line.
    - The box is 5 pixels of the timeline taller than the lines, above and below, at any text size and timeline height.
    - The box is 0.3 times the Size number wider than the widest line, each side. These are pixels of the timeline.
    - The Corner Radius of the box is 0.010 of the picture height, about 10 pixels at 1080p. It does not grow with the text.
    - Centered, and wrapped at 90% of the width.
    - The middle of the block is at 80% of the height.
    - For a cue placed in the middle, the middle of the block is at 50%. For a cue at the top, it is at 20%.
  - The Corner Radius is not measured at other heights.
  - An export at another size scales the whole picture, thus these pixels too. A smaller export is measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2.
  - The font is Open Sans where it is installed, else the sans-serif font of the system.
  - For scripts that the font does not have, the system's fonts are used.
  - Cues that show together at one place are one block, in the order they start.
  - HDR white is 203 cd/m², the graphics white of ITU-R BT.2408.
  - A track's style (`track.set`, the Subtitle Track section of the Inspector) replaces that look where it is set:
    - `font` is a family name. If it is not installed, the text is drawn in the usual font.
    - `size` is the text height as a fraction of the picture height, from 1/1080 to 400/1080.
    - Size 1..400 is the range of a subtitle track, measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2. One Size is one pixel of em at 1080p.
    - `color` is `#rrggbb`, `#rgb` or a name such as yellow. A color with an opacity is refused.
    - `outline` is a color and a width in pixels of the timeline, from 0 to 16. 0 draws no outline.
    - A `size` is Stroke Size, measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2.
    - It is centered on the edge of the letters, over them. Thus half of it eats into each letter.
    - It takes the same pixels at any text size and any timeline height.
    - With `outside`, the box Outside Only, it goes behind the letters. Its outer edge stays, and the letters stay whole.
    - In both modes it reaches half its width past the letters, measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2.
    - An export at another size scales the whole picture, thus the stroke too. A smaller export is measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2.
    - `outline` left out is the white stroke. An `outline` with no color is white.
    - `background` is a color and an opacity from 0 to 1. An opacity of 0 draws no box. A background with no opacity is 50% opaque.
    - `margin` is the inset of the block from the left and right, as that fraction of the width.
    - For a cue placed at the top or the bottom, `margin` is also the inset from that edge, as that fraction of the height.
    - `margin` is from 0 up to, but not including, half. A cue in the middle stays centered.
    - `bold` uses the bold face of the font.
    - `case` draws every letter `upper` or `lower`.
    - `shadow` is the drop shadow of a title (D65), behind the text and its outline.
    - `off` in `background` or `shadow` draws none and keeps the values.
  - The Subtitle Track section has these rules for empty boxes and for switches:
    - A color has no empty state, measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2. An outline, background or shadow color box that the user empties keeps its color.
    - Size 0 draws no stroke, and the color stays, measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2.
    - Show Background and Show Shadow are the switches of Background and Drop Shadow, measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2.
    - The Drop Shadow switch off greys the values and keeps them, measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2. On again gives the same values.
    - Show Background does the same. Its greying is not measured.
    - The two shadow offset boxes are separate, measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2. One typed box is accepted, and the other keeps its value.
    - One typed Across or Down box is accepted. The other box takes the position of the track, else the place of the block.
  - A margin moves a block to the edge:
    - Without a margin, the middle of a bottom block is at 80% of the height.
    - With a margin, its bottom edge is at the margin.
    - Example: a margin of 5% puts the line of a one-line cue at 87.7–95% of the height.
    - The line is 7.3% of the height (5.37% × 1.362). This is lower than the usual look.
    - Likewise, with a margin, the top edge of a top block is at the margin. Without a margin, its middle is at 20%.
  - A project file with a style that `track.set` would refuse is refused.
  - The viewer draws the style as a burn-in does (D56).
  - An IMSC1 file has the same look (D64), with the bold face and the capitals.
  - An IMSC1 file has no shadow, because the IMSC1 text profile does not have one.
- **D62. Cues from speech** (§8.12; p. 1235).
  - The manual names the options, not how speech becomes cues.
  - A cue holds one sentence, or a part of one.
  - A cue ends at the end of a sentence, and where the speech pauses for a second or more.
  - A sentence too long for one cue is split into cues of about equal length, at a comma if one is near.
  - Words wrap onto the lines at the characters per line.
  - A cue starts at its first word and ends at its last word.
  - A cue stays at least 5/6 s, where the next cue allows it.
  - The frames between subtitles shorten a cue that would end closer to the next cue.
  - Parakeet Ultra, the default, times each word.
  - A model that gives no times has its text spread over the speech that the voice-activity model found.
  - Qwen3-ASR gives no times: transcribe.cpp does not run its aligner.
  - The text is spread by its characters, in pieces of 10 s at the most. Thus the times of its cues are estimates.
  - Where the GPU in use has less memory than the model wants, the answer warns, and the model runs anyway.
  - Parakeet Ultra wants 2 GB, Whisper large-v3 4 GB and Qwen3-ASR 8 GB.
  - A model file that the user names gets no warning.
  - With Find Speech By Sound Level (`find: sound`, the default), the model hears all sound louder than −40 dBFS (`sound_db`).
  - Quiet of half a second or more is left out.
  - This keeps singing, speech over music and a launch countdown. The voice-activity model drops these.
  - With Voice (`find: voice`), the model hears what the voice-activity model finds.
  - If that keeps less than a quarter of a range that is mostly louder than −40 dBFS, the answer says so.
  - Words kept for a file are used again only when they were found the same way, at the same level, as asked.
- **D63. Word times and cutting them** (§8.13; pp. 1004–1008).
  - The manual does not say how the time of a word becomes frames.
  - It does not say what a delete of words that touch cuts.
  - `analyze.words` answers with each word, its frames, and whether it joins the word before it without a space (`joined`).
  - The words come from one of these:
    - the words that the project takes from the transcripts of a media (X50), in source frames
    - the media of each clip, in timeline frames through the range and speed of the clip (a word whose middle is in the clip)
    - speech heard from the same mix as Create Subtitles from Audio
  - Only a model that times words can give them:
    - Parakeet Ultra times words.
    - Whisper large-v3 times phrases, and Qwen3-ASR gives no times. Thus they are refused.
    - The estimates used for their cues are never returned as word times.
  - `edit.ripple_delete_words` takes the named spans out of every unlocked track, and closes the gap, with ripple on or off. With `lift`, a gap is left.
  - Spans that touch are one cut.
  - Times go to the nearest frame. A word shorter than a frame still takes one frame.
  - A cut at the word edges of the model can take the start of the next word, where no pause separates them.
  - With `quiet`, as the Transcription window cuts, each edge moves to the quietest frame boundary nearby:
    - It uses the sound of the tracks heard there.
    - When two boundaries are equally quiet, it takes the nearer one.
    - The limits that follow apply as far as the transcripts of the clips show the words.
    - Inward, it moves no further than a third into any word fully inside the cut, whichever track's transcript has the word.
    - Thus each word that is cut loses its middle.
    - Outward, it moves only into the pause beside it, up to the next word, within about a tenth of a second.
    - Without a transcript, the edges stay.
  - Filler words are marked from built-in lists, one for each of the 25 languages of Parakeet Ultra.
  - The list is for the language of the transcript, or for the language that `language` names. With neither, no words are marked.
  - A list for a language in the preferences (`transcripts.fillers`) replaces its built-in list.
  - The list of a project (`project.set` `fillers`) replaces both.
  - A word matches whatever its capitals and the punctuation around it.
  - The Transcription window shows a pause of a second or more between words as (...).
  - X takes the sentence around a word: from after the last word that ends with . ! ? or … up to the next such word.
- **D64. Subtitle file layout** (§8.12; p. 1247).
  - The manual does not say how the look of a track is written to an IMSC1 file.
  - vidcut writes the look of D61, styled or not.
  - Sizes are percentages, because IMSC1 allows `c` units in a text document only in `ebutts:linePadding`.
  - `ttp:cellResolution` makes the picture 100 rows of cells.
  - The text height is a percentage of one cell: 5.5% of the picture is `550%`.
  - Lines are `136.2%` of it apart.
  - `proportionalSansSerif` follows the font family.
  - A name with whitespace in it is in single quotes (`'Open Sans',proportionalSansSerif`).
  - Reason: TTML1 §8.3.5 takes a name bare or quoted, and IMSC1 §7.4 asks for no whitespace between the parts of the value.
  - A font name with a quote or a comma in it is refused.
  - The outline is the part of the stroke outside the letters, as a percentage of the text height. The file does not have the part over the letters.
  - IMSC1 allows at most a tenth of the text height. A wider outline is written as a tenth.
  - That outline is named in the notes of the export, and in the warnings of `subtitles.export`.
  - The background is a span around the text of each cue. Thus each line has its own square box in the file, not one round box behind the block.
  - Each place has a region:
    - The region is as wide as the text wraps.
    - It is anchored where the block is: at the margin, or centered at 20%, 50% and 80%.
    - It is as tall as the picture allows, while the middle of a top region stays above 40% of the height.
    - The middle of a bottom region stays below 60%.
    - Thus vidcut reads each cue back at its place.
    - A region whose cues need more lines than fit in that height takes all the height that the picture allows.
  - IMSC1 lets at most four regions show at once (§6.8.3), one more than the three places.
  - IMSC1 forbids overlap only between regions shown at the same time (§6.8.2).
  - Thus, where cues at two places show at once, their two regions share the room between them.
  - Each region keeps the lines its cues need, where both fit.
  - A cue that still has more lines than its region holds is named in the notes of the export, and in the warnings of `subtitles.export`.
  - Reason: players can cut it off.
- **D65. Titles** (§8.14; pp. 763, 1178).
  - The manual does not give the look of a title, how its text sits, or how it plays over other tracks.
  - The usual look of a title:
    - The font of the burn-in (D61).
    - White, with no outline and no background (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
    - Text 96/1080 of the picture height for Text: the height of Resolve's 96-point Text title (D67).
    - Text 108/1080 for Scroll (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
    - For a lower third, 76/1080 on its lower line and 48/1080 on its upper line (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
    - A `size` in the style goes on both lines of a lower third.
    - A Text title and a lower third draw a whole number of timeline pixels for the size. The style keeps the exact number.
    - The size goes to the nearest whole number, and .5 goes up. Thus 99.5 and 100.4 draw as 100, and 100.5 draws as 101 (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
    - The letters, the line height, the text lines, the tracking and the small size of `<sup>` and `<sub>` all use that whole number.
    - A lower third has a tracking of 11 on its upper line, unless the style has a `tracking` (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
    - Resolve's OTIO export leaves that tracking out, and its reader then draws none. Thus a lower third from a file has only the tracking of the file.
    - A font gives three heights for a line: ascent, descent and gap (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2 on Windows with 14 fonts).
      - If the font sets `USE_TYPO_METRICS` in its `OS/2` table, these are the three typographic values of that table.
      - Otherwise, the ascent and the descent are `usWinAscent` and `usWinDescent`. The gap is what the `hhea` line height has more than these two, or 0.
    - The height of a line is the sum of the three. Each one goes down to a step of 1/64 of a pixel. The sum goes up to a whole pixel.
    - That step is measured with 8 fonts at 24 sizes where it gives a different height from the exact sum.
    - A line's baseline in a Text title or a lower third is the ascent below the line's top. The gap goes below the text.
    - The middle of a block rounds to a whole pixel. The top of the block goes up by the anchor's part of the height. Then it goes down to a whole pixel (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2 with four fonts at four sizes).
    - The left of a block starts at its middle, rounded to a whole pixel. It goes left by the anchor's part of the width. That width is the whole pixels of the widest line (same renders).
    - In Resolve Studio 21.1.2, some blocks are half a pixel from this rule, as "HHH" at anchor middle in Arial 96. The reason is not known.
    - Each line sits in the block by the exact difference from the widest line, with no rounding (same renders).
    - The letters of a Text title or a lower third have no hinting. Their advances are the font's own (same renders).
    - The font's kerning also goes across a space, a hyphen and a slash, inside one style. A change of style stops it, and tracking keeps it (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
    - A scroll and subtitles do not have this kerning across words, because it is not measured there.
    - Each letter goes over the letters before it. A letter has only its fill, with no edge around it (same renders).
    - Curves are straight lines within 1/6 of a pixel.
    - In Resolve Studio 21.1.2, Arial, Segoe UI and Verdana at sizes 48 to 384 come within 8 levels of 255. Times New Roman comes within 31 levels.
    - Zoom draws the letters as the bigger font. The height of a line is its height at zoom 1, in whole pixels, times the zoom (same renders).
    - A scroll keeps hinted letters (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2). Subtitles keep them too.
    - A title that Resolve reads from an OTIO file has one more edge on its letters. vidcut does not draw that edge ([X112](deviations.md#x112-a-title-from-an-otio-file-has-no-black-edge)).
    - The lines of a scroll are 4/3 of the sum of the three heights plus 10 timeline pixels apart. This fits Resolve Studio 21.1.2 within 5 pixels with four fonts.
    - Line spacing adds pixels of the timeline below a line, also negative (p. 1184). `line_spacing` in the style goes on each line. `spacing` in markup goes on its line.
    - The value takes the same pixels at any timeline height, and zoom scales it (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
    - Below the last line, a positive value makes the block taller and a negative value does nothing (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
    - A very negative value keeps all the lines (X111).
    - Tracking adds space after each letter, also after the last letter (p. 1184). `tracking` in the style goes on each letter. `tracking` in markup goes on its words.
    - One unit is 1/100 of the width of the font's letter X, and 1/200 below 0. Zoom scales it.
    - The width of X is in whole pixels, for the size in whole pixels of the timeline, with no zoom.
      - Below the size in the font's `LTSH` table for X, it is the width from the font's hinting for one-bit drawing.
      - Otherwise, it is the font's own width of X, rounded.
    - The space on a letter goes toward 0, to a step of 1/64 of a pixel.
    - A negative value does not go after the last letter of a line. Thus it does not make the block narrower there.
    - These rules are measurements in Resolve Studio 21.1.2 on Windows, with 12 fonts at sizes 24 to 384.
    - In these renders, some blocks with a negative value are up to 1 pixel from this rule, as "Ab" in Arial 96. The reason is not known.
    - A scroll has no line spacing, tracking or case, as in Resolve's own Scroll.
    - Font case is `mixed`, `upper`, `lower`, `small` or `title`: Mixed, All Caps, All Lowercase, Small Caps and Title Caps (p. 1184).
    - `small` draws small letters as capitals at 0.7 of the size in whole pixels at zoom 1, rounded to a whole pixel. The zoom scales it (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
    - Digits, punctuation and capitals stay at the full size.
    - A small letter is a letter of the Unicode category `Ll`. `ª`, `º`, `ʰ`, `ⅷ` and `ⓐ` are in other categories, so they stay as they are, at the full size (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
    - `title` makes the first letter of each word a capital, and it keeps the other letters. `mcDONALD` becomes `McDONALD`.
    - A word starts as in Unicode word boundaries (UAX 29), and also after `.` and `:` (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
      - A letter becomes a capital after a space, a hyphen, a comma, a bracket, or an apostrophe that is not between two letters.
      - A letter stays after a letter, a digit, `_`, a combining mark, or an apostrophe between two letters. `rock'n'roll` becomes `Rock'n'roll`.
    - `upper`, `lower`, `small` and `title` change each letter alone (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
      - A letter stays when its other form has more than one letter: `ß`, `ﬁ` and `ŉ` in capitals, and `İ` in small letters. With `small`, `ß`, `ﬁ` and `ŉ` draw at the small size, and `İ` at the full size.
      - `Σ` becomes `σ` also at the end of a word.
    - `<o>` draws a line over the words and `<s>` a line through them (p. 1184).
    - The height of these lines and `<u>` is `underlineThickness` in the font's `post` table.
    - The left end is the start of the words, rounded up to a whole pixel.
    - The right end is the exact start plus the width. The width goes down to whole pixels at zoom 1, and again after the zoom.
    - The middle of `<u>` is under the baseline by a third of the thickness plus 1/2, rounded up, plus 1/2 pixel.
    - That distance comes from the thickness at zoom 1, and the zoom scales it.
    - The middle of `<s>` is a third of the ascent over the baseline. The middle of `<o>` is the ascent over the baseline.
    - The top of the block cuts these lines. Thus `<o>` on the first line shows only its lower half.
    - These rules are measurements in Resolve Studio 21.1.2 on Windows, with 10 fonts at sizes 48 to 200, at three zooms and on two lines.
    - `<sup>` and `<sub>` draw the words at 2/3 of their size (p. 1184).
    - The small size is 2/3 of the size in whole pixels at zoom 1, rounded down to a whole pixel. The zoom scales it.
    - `<sup>` raises the words by half of the small font's ascent plus descent. `<sub>` lowers them by 1/6 of it. There is no rounding.
    - The baseline and the height of the line stay as for the full size.
    - These rules are measurements in Resolve Studio 21.1.2 on Windows, with 10 fonts, at three zooms and on two lines.
    - Lines wrap at the width of title safe, 90% of the picture width.
  - The block of a Text title is as wide as its widest line (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
    - `align` sets each line in the block: left, middle or right. By default, it is middle.
    - `anchor` names the point of the block that goes to `position`: a corner, the middle of a side, or the middle.
    - By default, `anchor` is the middle, and `position` is the middle of the picture.
    - A change of `align` or `anchor` keeps `position`.
    - A file's `justify` reads as left. Resolve draws it as left, because its lines do not wrap (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
  - The lines of a lower third align left, center or right.
  - The lower line of a lower third hangs from a point that is 10%, 50% or 90% across and 78% down, by its alignment.
  - The upper line stands on a point 80% down, at the same place across.
  - These are the points of Resolve's own lower thirds (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2). The two lines overlap by 2% of the picture height.
  - `position` moves the point of the lower line (X54). Lines that go together keep the 2% between their points.
  - Markup in the text styles words separately (X53): `<b>`, `<i>`, `<u>`, `<o>`, `<s>`, `<sup>`, `<sub>` and `<font>`.
  - `<font>` takes a `color`, a `face`, a `size`, a `spacing`, a `tracking` and a `case`.
  - A line takes the height of its largest size.
  - A larger size grows the upper line up from its point and the lower line down from its point (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
  - `size` is from 1/1080 to 4096/1080, also in markup. Size 1..4096 is the range of a title, measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2.
  - Color, outline and background take the values and limits of the subtitle style (D61).
  - The background is one box behind the block. It reaches 0.15 of the text height past its lines.
  - `shape` gives the background its own size and place (p. 1185). All of these are measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2:
    - `size` is its width and height, as fractions of the picture, from 0 to 2. A side of 0 draws no box.
    - The box has hard edges on whole pixels. Its width and height round down, and then its left and top round to the nearest pixel.
    - Its middle is the title's `position` plus `center`, at any anchor and any text. `center` is in fractions of the picture.
    - A lower third's box takes the position of the lower line. The upper line does not move it or turn it.
    - A Scroll's box stands at the middle of the text box as wide as the picture. It does not scroll with the text.
    - The y of a Scroll's `position` moves its box, and it does not move its text.
    - Zoom does not scale the box. Rotation turns the box and its `center` about the position.
    - The picture's edges cut the box before it turns. A cut edge has no outline.
    - `corner` is its radius, as a fraction of the picture height. No radius passes half of a side, on each axis apart.
    - `outline_width` is in pixels of the timeline, and half of it is outside the box.
    - The outline is not changed by the background's opacity.
  - The Title section shows `shape` as Box Width, Box Height, Box Across, Box Down, Box Corner, Box Outline and Box Outline Width. Empty Width and Height fit the box to the text. Empty Width alone takes 90%, the usual width in a file.
  - A drop shadow is a blurred copy of the text and its outline. It is over the background and under the text, as in Resolve.
  - By default, the shadow is black, 75% opaque and blurred 20. These are Resolve's own values.
  - Its offset is 0, which draws no shadow (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
  - Zoom draws the text larger, and the outline and the background box grow with it. The zoom scales the outline, measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2.
  - Rotation turns the whole title, box and shadow too, counterclockwise.
  - The picture's edges cut a title after its zoom and before it turns. Thus a turned title does not show what is past those edges (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
  - Zoom and rotation work about the `position` of a Text title (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
  - For a lower third, they work on each line about its own point (D67).
  - Each line of a lower third has its own `position`, `zoom`, `rotation` and keyframes (p. 1178):
    - `line` in `titles.set` names the line.
    - In the `keyframes` requests, these values name the line: `upper_position`, `upper_zoom`, `upper_rotation`, `lower_position`, `lower_zoom` and `lower_rotation`.
    - The Title section has Across, Down, Zoom and Rotation under Upper Line and under Lower Line.
    - The two lines go together until a request names a line. Each line then keeps its values and goes apart.
    - A request that names no line then changes the lower line.
    - The `position` of a line is its own point. The Title section shows each line's point.
    - The background box follows the place, zoom and rotation of the lower line. The upper line does not move it (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
    - Each line has its own shadow, which turns with the line (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
    - From the bottom, the order is the box, the lower line and the upper line. Each line is over its own shadow (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
    - Opacity fades them as one picture. Text at half opacity over the box does not show the box (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
    - Opacity, text and style are the whole title's.
    - A switch to Text or Scroll drops the upper line's values.
  - Over the tracks below (§8.14):
    - A clip on a track above covers the title where its picture covers the frame (D75).
    - A clip that fades in or out above the title fades over it.
    - Subtitles go over the title.
  - The lines of a scroll wrap at title safe, and align left, in the middle or right. By default they align left, 1% of the picture width in from its left edge (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
  - They rise at a steady speed (X55). At the first frame of the clip, they are just below the picture. At its last frame, they are just above it.
  - A dissolve at the edge of a title fades it in or out, as Resolve does.
  - The title is drawn through all of the frames of the dissolve, at the level of the dissolve, on both sides of its edge.
  - The `opacity` of a title fades the whole title. It is Composite's Opacity in Resolve.
  - A title has no speed.
  - It draws the same in every export, in `frame.render` and in the viewer, whatever the subtitle settings.
  - HDR white is graphics white, as for burned-in subtitles.

- **D67. Titles in OTIO files** (§8.14; deviation X9).
  - The manual does not say how a title goes into an OTIO file.
  - vidcut writes a title in the shape of Resolve Studio 21.1.1's own file of its titles (made through its scripting API).
  - **The clip:**
    - It is a `Clip.2`. Its media is a `GeneratorReference`.
    - The kind is `Rich` for Text, `Rich Lower Left`, `Rich Lower Middle` or `Rich Lower Right` for a lower third, or `Scroll`.
    - The name is Text, Left, Middle or Right Lower Third, or Scroll.
    - The parameters are a `Rich Text` group for a Text title, and two for a lower third.
    - Then come `Drop Shadow` at Resolve's defaults, `Stroke` and `Background`.
  - **A `Rich Text` group:**
    - It holds its lines as Qt rich text (`title blob`).
    - It has a span for each run of words with the same style.
    - A span has its font: for a title that names none, the family that vidcut draws it in.
    - A span also has its size in points, its weight (600 bold, else 400), italic, underline and color.
    - A span with tracking has it in `letter-spacing`, in `px`. One `px` is 100/85 of a unit, and 200/85 below 0 (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
    - A span with lines has `underline`, `overline` or `line-through` in `text-decoration`.
    - A shifted span has `super` or `sub` in `vertical-align`.
    - A span has its case in `text-transform` (`uppercase`, `lowercase`) or in `font-variant` (`small-caps`).
    - Resolve does not draw `text-transform:capitalize` from a file. Thus for `title` vidcut writes it and the capital letters.
    - Title Caps in Resolve's Inspector writes nothing in the file, and the text stays as typed.
    - Each paragraph has its line spacing in `line-height`. A Text title has 0, and the upper and lower line of a lower third have 1 and 0, unless a title sets another value.
    - The group has the alignment of the lines.
    - Its `anchor` is on a 3×3 grid, counted from the bottom left: 0 bottom left, 4 middle, 8 top right.
    - Its `position` is in fractions of the picture, from its left and bottom.
    - Its `transformationZoomX` and `Y` are the zoom of its title or line.
    - Its `transformationRotationAngle` is the rotation of its title or line in degrees, counterclockwise.
    - The zoom and the rotation are about its anchor point.
    - Resolve zooms the text alone, and turns the background and shadow with it.
  - **Points:**
    - A point is a pixel of the picture, as Resolve draws a title that it reads from a file.
    - Resolve's own titles have 1/1080 of the picture height to a point ([gotchas](gotchas.md#resolves-otio-files)).
  - **Positions:**
    - The `position` of a Text title is the point of its block that its `anchor` names.
    - The upper line of a lower third stands on its point (anchor 0, 1 or 2). By default, this is 20% up the picture.
    - Its lower line hangs from its point (6, 7 or 8). By default, this is 22% up the picture.
    - The file holds the points of `position`, with y counted from the bottom.
    - Resolve's own lower thirds put their upper line first.
  - **Stroke:**
    - `strokeSize` is the width of the outline in pixels of the timeline, as a whole number. Thus 16 is the maximum of both.
    - `strokeOutsideOnly` holds `outside`.
    - An outline of 0 is `strokeSize` 0.
  - **Background:**
    - With `shape`, these are its values. Without `shape`, it is the box that vidcut draws, with square corners.
    - `backgroundWidth` and `backgroundHeight` are in fractions of the picture.
    - `backgroundCenter` is from the anchor point of the last `Rich Text` group. Resolve centers its box on that point.
    - `backgroundCornerRadius` is `corner`, and `backgroundOutlineWidth` and `backgroundOutlineColor` are the outline.
    - No background is a height of 0.
  - **Drop shadow:**
    - `shadow offset` is in fractions of the picture, right and up.
    - `shadow` is its blur: a Gaussian whose radius is that many 3300ths of the picture height.
    - `shadow opacity` is in percent, and `shadow color` is its color.
    - No shadow is an offset of 0, which Resolve does not draw.
  - **Opacity:**
    - The opacity of a title is the `opacity` of the clip's `Composite` effect, in percent.
    - Resolve draws it (checked at 50%).
    - A `Cross Dissolve` at its edge fades it there, as in vidcut (checked frame by frame).
  - The `Title` itself also goes in the `vidcut` metadata of the clip. Thus vidcut reads its own titles back exactly.
  - Resolve drops this metadata.
  - **Import:** a Resolve title of those kinds comes in as a title, with these parts:
    - its lines
    - the font, size and color of its upper line (of the first line, for a Text title)
    - an outline, from a `strokeSize` above 0
    - its other spans as markup, where they are different from that line
    - a background with its `shape`, from a `backgroundWidth` and a `backgroundHeight` above 0
    - a drop shadow, from an offset other than 0
    - the place, the zoom (X), the rotation and the keyframes of its Text group, or of each line of a lower third
  - The lines of a lower third go apart when the place, zoom, rotation or keyframes of their two groups are different.
  - Resolve's own lower thirds are different: the upper line stands at 20% from the bottom, and the lower line hangs from 22%.
  - The lower line of a lower third is the group anchored by its top, else its second group.
  - A Text title takes the alignment of its first line and the `anchor` of its group. The manual gives one alignment and one anchor for the entire title (p. 1178).
  - **A Scroll** has these parts:
    - a `Text` group, with its plain `text` and its `position`. The x of the position is the middle of a text box as wide as the picture.
    - a `Formatting` group, with its `font` as Qt writes one, its `size` in points (pixels, as above), its `alignment`, `anchor` and `color`. The `alignment` is 0 left, 1 middle or 2 right in that box.
    - then `Drop Shadow` and `Background`, with no `Stroke`
  - vidcut writes the left edge, middle or right edge of its lines there, in plain text, because Resolve's Scroll has no rich text.
  - vidcut reads a Scroll back with no outline.
  - vidcut reads the generators that it draws (§8.17). It carries the other generators.
  - Resolve writes Text+ as a `MissingReference`, and loses it itself.
  - vidcut reads Text+ as a clip with no media (D40).
  - Resolve Studio 21.1.1 draws a file that vidcut writes as vidcut does, within a few pixels at 3840×2160, except for the outline.
  - vidcut reads Resolve's export of that file back to the same titles (checked through its scripting API).

  - The default of the request stays files.
- **D70. Word times on cues** (§8.12; p. 1241).
  - In the manual, a button updates the word timings. It does not say what edits do to them.
  - Measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2: no edit changes the word times of a cue ([X58](deviations.md#x58-animated-subtitles-are-a-track-style-made-from-the-speech-in-one-step)).
  - A cue keeps the frame on which each word starts, counted from the start of the cue.
  - A word is what spaces and line breaks separate in its text, without the markup.
  - Create Subtitles from Audio keeps the word starts of the model.
  - A model without word times gives the estimates that the times of its cues come from (D62).
  - `analyze.words` never returns these estimates (D63).
  - WebVTT inline timestamps import as word times. A word without one is spread between its neighbors.
  - Edits of cues:
    - A move of the edge of a cue keeps each word on its frame, clamped into the cue.
    - A split leaves each part the words of its own frames. The words outside it go to its edge.
    - A join keeps the words of the first cue only.
    - Re-time scales the words of a cue with its length.
    - A timeline edit maps each word through the frames that its cue follows (D57).
    - A word on a removed frame starts on the next frame that is kept.
  - New text with the same number of words keeps their times.
  - With another number of words, the times are read again from the words kept for the files of the clips under the cue.
  - Else the words are spread over the cue by their characters.
  - Synchronize Word Timings (`subtitles.time_words`, in the menu of a subtitle track header) hears the speech as Create Subtitles from Audio does.
  - It matches the words of the cues to the speech in order, across all the cues timed, as Re-time does.
  - Thus captions that are off from the speech still find their words.
  - A matched word starts where it was heard, kept inside its cue.
  - The other words are spread between their matched neighbors by their characters.

- **D75. Transform and Crop** (§8.15; pp. 766–768; deviation X65).
  - The manual gives the controls.
  - It does not give the units, the order in which they work, or how a placed clip plays over the tracks below.
  - The order, and how a placed clip plays, match Resolve's renders.
  - **Order:**
    - The picture of a clip is fitted into the frame.
    - Then it is cropped, flipped, leaned by pitch and yaw, zoomed, turned and moved, in that order (D93).
    - Its composite mode then mixes it with the tracks below.
    - The soft edges of the crop and the opacity fade that mix over the tracks below.
    - The crop and the flips work about the middle of the whole picture.
    - The zoom and the turn work about its anchor point (D92). The usual anchor point is the middle.
    - Pitch and yaw work about the middle of the picture, in its own axes (D93).
    - The zoom works across and down in the picture's own axes, before the turn.
    - The rotation is counterclockwise.
    - The crop cuts the source picture, and what is left stays where it was.
    - The crop cuts only whole source pixels. These facts are measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2, in a timeline of 1280 x 720.
      - The number of pixels that a side loses is its crop in source pixels, rounded down.
      - On a 1280 x 720 picture, Crop Left 0.75 cuts nothing, and Crop Left 1.5 cuts one column.
      - On a 3840 x 2160 picture, Crop Left 1.5 is 4.5 source pixels. It cuts 4, so the edge is at x = 1.333.
      - On a 1000 x 562 picture, Crop Top 1 is 0.78 of a source pixel, and it cuts nothing.
      - The rule is the same with Zoom, Rotation and Softness.
      - A crop with keyframes, pixels that are not square and a rotation in the file were not measured. vidcut uses the same rule, in the pixels of the upright file.
      - A compound clip was not measured. vidcut counts its crop in pixels of the frame.
  - **Over the tracks below:**
    - A clip covers the tracks below it only where its picture covers the frame, at its usual values too.
    - The tracks below show in the bars around a picture of another shape, outside a placed picture, and where it is cropped.
    - Where there are no tracks below, these areas are black.
    - A clip whose fitted picture leaves no bar covers them all ([§13](#the-limit-for-a-thin-bar)).
    - Its edges are smoothed over a pixel.
    - The two sides of a dissolve mix into each other first, and then go over the tracks below.
    - Thus the tracks below do not show through where both sides cover the frame (D50).
  - **Soft edges and opacity** (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.1, with 16-bit renders):
    - Opacity mixes the clip with what is below it: below + (clip − below) × opacity.
    - The fade of Softness is |Softness| × the width of the fitted picture / 400 pixels, rounded down. The zoom scales it with the picture.
    - Above 0, each cropped side fades out over what it cuts off. The edge of the picture still cuts it. Thus a side that is not cropped stays hard.
    - Below 0, all four sides fade in over the picture, cropped or not. The crop still cuts it.
    - Across the fade, the coverage follows a smoothstep. It is full over the first eighth of the fade.
    - At a corner, the distances outside the two sides join in a smooth maximum. Thus the corners are round.
    - Keyframes of Softness and Opacity without handles change in a straight line. After the last keyframe, the value stays.
  - **Composite modes** (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.1, with 16-bit renders):
    - A mode works on the R'G'B' code values of the clip (a) and of what is below it (b), from 0 to 1.
    - Where there are no tracks below, b is black.
    - The mix of the mode is M. The result is b + (M − b) × the opacity.
    - vidcut also fades M by the coverage of the edges and the soft edges, as it fades a clip at Normal.
    - Resolve keeps values above 1 and below 0 between the layers.
    - Each channel apart:
      - Add and Linear Dodge: a + b. Subtract: b − a. Difference: |a − b|.
      - Multiply: a × b. Screen: 1 − (1 − a)(1 − b). Exclusion: a + b − 2ab.
      - Darken and Lighten: the smaller and the larger of a and b.
      - Overlay: H(b, a). Hardlight: H(a, b). H(x, y) is 2xy for x ≤ 0.5. Above 0.5, it is 1 − 2(1 − x)(1 − y).
      - Softlight: the soft light of the W3C, with a as the source.
      - Color Dodge: b / (1 − a), at most 1. For a ≥ 1, it is 1.
      - Color Burn: 1 − (1 − b) / a, at least 0. For a ≤ 0, it is 0.
      - Divide: b / a, at most 1. For a ≤ 0, it is 1 when b > 0, else 0.
      - Linear Burn: a + b − 1. Linear Light: b + 2a − 1.
      - Vivid Light: the Color Burn of b by 2a for a ≤ 0.5. Above 0.5, the Color Dodge of b by 2a − 1.
      - Pin Light: min(b, 2a) for a ≤ 0.5. Above 0.5, max(b, 2a − 1).
      - Hard Mix: 1 when a + b ≥ 1, else 0.
    - The whole color:
      - Lighter Color and Darker Color take the color with the larger or the smaller sum of R, G and B. On a tie, they take b.
      - The spread of a color is the length of (R − G, G − B, B − R). The luma here is the luma of BT.709.
      - Hue: the luma of b, plus a minus the luma of a. That difference is scaled by the spread of b over the spread of a.
      - Saturation: the luma of b, plus b minus its luma. That difference is scaled by the spread of a over the spread of b.
      - Color: the BT.601 Y of b with the Cb and Cr of a. Luminosity: the Y of a with the Cb and Cr of b.
      - Resolve goes back from Y, Cb and Cr by a matrix a little off BT.601. vidcut uses the matrix fitted to the renders.
    - The manual's words do not match the renders for some modes, for example Subtract and Hard Mix (pp. 1200–1202). vidcut follows the renders.
    - vidcut also keeps these values between the layers. The export keeps them in 32-bit floats, and the viewer in 16-bit floats.
    - In an export, a title between layers cuts them ([todo](todo/composite-masks.md)).
  - **Units** (vidcut's, X65):
    - `position` is where the middle of the picture goes before the zoom and the turn: `[x, y]` in fractions of the frame from its top left.
    - Each value of `position` is from −3.5 to 4.5: 4 frames either way from the middle. `[0.5, 0.5]` leaves the picture in place.
    - `zoom` is how much larger the picture is drawn across and down, each from 0 to 100. One number sets both. A zoom of 0 shows nothing of the picture.
    - `rotation` is in degrees, from −100000 to 100000.
    - `anchor` is the point that the zoom and the turn work about, in fractions of the picture (D92). Each value is from −3.5 to 4.5.
    - `pitch` and `yaw` are each from −1.5 to 1.5. Usual: 0.
    - Positive pitch puts the top farther away. Positive yaw puts the left closer.
    - The flips mirror the picture left to right and top to bottom.
    - The crop cuts each side by a fraction of the source picture, from 0 to 1. The sides have no limit together. A crop of the whole picture shows nothing of it.
    - `crop_softness` is from −100 to 100, as Softness in Resolve. Usual: 0.
    - `composite_mode` is `normal` or the name of another mode in snake case, for example `color_dodge`. Usual: `normal`.
    - `opacity` is from 0 to 1. Usual: 1.
    - Values at their usual place are left out of the project file.
  - **Keyframes:**
    - Position, zoom, rotation, the anchor point, pitch, yaw, each crop side, the crop softness and the opacity can have keyframes.
    - A keyframe is the volume `Keyframe`: a source frame, a value, an In handle and an Out handle (D81).
    - Position stores X on the keyframe and Y beside it, one Y for each keyframe.
    - Zoom and the anchor point do the same. The other values store one value.
    - The curve between two keyframes is the volume Bezier curve (X74).
    - A handle value moves the key's own value.
    - Position Y, zoom Y and anchor Y keep the handle frames and use a value offset of 0.
    - Before the first keyframe the value is the first. After the last it is the last.
    - With keyframes, that curve is the value. The plain value is not played.
    - A first keyframe does not change the plain value. When the last keyframes of a value go, the plain value returns.
    - The viewer and the export sample the curve at the source frame of each frame.
    - A change of a keyed value at `at` sets a keyframe there, as volume does (D82).
    - `picture.set` takes `at`. With no `at`, it uses the playhead.
    - A value with no keyframes ignores `at`.
    - `keyframes.list`, `keyframes.add`, `keyframes.set`, `keyframes.move` and `keyframes.remove` take these values in `param`.
    - `keyframes.copy`, `keyframes.cut` and `keyframes.paste` take them in `param` too.
    - `timeline.get` names each picture value that has keyframes, as it names `volume`.
    - The Inspector shows the value at the playhead. Its Keyframe button adds or removes the keyframe there (X70).
      - With the playhead outside the clip, the clip time is the playhead's frame minus the clip's first frame (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
      - The Inspector then shows the curve at that time, and the button or a new number adds the keyframe there, outside the clip.
    - With several clips selected, the header of Resolve's Inspector is Multiple Clips, on the Cut page and the Edit page (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
      - No value has a Keyframe button or arrows.
      - A value with keyframes on one of these clips is off. It takes no typed number, and its Reset does nothing.
      - A value without keyframes takes a typed number on all these clips.
      - vidcut's Inspector has the same rules. The Reset shows on a value that is off.
      - A request that names several clips still keys each clip at its own frame under `at`.
    - Previous and Next move the playhead to the keyframe before or after it, also outside the clip (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
    - A right-click on the button sets Linear, Ease In, Ease Out or Ease In and Out.
    - The Reset of one value removes its keyframes and puts the usual value back.
      - Zoom, Position and Anchor Point each have X and Y on one row with one Reset (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
      - That Reset removes all the keyframes of the row. X and Y go back to 0, or to 100% on Zoom. The Reset of Zoom also turns the link on.
    - The Reset of Transform, Crop or Composite removes the keyframes of that section.
    - A clip with any keyframes shows a mark on its timeline bar.
    - Flips, the composite mode and Dynamic Zoom have no keyframes.
    - A speed change keeps each keyframe at its frame of the clip, as volume does (D81).
    - `edit.decompose` moves a compound clip's keyframes onto the source frames of each clip inside.
  - **Compound clips:**
    - A compound clip is placed as one picture. The pictures and the titles inside it go with it, cut at its frame and by its crop.
    - Its tracks mix first, as the tracks of the timeline do. Its opacity then fades that mix, or its composite mode mixes that mix with the tracks below.
      - Measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2, with 16-bit renders. Inside the compound clip, a picture cropped to its left half is over a full picture.
      - At an opacity of 50% over white, the frame is 0.5 × the mix + 0.5.
      - At Multiply over 50% gray, the frame is 0.5 × the mix.
    - Its soft edges fade each picture and title inside it ([todo](todo/compound-opacity.md)).
    - A title inside it keeps its own place, zoom and rotation (X54) in the frame of the compound clip.
    - The tracks below it show wherever its tracks leave its frame uncovered.
    - A clip inside it that fills the frame at its usual values has the edges of the compound clip only. Thus they are smoothed once.
    - `edit.decompose` gives the picture of the compound clip to each of its clips that has no picture of its own and fills the frame.
    - With a Dynamic Zoom, a clip takes the picture only when it spans all of the compound clip.
    - Thus each of these clips stays where it was in the compound clip, with the same size, crop and zoom.
    - The other clips do not take the picture. A warning names them.
  - An export fits a placed picture at the size it shows, up to the size of the source. Thus a punch-in keeps the detail of the source.
  - The viewer draws it from the decoded picture with the same sums.
  - Titles keep their own place (X54).
  - **Viewer handles** (those of a title, X52):
    - A drag inside moves the picture. Its middle, as drawn, snaps to the middle and thirds of the frame, and a guide shows.
    - Shift keeps the drag on one axis, and Alt turns snapping off.
    - A corner zooms both ways in proportion, and a side zooms one way, about the anchor point. The pointer shows the zoom.
    - A corner reaches Zoom 0 with the pointer on the anchor point. Past that point the zoom grows again, with no mirror (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
    - The handles of a picture at Zoom 0 stay on the anchor point. A corner drag from there sets the zoom from the distance to the anchor point.
    - The handle above the box, or a drag just outside a corner, turns the picture about the anchor point.
    - A cross marks the anchor point. It has no drag (X82).
    - Shift turns it in steps of 15°. The pointer shows the angle.
    - The Crop handles move one side each. The uncropped picture shows dashed.
    - A Crop handle crosses the picture until the crop of its side is 1, the whole picture. The handle stays (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
    - When two Crop handles are on the same point, a drag takes the side with the larger crop.
    - While the Transform handles show, the wheel or a pinch over the picture zooms it as a corner does, 10% for each notch.
    - Alt+wheel turns it, 1° for each notch, counterclockwise for wheel up.
    - With Shift too, Alt+wheel turns it to the next multiple of 15°.
    - The picture follows the drag or the wheel.
    - One `picture.set` follows on release, or when the wheel has been still for a moment.
    - It also follows when the handles hide, the clip changes or a drag starts.
  - **Tools row:**
    - Each flip is one `picture.set`.
    - Reset is `picture.clear`, as in the right-click menu.
    - They act on the clip that the handles go on: the selected clip at the playhead, else the top clip there.
    - While there is no such clip, the buttons of the row are greyed out.
    - A lit Transform or Crop is not greyed out, and still hides its handles.
  - `picture.get`, `picture.set` and `picture.clear` do the same from a script.
  - `edit.copy` and `edit.paste` carry the picture of a clip.
  - **OTIO:**
    - The picture goes in the `vidcut` metadata of the clip.
    - It also goes in Resolve's Transform, Cropping and Retime and Scaling, fitted.
    - An import takes vidcut's own metadata. Else it reads those effects.
    - Resolve's crop, fill and stretch become a zoom, and its anchor point becomes the `anchor` ([gotchas](gotchas.md#resolves-otio-files)).
    - Keyframes of position, zoom, rotation, the anchor point, pitch, yaw and crop are read and played when they fit this model.
    - A parameter vidcut does not key, or a keyframe it cannot read, leaves Transform, Cropping and Retime and Scaling on the clip, unplayed (X9).
    - Pitch and yaw are played, with or without keyframes (D93).
    - Anchor point keyframes are read only on a picture that Retime and Scaling leaves at its fitted size (D92).
    - This is also true when they make a picture that vidcut refuses, such as a crop that leaves nothing.
    - The export names the clips moved further than Resolve's Pan and Tilt go, 4 pictures, and writes them at 4.
  - **Dynamic Zoom.** The manual gives the boxes and the eases, not the units or the timing.
    - The units, the order and the timing match Resolve Studio 21.1.1 ([gotchas](gotchas.md#resolves-otio-files)). The eases are vidcut's own (X67).
    - **The boxes.** `dynamic_zoom` has a `start` box, an `end` box and an `ease`. A box has the shape of the picture.
    - The `center` of a box is `[x, y]` in fractions of the picture from its top left, each from −100 to 100.
    - The `size` of a box is its width and height as a fraction of those of the picture, from 1/100 to 100.
    - **The order.** The picture is drawn at 1/size about the center of the box, and that center goes to the middle of the picture.
    - This step comes after the flips and before the zoom: fit, crop, flip, Dynamic Zoom, pitch and yaw, zoom, turn, move.
    - The crop moves with the picture, and the outline of the picture grows with the zoom.
    - **The timing.** On frame k of the clip's n frames, t is k/n, and e is the ease of t.
    - The box is e of the way from `start` to `end`, on a straight line. Thus the end box comes one frame after the last frame.
    - In a compound clip, t follows the frame of the inner clip that shows. A reversed compound plays the zoom backward, and a frozen one holds it.
    - **Turned on**, it is a pull out from 125%, with Linear. The start box has the size 0.8 and the end box the size 1, both in the middle.
    - **How it plays:** a clip with a Dynamic Zoom is placed over the tracks below it. The viewer and the export draw the same frame.
    - **In the viewer**, Alt+T shows the boxes on the clip that T uses (X65).
    - The viewer still draws that clip with its Dynamic Zoom, as the output shows it.
    - The boxes on the screen are not the `start` and `end` boxes of the data. They show where these boxes put the picture.
    - The green box shows where the picture is at the start of the clip. The red box shows where it is at the end.
    - A Start Zoom of 71% in the Inspector is a `size` of 1/0.71. Then the green box is 71% of the width and of the height of the clip.
    - The boxes stay in place while the clip plays. The picture moves from the green box to the red box.
    - The manual says that the green box shows the starting size and position of the transform, and the red box the ending ones (p. 768).
    - A line joins the centers of the two boxes.
    - A drag inside a box moves it, and Shift keeps the move on one axis.
    - A drag at a corner resizes the box about its center. One `picture.set` follows on release.
    - Dashed boxes show a clip without a Dynamic Zoom, and a drag turns it on.
    - **From a script**, `picture.set` takes `dynamic_zoom`: the fields given replace their own, and null removes it.
    - `picture.clear` with the tool `dynamic_zoom` removes it too.
    - **In an OTIO file**, the Dynamic Zoom is in the `vidcut` metadata of the clip and in Resolve's Dynamic Zoom effect.
    - An import reads Resolve's effect when its center and its scale each have exactly two keys.
    - Otherwise the effect stays on the clip unplayed (X9), with Transform, Cropping and Retime and Scaling.
- **D76. Ducking** (§9; pp. 744, 778–779; deviations X68, X69).
  - The manual gives the controls and their defaults. It does not give the gain curve, the threshold or the limits.
  - **Resolve's gain.** These facts come from renders of Resolve Studio 21.1.1 on Windows, at 48 kHz:
    - The source track had 3 kHz tone bursts of 50 ms to 2 s, at −6 to −60 dBFS peak.
    - The target track had a steady 220 Hz tone at −12 dBFS.
    - The gain is the rendered target divided by the input target, after the source is subtracted.
    - The dip is the Duck Amount exactly: 2.700 dB at 2.7, and 10.000 dB at 10.
    - The level of the source does not change the dip. Bursts at −6, −12, −24 and −36 dBFS give the same curve.
    - Bursts at −48 and −60 dBFS give no dip. Thus the threshold is fixed, between −48 and −36 dBFS peak.
    - A burst of 50 ms gives the full dip, and the same recovery as a burst of 2 s.
    - A gap of 100 ms between two bursts keeps the full dip, with a Hold of 150 ms.
    - The render has no delay. The source and the target stay aligned with the timeline.
    - The recovery, and the fall with a Rise Time of 200 ms, are half cosines in dB.
    - The half cosine fits the recovery to 0.011 dB rms or better. A line in dB fits it to 0.17 dB rms at best.
    - The Recovery Time is 1000 ms at most. A typed 2000 becomes 1000.
    - A Rise Time of 200 ms, a Hold of 500 ms and a Lookahead of 100 ms stay as typed. The other limits are not measured.
    - On a timeline with two audio tracks, Source 1 of a new Ducker is the other track. With three tracks, Source 1 is empty.
  - **A mono target track in Resolve**, with a Duck Amount of 10 dB:
    - The times are in ms. A fall time is from the start of the source. A recovery time is from the end of the source.
    - "Full dip" is within 0.01 dB of the Duck Amount. "Recovery ends" is within 0.001 dB of 0 dB.

    | Lookahead | Rise Time | Hold | Recovery Time | Fall starts | Full dip | Recovery starts | Recovery ends |
    |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
    | 15 | 10 | 150 | 750 | −40 | −6 | +151 | +898 |
    | 15 | 200 | 150 | 750 | −230 | −30 | +171 | +918 |
    | 100 | 10 | 500 | 1000 | −120 | −86 | +512 | +1506 |

    - The fall starts Lookahead + Rise Time + 10 to 15 ms before the source.
    - The recovery starts Hold + 1 to 21 ms after the source ends.
    - The recovery lasts the Recovery Time.
    - A stereo target track has other times (X68). The fall with a Rise Time of 10 ms has another shape (X69).
  - **vidcut's gain**, for a Duck Amount of A dB:
    - A source is active from s to e.
    - The fall starts at s − Lookahead − Rise Time, and ends at s − Lookahead.
    - Fall: gain = −A × (1 − cos(π t / R)) / 2 dB. R is the Rise Time, and t is the time from the start of the fall.
    - The gain stays at −A dB until e + Hold.
    - Recovery: gain = −A × (1 + cos(π t / T)) / 2 dB. T is the Recovery Time, and t is the time from e + Hold.
    - Where the curves of two active parts meet, the lower gain applies. Thus a gap of up to Hold + Lookahead keeps the full dip.
    - With several sources, a part is active where any source is active.
    - Thus a fall starts 10 to 15 ms later than in Resolve, and a recovery 1 to 21 ms sooner.
    - The gain is computed for each sample. Every channel of the track gets the same gain (X68).
    - The preview and the export hear the same samples. The loudness gain of an export comes after the ducking.
  - **What is active** (X69):
    - Speech: a source is active where Silero VAD hears speech in its own sound, as for Ripple Delete Silence (X47).
    - The rule has the defaults of Silero: a probability of 0.5, speech longer than 0.25 s, and pauses of 0.1 s or more.
    - Sound level: a source is active where a 10 ms peak, after its clip gain and fader, is at or above the Threshold.
    - The default Threshold is −42 dBFS, the middle of the range measured in Resolve.
    - A source counts only where it is heard. A muted clip, a muted track and a track that solo silences dip nothing.
  - **A source not yet measured is measured first:**
    - `track.set` measures the sources of the ducking that it turns on or changes. A missing speech detector needs `download`.
    - The preview plays a new source clip without its ducking until its measurement is done. The measurement runs as a job.
    - An export and `analyze.loudness` measure what is missing.
  - **Values:**
    - Duck Amount: 0 to 18 dB (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
    - Lookahead: 0 to 500 ms (measured).
    - Rise Time: 0 to 1000 ms (measured).
    - Hold: 0 to 1000 ms (measured).
    - Recovery Time: 0 to 1000 ms (measured).
    - Threshold: −96 to 0 dBFS.
    - `track.set` refuses a value outside its range, and changes no value (X69).
    - The project file keeps only the values that are not the usual ones.
  - **Sources:**
    - A new ducking has every other track with sound as its sources (X69).
    - A source is a video or audio track. Only its sound counts. A track cannot duck under itself.
    - When a track is removed, it leaves the sources. The other sources follow their new numbers.
  - Each change is one undo step.
  - **Mixer:**
    - The strip of each track has Ducking, its sources (Under …) and its Amount.
    - Advanced opens the Ducking window: Detect, Threshold, Lookahead, Rise Time, Hold and Recovery Time.
  - **OpenTimelineIO:**
    - Resolve Studio 21.1.1 leaves the Ducker out of its OTIO export. Its tracks hold only the audio type, the lock and the solo.
    - This was checked with two active Duckers. A render showed their dips of 10 dB, and the exported file had no trace of them.
    - The ducking goes in the `vidcut` metadata of the track.
    - The notes of an OTIO export say that other editors will not hear it.
- **D77. The volume line** (§9; pp. 1109, 1112; deviation X71).
  - The manual does not give the scale of the volume overlay. It does not tell how much finer Shift makes a drag.
  - The line is in the sound part of the clip, 2 px in from its top and its bottom.
  - It shows only where the sound part is 20 px high or more.
  - **Scale:**
    - −100 dB is at the bottom, and +30 dB is at the top.
    - 0 dB is at 10/13 of the height.
    - A gain g is a part x of its side of 0 dB, from 0 to 1. Above 0 dB, x = g / 30. Below 0 dB, x = −g / 100.
    - The line goes the part x × (2 − x) of the way from 0 dB to the end of its side.
    - Thus near 0 dB, a change of 1 dB moves the line farthest.
    - Near −100 dB and +30 dB, a change of 1 dB moves the line almost not at all.
    - The two sides have the same slope at 0 dB. Thus a drag across 0 dB keeps its speed.
  - **Drag:**
    - Within 4 px of the line, the pointer becomes an up-down arrow.
    - The trim handles at the ends of a clip, and at the sound edit of a J or L cut, come before the line.
    - The line comes before the slip handle of the clip.
    - A drag moves the line as far as the pointer moves. With Shift, it moves the line a tenth as far.
    - Shift can go on or off during the drag. The line does not jump.
    - The gain of the dragged clip goes in steps of 0.1 dB. During the drag, a label beside the line shows it with one decimal.
    - A drag that ends at the gain of the clip, to one decimal, changes nothing.
  - Reason: the usual gains are near 0 dB, as on a fader. `clip.set` sets an exact value anywhere in the range.
- **D78. Characters per second** (§8.12; pp. 1231, 1233; deviation X43).
  - The manual shows the CPS column of the Captions list. It does not tell how CPS is counted.
  - These facts come from the Cut page of Resolve Studio 21.1.1:
    - The count of characters includes the spaces. "Second line" has 11 characters.
    - CPS is the count of characters divided by the duration in seconds, rounded up.
    - 11 characters over 2 s give 6. 11 characters over 1.5 s give 8. 10 characters over 2 s give 5.
    - CPS changes when the duration changes.
  - The manual and these measurements do not tell how Resolve counts a line break.
  - vidcut counts the characters of the text that shows, as Resolve does. The style tags (`<i>`, `<b>`, `<u>` and `<font>`) do not count.
  - A line break counts as one character, as the space between two words on one line does.
  - Thus a text has the same CPS on one line and on two lines.
- **D79. The In and Out fields against other cues** (§8.12; deviation X43).
  - Measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2: an In typed inside the cue before, or an Out typed inside the cue after, changes nothing. Both cues stay.
  - vidcut does the same. Its field shows the stored time at once, and Resolve's field shows it when the cue is selected again. The result is the same.
  - `subtitles.set` does not refuse such a value. A script in Resolve is not measured.
  - A new In or Out overwrites only the part that it newly covers (X43).
  - Measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2: a track has no overlaps, so a field cannot put a cue inside a longer cue.
  - In vidcut, only a cue that already overlaps the edited cue can do that (D56).
  - Such a cue stays as it is. Thus both cues show in that part, as they show together in the overlap.
  - Reason: a split would show its text twice. A trim would take frames outside the newly covered part.
- **D80. Normalize Audio Levels** (§9; pp. 1110–1111; deviation X72).
  - The manual does not give the defaults, the range of the target, or the part of a clip that is measured.
  - **Resolve Studio 21.1.1** (its Edit page):
    - The window keeps the last choice that was applied, in all projects. Thus its factory defaults are not known.
    - On the install that was checked, it opened at Sample Peak Program, −9.0 dBFS, and Relative for two clips.
    - Set Level shows only when two or more clips are selected.
    - A mode with a loudness target and a peak limit takes the lower volume of the two.
      - A clip at −30 LUFS with peaks near 0 dBTP got −1.0 dB with YouTube, not the +16 dB that −14 LUFS needs.
    - ITU-R BS.1770-1, ITU-R BS.1770-4 and Netflix measured a clip that is half silence as the gated loudness.
  - **Modes** (`NormalizeMode`). vidcut has the modes of Resolve, with the numbers that its window showed:

    | Mode | Loudness (LUFS) | Peak limit (dBTP) |
    |---|---|---|
    | Sample Peak | the target is a peak, −9 by default | none |
    | True Peak | the target is a peak, −2 by default | none |
    | ITU-R BS.1770-1 | −23, can change | none |
    | ITU-R BS.1770-4 | −23, can change | −2, can change |
    | ATSC A/85, OP-59, AGCOM 219/09/CSP | −24 | −2 |
    | EBU R128 | −23 | −1, can change |
    | TR-B32 | −24 | −1 |
    | Netflix, Disney 5.1+ | −27 | −2 |
    | YouTube | −14 | −1 |
    | Disney 2.0 | −24 | none |
    | Tencent Video | −16, can change | −2, can change |

  - **Defaults:**
    - The mode is YouTube: −14 LUFS with a −1 dBTP peak limit.
    - It is the value for YouTube that the export's loudness setting names. The limit keeps loud peaks under full scale.
    - Sample Peak starts at −9 dBFS, and True Peak at −2 dBTP.
    - These two come from the Deliver page of Resolve Studio 21.1.2, in a project that never used it.
    - Set Level is Independent.
  - **Range** (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2):
    - A loudness target is from −30 to 0 LUFS. The export's loudness setting takes the same range.
    - A peak target and a peak limit are from −20 to 0 dB.
  - **Measured part:** the sound that the clip plays, with its J or L cut and at its speed.
    - The clip is measured alone, without its volume, mute and Voice Isolation, and without transitions.
    - Its track's volume, mute, solo, Voice Isolation and ducking do not apply.
    - Thus the mixer does not change the result. A muted clip is measured too.
  - **Levels:**
    - Every loudness mode measures the integrated loudness of EBU R128. A loudness at or below −70 LUFS counts as no level.
    - Sample Peak is the highest sample. True Peak is the highest true peak of ITU-R BS.1770.
  - **Gain:**
    - Independent: the new volume is the target less the level. A clip with no level keeps its volume.
    - Relative: the highest level plus volume reaches the target. Every volume changes by that same number of dB.
    - The manual names the highest peak for Relative. vidcut uses the highest level for each mode.
    - A peak limit lowers the change to the limit less the true peak. In Relative, it uses the highest true peak plus volume.
  - Each volume stops at −100 dB and +30 dB (D12). The result marks each clip that stopped (`held`).
  - The result also marks each clip that the peak limit stopped (`limited`).
  - Reason: the export already measures EBU R128 loudness and true peak. Thus the two features agree.
- **D81. Volume keyframes** (§9; pp. 1109, 1112–1113, 1258; deviations X73, X74).
  - The manual does not give the curve between audio keyframes, or what edits do with them.
  - These facts come from Resolve Studio 21.1.1, from its renders and its OTIO files:
    - A volume keyframe is on a whole frame. Its value is in dB.
    - An ease is a Bezier handle on the keyframe: a time and a value from it (`InBez` and `OutBez`).
    - The curve between two keyframes is the cubic Bezier curve of the two keyframes and their handles. It fits the renders within 0.01 dB.
    - A missing handle is on its keyframe. Thus a Linear change is a straight line in dB.
    - Trims, slips and moves keep the keyframes on their source frames. A split gives all of them to both parts.
    - The speed tool of the Cut page keeps each keyframe at its frame of the clip.
  - **Model:**
    - A keyframe has a source frame, a value, an In handle and an Out handle (`Keyframe`).
    - The source frame is a whole frame, as in Resolve's files. vidcut has no keyframes between frames.
    - Before the first keyframe, the volume is the value of the first. After the last keyframe, it is the value of the last.
    - Between two keyframes, the volume follows the Bezier curve. A handle reaches no farther than the next keyframe.
    - With keyframes, the curve is the volume of the clip. `gain_db` is not heard.
    - A first keyframe does not change `gain_db`. When the last keyframes go, `gain_db` is heard again.
    - A keyframe and the curve take −100 to +30 dB (D12).
  - **Eases:**
    - Ease In sets a flat In handle a third of the way to the keyframe before. Ease Out sets a flat Out handle a third of the way to the next one.
    - Ease In and Out sets both. Linear removes both.
    - A keyframe keeps its ease when a neighbor comes, goes or moves. Its handles then go a third of the way to the new neighbors.
    - A handle that was dragged or given (`in`, `out`) stays as it is. Its keyframe then has no ease of the four.
  - **Edits:**
    - Trims, slips, moves and ripples keep the keyframes on their source frames. A keyframe can be outside the clip.
    - On a clip with a speed, a keyframe is on a frame of the retimed source (D48), not on a source frame.
    - A split gives all the keyframes to both parts. Thus `edit.join` gives back the clip.
    - A speed change, a freeze too, keeps each keyframe at its frame of the clip (D48). Thus the source frame under it changes.
    - A clip that plays backward plays its keyframes forward, on their frames of the clip.
    - `edit.copy` and `edit.paste` carry the keyframes with the clip. `keyframes.copy` and `keyframes.paste` copy keyframes alone (§2).
    - Compound clips have no volume of their own. Thus they have no volume keyframes.
  - **Play and export:** each sample takes the volume of the curve at its source position. The player and the export use the same mixer.
  - **OTIO:**
    - vidcut writes the keyframes in the `vidcut` metadata of the clip, and in the `Key Frames` of the clip volume effect.
    - An import reads the `vidcut` metadata first, else the `Key Frames` of the clip volume effect. Then they play (X9).
    - A time in `Key Frames` counts from the first source frame of the item. On a clip at a speed s other than 1, Resolve adds f × (1 − 1/s) and divides the frames from f by s. f is the first source frame.
  - **Requests:** each request that changes keyframes is one undo step. `keyframes.set`, `keyframes.move` and `keyframes.remove` take keyframes by their source frames (`frames`) or their timeline frames (`at`).
  - Reason: the curve is the curve that Resolve plays and writes. Whole frames keep the files that Resolve reads.
- **D82. A volume change on a clip with volume keyframes** (§9; p. 1251; deviation X70).
  - After the first keyframe of a value, Resolve's Inspector adds a keyframe at the playhead for each change of the value (p. 1251).
  - vidcut does the same. `clip.set` with `gain_db`, and the Inspector's Volume, set a keyframe at `at`, or at the playhead.
  - A keyframe that is already on that frame takes the new value.
  - `clip.set` with several clips gives each clip with keyframes a keyframe at its own frame under `at`, also outside the clip (D75). Each clip without keyframes takes the plain value.
  - With several clips selected, the Inspector's Volume is off if one of them has volume keyframes (D75).
  - Without `at` and a playhead, a clip with keyframes keeps its volume. If no clip takes the value, the request fails.
  - The Inspector shows the volume of the curve at the playhead. The Reset of Volume removes the keyframes and sets 0 dB.
  - A drag on the flat volume line of a selected clip also moves all the keyframes of the other selected clips by the same dB.
  - Reason: a change at one point keeps the rest of the curve, as in Resolve. A drag of several lines keeps the differences between the clips (X71).
- **D83. Normalize Audio Levels on a clip with volume keyframes** (§9; deviation X72).
  - The manual does not tell what Normalize does with keyframes.
  - Resolve Studio 21.1.1 moved each of four volume keyframes of a clip by the same −3.4 dB. Thus the curve kept its shape.
  - vidcut measures the clip without its volume and without its keyframes, as for every clip (D80).
  - The volume of a clip with keyframes is the value of its highest keyframe. Normalize sets that volume as it sets the volume of other clips.
  - Every keyframe of the clip then moves by the same dB. Each stops at −100 and +30 dB.
  - `from_db` and `gain_db` of the result give the highest keyframe before and after.
  - Reason: Normalize changes the volume of a clip. The curve keeps its shape, and its loudest part gets the volume that a flat clip would get.
- **D84. The volume curve in the measurements** (§9; deviations X47, X69).
  - The manual does not tell what these measurements do with keyframes.
  - These take the volume of the curve at each frame, as the mix plays it:
    - ducking by sound level (D76)
    - Ripple Delete Silence by sound level (X47)
    - the quiet edges of `edit.ripple_delete_words`
    - the meters of the Mixer
  - Resolve Studio 21.1.1 does the same in Ripple Delete Silence. It cut exactly the part that keyframes turned down to −60 dB.
  - Reason: they measure what the user hears. A part that a keyframe makes quiet is quiet for them too.
- **D85. Keyframe keys** (§11; pp. 1113, 1251).
  - In the manual, [ and ] go from keyframe to keyframe (p. 1251). Backspace deletes the selected keyframes (p. 1113).
  - vidcut: [ and ] go to the previous and the next volume keyframe of the selected clips. Only keyframes inside the clips count.
  - While Ripple Delete Silence is open, [ and ] go to its stripes (X48).
  - Delete and Backspace remove the selected keyframes, and nothing else.
  - Reason: these are the keys of the manual. The window of Ripple Delete Silence needs [ and ] for its stripes while it is open.
- **D90. Safe Area sizes** (§4.5; pp. 682–683, 1177).
  - The manual does not give the Action and Title sizes as percents of the frame.
  - Action safe is 93% of the extents width and of its height, centered.
  - Title safe is 90% of the extents width and of its height, centered.
  - These are the values in SMPTE ST 2046-1 and EBU R 95.
  - Title layout still uses 90% of the picture (D65). The overlay uses the same share of the extents.
  - The extents are the framing box. Default framing uses the picture.
- **D91. The viewer timecode menu** (§4.3; pp. 713–714; deviation X81).
  - Auto is the default. It follows the viewer.
  - Source modes show Source Timecode.
  - Timeline mode shows record timecode.
  - Source Timecode is the media frame on screen plus the file start timecode (X8).
  - A file without a start timecode starts at 00:00:00:00.
  - Source Frame is that frame as a whole number, from 0.
  - Record Timecode is the playhead on screen plus the timeline start.
  - Record Frame is that playhead as a whole number, from 0.
  - On a tape, Source Frame is the frame inside the clip.
  - Record Frame on a tape is the frame along the tape.
  - On the timeline, the source frame is the top shown clip.
  - `Clip::source_at` gives it, so a speed is included.
  - A gap, a title, a generator, or a compound has no source frame.
  - The field then shows a dash.
  - Timer 2 is the playhead as a timecode from 0.
  - It ignores the timeline start. It has no reset.
  - Timer 1 is the playhead minus a zero, as a timecode from 0.
  - Reset Timer 1 stores the playhead on screen as that player's zero.
  - The other player's zero stays.
  - Until a reset, Timer 1 matches Timer 2.
  - The count and the zeros last for the session.
  - They are not in the project, and not in `preferences.toml`.
  - A new session starts at Auto, with no zeros.
  - Opening a project leaves them.
  - Drop-frame follows D47.
  - A timecode uses `;` before the frames when the project drops frames.
  - A frame count has no drop-frame mark.
  - Copy Timecode copies the text in the field.
  - `player.state` returns that text, the count, and the zeros.
  - Paste Timecode and Enter move the playhead on screen to that count.
  - In Auto, a source viewer reads a typed timecode as Source Timecode. Thus the text that the field shows goes to the frame on screen.
  - A typed frame count is a whole number.
  - Digits in a frame count are not filled as a timecode.
  - `viewer.set` sets the viewer, the count, the zero, and a paste.
- **D92. The anchor point** (§8.15; pp. 735, 767; deviation X82).
  - The manual gives Anchor Point X and Y in the Inspector (p. 735).
  - It says that all transforms are centered on the anchor point, and that the rotation turns about it (pp. 735, 767).
  - Resolve's files show that Resolve flips about the middle ([gotchas](gotchas.md#resolves-otio-files)). vidcut does the same.
  - The zoom and the turn work about the anchor point. The flips and the Dynamic Zoom work about the middle.
  - **Order:** fit, crop, flip, Dynamic Zoom, pitch and yaw about the middle (D93), zoom and turn about the anchor point, move.
  - `position` is the move. The anchor point stays where `position` puts it, whatever the zoom and the turn.
  - Thus the zoom and turn handles of the viewer keep the anchor point still. They do not change `position`.
  - The anchor point is a point of the picture as it shows after the flips.
  - **Units:** `anchor` is `[x, y]` in fractions of the picture from its top left, each from −3.5 to 4.5.
  - `[0.5, 0.5]` is the middle. This is the usual value, and the project file leaves it out.
  - **Dynamic Zoom:** the anchor point is a point of the place of the picture, not of its content. A Dynamic Zoom does not move it.
  - Thus the picture zooms and turns about one point of the screen for the whole clip.
  - An anchor point on the content would move across the screen while the box moves.
  - **OTIO:** an import reads the anchor point of Resolve's Transform as `anchor`. An export writes it back.
  - With a crop, fill or stretch, the move that the scaling makes about the middle goes into `position` ([gotchas](gotchas.md#resolves-otio-files)).
  - **Keyframes:** the anchor point is one keyframe for X and Y, as Position is (D75).
    - Each key in the `Key Frames` of `transformationAnchorPoint` is a `POINTF`, as the keys of `dynamicZoomCenter` are.
    - On a 1920×1080 clip, the key's value is `[X / 1920, Y / 1080]`, with the signs of the Inspector.
    - In Resolve, an anchor point keyframe stays linear. The ease menu of the Inspector and the curve tools of the tray do not change it.
    - The tray draws no curve for the anchor point. Resolve writes no Bezier handles on these keys.
    - Thus vidcut's anchor point keyframes are linear. `keyframes.add` and `keyframes.set` refuse an ease or handles on them.
    - Resolve imports its own file with anchor point keys and gives the same keys.
    - The facts about these keys are measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2.
    - An import reads these keys only when Retime and Scaling leaves the picture at its fitted size, and only without Bezier handles.
    - With a crop, fill or stretch, the move of the anchor point goes into `position`. Thus `position` would need keys of its own.
  - `picture.set` takes `anchor`. `picture.clear` with `transform` puts it back in the middle.
- **D93. Pitch and yaw** (§8.15; pp. 735, 767; deviation X83).
  - The manual lists Pitch and Yaw with the other Transform values (pp. 735, 767).
  - It does not give their units or the order in which they work.
  - The range, the order and the lean come from Resolve Studio 21 stills and OTIO ([gotchas](gotchas.md#resolves-otio-files)).
  - Each is a number from −1.5 to 1.5. Usual: 0. The project file leaves 0 out.
  - They lean the picture about its middle, in its own axes.
  - The lean comes before the zoom and the turn about the anchor, and before the move.
  - The lean is a projective divide about the middle. There is no cosine foreshortening.
  - The flips and the crop come before the lean ([gotchas](gotchas.md#resolves-otio-files)). The renders do not show where the Dynamic Zoom comes.
  - vidcut leans the picture as it shows after the flips, the crop and the Dynamic Zoom.
  - Positive pitch puts the top farther away, and the bottom closer.
  - Positive yaw puts the left closer, and the right farther.
  - With no rotation, yaw keeps vertical edges vertical.
  - With no rotation, pitch keeps horizontal edges horizontal.
  - The viewer and the export use the same map.
  - The Inspector sets them. The viewer handles do not set them (X83).
  - `picture.set` takes `pitch` and `yaw`. `picture.clear` with `transform` puts both back to 0.
  - An import reads `transformationPitch` and `transformationYaw`. An export writes them back.
  - Each can have keyframes (D75). Their keys have the form of the keys of the rotation, in the units of the plain value.
- **D98. Render cache** (deviations X103, X104, X105, X106; the render cache pages, pp. 143–144 and 199–206, are outside the Cut page).
  - The render cache keeps on disk the frames that are slow to make. The viewer plays them from there. Exports do not use it.
  - The project has a mode (`render_cache`): Off, Smart or User. A new project has Off, as in Resolve.
    - Playback > Render Cache sets it. `project.set` with `render_cache` also sets it.
  - Each clip has a Render Cache value: Auto, On or Off.
    - The menu of a clip has Render Cache (p. 205). `clip.set` with `render_cache` also sets it.
    - Off keeps the slow media and the AI Super Scale of the clip out of both modes.
    - Off does not stop speed changes, Retime Processes, transitions, opacity and composite modes. Resolve caches these in its sequence cache, and no clip setting reaches it (p. 204).
    - With Off, a speed change of the clip renders only at the timeline level.
  - Each effect of a clip has a Render Cache check. It is off, unless set.
    - The menu of a clip has Render Cache Effects, with each effect of the clip in its order (p. 205). `effects.set` with `render_cache` also sets it.
    - A checked effect renders in Smart and in User mode, as in Resolve (p. 204). A checked effect that is off renders nothing.
    - Off on the clip does not stop its checked effects. Why: Resolve has two settings for them.
  - A video track can be out of the cache.
    - The menu of the track header has Exclude Track from Caching (p. 205). `track.set` with `render_cache` also sets it.
    - In User mode, the track gives none of its speed changes, Retime Processes, transitions, opacity and composite modes. Why: Resolve caches these in its sequence cache (p. 204). The setting is part of the User mode options of that cache (p. 205).
    - Its clips set to On, its checked effects and its AI Super Scale still render.
    - The manual gives this setting for User mode only. Thus it does nothing in Smart mode.
  - Smart renders each part of the timeline that has one of these:
    - a clip set to On
    - a checked effect
    - media that are slow to decode (X103)
    - a speed change
    - a Retime Process other than Nearest (X103)
    - AI Super Scale (X103)
    - a transition
    - an opacity below 100%, or keyframes of the opacity
    - a composite mode other than Normal
    - a part where frames drop steadily during play (X103)
  - Smart marks a part where frames drop steadily:
    - The timeline plays forward, at normal speed.
    - Frames drop in each second of a stretch of at least 5 s. In each of these seconds, a quarter of the frames or more come late or are skipped.
    - The frames of the stretch took longer to make than their frame time, on average.
      - Why: then the frames themselves are slow. A late draw on the screen does not mark a part.
    - The first second after a start or a seek does not count.
    - The time of a job, of the background render and of a hidden window does not count.
    - The mark covers each part of the plan that holds the stretch. A moved part keeps its mark. An edit that changes the frames of a part makes a new part without a mark.
    - The marks are on this computer, with the cache. Delete Render Cache > All removes them.
    - Only Smart marks parts and uses the marks.
  - User renders the clips set to On and the checked effects. It also renders the kinds that Preferences turns on:
    - Transitions: off, unless set.
    - Composites: off, unless set. It includes opacity, as in Resolve.
    - AI: on, unless set. It is AI Super Scale and the AI Retime Processes. It stands in for the Fusion effects of Resolve.
  - The cache has two levels, as in Resolve (pp. 203–204).
  - The clip level keeps the picture of one clip, before the tracks mix. It is the Render Cache Fusion Output of Resolve (p. 203).
    - It holds the clips set to On, media that are slow to decode, speed changes and AI Super Scale.
    - It holds stills too.
    - It holds a Retime Process other than Nearest, and the checked effects (p. 204).
      - It holds the frame at the size of the timeline. Why: a Retime Process and an effect make each frame at the size that the clip shows.
      - With a checked effect, it holds the effects up to the last checked effect. The viewer applies the later effects, as in Resolve (p. 202).
      - With a Retime Process, it holds all the effects. Why: the effects come before the frames between two source frames (§13).
      - For a placed clip, it holds the colors of the frame, with no edges and no alpha. The viewer makes them again from the placement. Thus the cache needs no alpha.
      - A change of the Transform, the crop or an effect that it holds renders it again.
      - A move, a split, a trim, or a change of the opacity, the composite mode or an effect after the last checked effect keeps it.
    - A move, a split, a trim or an edit on another track keeps it.
    - Without a Retime Process or a checked effect, it holds the picture at the size of the media, or of its proxy with proxies on. Effects and Transform come after it.
    - With AI Super Scale, it holds the enlarged picture, at the size that the upscaler makes. A change of the zoom or of the timeline size can change that size. Then the clip renders again.
    - Media with an alpha channel is at the clip level only with ProRes 4444 or 4444 XQ (p. 201, X106). Its file then keeps the alpha channel.
      - With a checked effect, or with a Retime Process while it plays forward, it stays at the timeline level. Why: its file holds only the colors of the placed clip.
      - With another codec, it stays at the timeline level.
  - The timeline level keeps the frame after the tracks mix. It holds the other reasons.
    - Titles on top of all tracks stay out of it. Thus an edit of their text keeps the cache.
    - A checked effect on a compound clip around such a title does not make a cache part.
    - A part that moves keeps its cache when the tracks under it stay the same.
    - An edit that changes the frames of a part renders it again.
  - The background render starts after a time without activity: 5 s, unless set (`render_cache.after_secs`). The time goes from 1 to 30 s (measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2).
    - Activity is an edit, a play, a seek, a scrub, the preview of a change, or a job.
    - Each activity stops the render at once. The render continues later.
    - It renders from the playhead forward, then from the start of the timeline.
    - Where the memory keeps the frames that played before the playhead, it starts at the first of them (X103).
    - With proxies off, it draws a frame that the memory keeps. It does not make that frame again.
    - In an HDR timeline, it makes these frames again:
      - an AI picture
      - a frame that a Retime Process makes
      - a frame of a Smooth Cut
    - At the clip level, it makes these frames again:
      - a frame of a placed clip with a Retime Process
      - a frame of a clip with a checked effect
      - a frame of a Smooth Cut
    - A frame of a Smooth Cut comes from two clips. Thus it is not a frame of one clip.
    - `render_cache.background` turns it off. Then the cache keeps what it has, and renders nothing new.
  - The codec of the cache files is a preference of this computer (X103). Proxies and Render Cache > Advanced > Codec sets it. `render_cache.codec` also sets it.
    - It is Automatic, unless set. With proxies on, Automatic takes the codec of proxies. With proxies off, it takes ProRes 422 HQ, HEVC or AV1 ([playback](design/playback.md#the-render-cache)).
    - The other choices:
      - ProRes 422 Proxy, LT, 422 and HQ, and ProRes 4444 and 4444 XQ
      - DNxHR LB, SQ, HQ, HQX and 444
      - HEVC and AV1
    - A chosen codec goes to the encoder as it is. No other codec takes its place.
    - When no encoder on this computer makes it, the cache stays empty. Preferences then says so under Codec, and `cache.status` gives the error.
    - DNxHR LB, SQ and HQ have 8 bits. Thus an HDR timeline gets no cache with them.
    - DNxHR 444 files keep no alpha channel. The DNxHR encoder of FFmpeg has no pixel format with alpha.
    - A change of the codec renders the cache again. The files of the other codec stay until Delete Render Cache > Unused removes them.
  - The cache files are in `render-cache` in the folder of playback files, beside the proxies (X26, X103).
    - Proxies and Render Cache > Folder sets that folder for proxies and the render cache.
    - Proxies and Render Cache > Advanced > Folder sets a folder for the render cache only (`playback_files.render_cache`).
    - Outside the cache dir, the files do not count in the limit of the disk cache. Delete Render Cache removes them.
    - Outside the cache dir, the render stops when the disk of the folder has less than 10% free. `cache.status` then gives the error. In the cache dir, the limit of the disk cache keeps that space free.
  - Playback does not write the cache (X103).
  - Playback > Delete Render Cache has three choices:
    - All empties the cache. It removes the cache files and leaves any other file in the folder.
    - Unused keeps only the frames of the open timeline.
    - Selected Clips removes the files that hold frames at the times of the selected clips. At the clip level, it removes only the frames of their media. The media of a compound clip are the media of the clips in it.
  - Playback > Manage Render Cache shows the render cache of each project that vidcut opened on this computer (p. 206, X105).
    - It opens also when no project is open.
    - Current Project shows the row of the open project.
    - Location and Project Library select the rows to show. Location has All, Local and Network.
    - The columns are Location, Project Library, Project Name and Render Cache. A click on a column title sorts the rows. A second click reverses the order.
    - Render Cache shows the size of the cache, also when it is 0 kB. Its checkbox selects the row.
    - Clear Selected Cache deletes the cache of each checked row that is shown. As in Resolve, there is no warning and no undo.
    - When it deletes the cache of the open project, the render of that cache starts again.
    - Close closes the window.
  - In Smart and User mode, a line along the bottom of the ruler of both timelines shows the state (p. 203, X104).
    - Red: the mode renders this part. Blue: the part plays from the cache.
    - A part plays from the cache where its timeline level is cached. A part that needs only the clip level plays from the cache where each of its clips is cached.
    - The pointer over the line shows the reasons of the part.
    - A Retime Process and AI Super Scale name their process, as Stats for Nerds does. Example: "Retime Process: Frame Blend".
    - During the preview of a drag, a trim or a step of the history, the line is not shown. Why: the line is of the timeline before the change.
  - Stats for Nerds has a Render Cache row in How It Was Made (X104).
    - It tells for the frame on screen: Cached, To render with the reasons, or Not needed.
    - For a picture from the cache, How It Was Made shows how each layer was made when the cache was written.
    - Cached then also tells when the cache file was written. Example: "Cached, written 2 min ago".
  - For a picture from the cache, the Super Scale group names the AI upscaler that made it, from the render cache.
  - The Retime Process group names the Retime Process in the same way.
  - `cache.status` gives the parts to render, their reasons and their cached frames, and the size on disk. `cache.delete` deletes.
  - `cache.list` gives the rows of Manage Render Cache. `cache.clear` deletes the cache of the given project files.
  - `cache.render` renders the cache now, for scripts.
    - In a running GUI, it starts the background render at once, also with `render_cache.background` off. It answers at once, as `cache.status`.
    - Without a GUI, the CLI renders all that the mode renders, and then answers. Ctrl-C stops it.

## 13. Order of operations (pp. 287–296, 3407)

The manual gives the order in which each picture is processed, on each page, as a chart (Ch. 144, p. 3407). vidcut keeps this order for the stages that it has.

- A different order is a deviation, as for any other behavior.
- [design](design/README.md#picture-order) gives the order of the code. [Deviation X98](deviations.md#x98-in-an-hdr-timeline-ai-slow-works-before-the-conversion) tells where it is different.

Resolve converts each clip into full-range, uncompressed 32-bit float data when it reads it (p. 222). All stages then work on that data.

**Source processing**, for each clip:

1. Camera Raw settings.
2. Lens Correction.
3. The input goes into the timeline color space: Resolve Color Management, or the ACES IDT.
4. The input LUT.
5. Deinterlace.
6. Super Scale.

Then the compositing of the Fusion page.

**Timeline processing:**

1. Edit Sizing: the Transform and Cropping values of the Inspector, and Dynamic Zoom (pp. 294–295).
2. Input Sizing: the Sizing palette of the Color page. Image Stabilization comes between Edit Sizing and Input Sizing (p. 295).
3. Fusion effects.
4. The Pre-Clip grade of a group.
5. The Open FX and Resolve FX of the Cut page and the Edit page.
6. The clip grade.
7. The Post-Clip grade of a group.
8. Speed Effects.
9. Track compositing: alpha, composite modes and opacity.
10. The timeline grade.

**After the timeline:**

1. Subtitles.
2. Output Sizing, then its Blanking (p. 296).
3. The output LUT.
4. The timeline color space goes into the output color space: Resolve Color Management, or the ACES RRT and ODT.
5. Data Burn-In.
6. The output of the Deliver page.

The display LUT of the Color page is on a branch after Data Burn-In. The output of the Deliver page does not get it.

**Sizing:**

- Resolve combines the fit to the timeline, Edit Sizing and Input Sizing into one transform of the source (p. 287).
- Thus a picture that one stage makes smaller and a later stage enlarges keeps the detail of its source (p. 290).
- Output Sizing does not go back to the source. It enlarges the picture that the earlier stages give (p. 296).

The chart does not show titles, generators or transitions.

### The LUT tables

These facts are measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2, with no color management. The LUT is on the node of a clip, or it is the `Input LUT` property of a Media Pool clip.

- A 3D table is trilinear. Red changes fastest, then green, then blue.
- A 1D table is linear on each channel.
- The input is clamped to 0..1 before the lookup. The values of the table and the output are not clamped.
- `DOMAIN_MIN` and `DOMAIN_MAX` have no effect.
- `LUT_1D_INPUT_RANGE lo hi` and `LUT_3D_INPUT_RANGE lo hi` apply: the place in the table is `(x - lo) / (hi - lo)`, clamped to 0..1.
  - The same rule applies when `lo` is above `hi`. A range of `1 0` reads the table backward.
  - When `lo` equals `hi`, each pixel gets the first row of the table.
- A file with both tables runs the 1D table, then the 3D table. Each table uses its own range. The 1D rows come first in the file.
- These sizes work:
  - `LUT_3D_SIZE`: 2, 3, 5, 17, 33, 65, 129 and 256.
  - `LUT_1D_SIZE`: 2, 5, 17 and 65536.
- `TITLE`, comments, blank lines and tabs are read.
- With no LUT, the input comes out unchanged, also below 0 and above 1.
- A LUT does not change the alpha of a picture. In these results, `T` is the table, `C` is the color in the file and `A` is the alpha:
  - With the Alpha Mode Straight, the result is `T(C) * A`.
  - With the Alpha Mode Premultiplied, the result is `T(C / A) * A`. With `A` at 0, the result is 0.

**Where a LUT goes:**

- The `Input LUT` of a clip and a LUT on its node use the same math. On 15 tables, a few pixels differ, by 0.014 at most.
- The `Input LUT` is a property of the Media Pool clip (p. 3480). Each use of the file in a timeline gets it.
- A script cannot set the `Input LUT` of a compound clip or of a timeline.
- A LUT on the node of a compound clip gives the same picture as a LUT on the node of a plain clip.
- A generator and a title have no Media Pool clip. A script gets no node graph for them, so it cannot give them a LUT.

A script gets no error for a broken file:

- A lower-case `lut_3d_size` line, or no size line, gives no LUT.
- 100 rows where 125 are due give a changed picture, which the rule above does not fit.
- 130 rows where 125 are due use the first 125 rows.
- `LUT_3D_SIZE 1` gives a black picture.

### The scaling filter

The default Resize Filter of a project is Sharper. These facts are measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2.

- An export uses this filter for a zoom, a fit to the timeline, a rotation and a move by a fraction of a pixel.
- It also uses this filter when it rounds an odd frame size for an encoder.
- For each output pixel, the filter reads 16 source pixels across and 16 down. A rotated picture reads along its own axes.
- `d` is the distance from the output pixel to a source pixel, in source pixels.
- `s` is the output size divided by the source size. `s` is 1 when the picture enlarges.
- At each half pixel of `d`, the weight is `s × sinc(s × d) × (0.5 + 0.5 × cos(π × d / 8))`. From `d` = 8, the weight is 0.
- Between two half pixels, the weight is on a straight line.
- The weights of each axis are divided by their sum.
- A read past the edge of the picture takes the edge pixel.
- The filter works in the timeline gamma.
- This formula is measured down to a shrink of 18.6:1. Above a shrink of 8:1, vidcut is different ([X110](deviations.md#x110-a-shrink-above-81-reads-each-source-pixel)).

### The fit to the timeline

These facts are measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2, in a default project that scales a picture to fit.

- One factor scales the picture on both axes. It is the smaller of two values: frame width / picture width, and frame height / picture height.
- The picture is centered. A bar at its side can be a part of a pixel.
- A pixel that the picture covers in part reads that part of its value, over the track below or over black.
- The Zoom and the Position of a clip work on this fit. They do not start from a rounded size.
- These pictures were measured in a timeline of 1280 x 720:
  - 1000 x 562: the factor is 1.28 and the height is 719.36. Each bar is 0.32 of a pixel, and the top and bottom rows read 0.68 of their value.
  - 999 x 800: the factor is 0.9 and the width is 899.1. Each bar is 190.45 pixels, and the edge columns read 0.55 of their value.
- 1920 x 1080 in a timeline of 1921 x 1081 has the factor 1921 / 1920 and a bar of 0.21875 of a pixel. The edge rows read a part of their value.
- A picture with pixels that are not square, or with a rotation in its file, was not measured.
  - vidcut gives such a picture a fitted size that is rounded to even, and a place in whole pixels.

### The limit for a thin bar

These facts are measured in Resolve Studio 21.1.2, in timelines of 1280 x 720 and 1920 x 1080.

- For each corner of the frame, take its distance to the picture. The distance is 0 when the corner is inside the picture.
- If all four distances are under 0.2 of a timeline pixel, no bar shows.
  - Each edge pixel reads its full value, and the track below does not show.
  - The picture keeps its exact scale and place. It does not stretch to the frame.
- If a distance is 0.2 or more, each bar shows at its exact width. This includes a thinner bar at another side.
- The limit is the same for the fit, the Zoom, the Position and the Rotation, alone or together.
- These cases were measured in a timeline of 1280 x 720:
  - 3840 x 2159: the fit leaves bars of 0.1667 of a pixel. No bar shows, and the scale on both axes is 1/3.
  - The same picture with a Position of 0.1 down: the bars are 0.0667 and 0.2667, and both show.
  - Position 0.198 across: no bar. Position 0.2 across: the edge column reads 0.8 of its value.
  - Position 0.14 across and down: the distance is 0.198, and no bar shows.
  - Position 0.15 across and down: the distance is 0.212, and the bars show.
  - A Zoom that leaves bars of 0.17 and 0.0956: the distance is 0.195, and no bar shows.
  - A Zoom that leaves bars of 0.18 and 0.10125: the distance is 0.2065, and the bars show.
  - Rotation 0.016: no bar. Rotation 0.02: the bars show. The rule gives the change at 0.0179. That value was not measured.
  - A part of the picture past the frame does not count. A left bar of 0.15 on an enlarged picture shows no bar.
- These cases were not measured:
  - Softness: vidcut does not use the limit on a picture with Softness.
  - Pitch and Yaw: vidcut uses the same rule on the corners of the leaned picture.
  - A compound clip: vidcut uses the rule on each level by itself.
  - The rounding of an odd frame size for an encoder: vidcut does not use the limit there.
