# Kept pictures and their decoders' pools

A picture the player keeps as decoded holds its decoder's whole frame pool, which never shrinks while any of its frames lives (gotchas.md). NVDEC's are copied into memory of their own (`cuda::keep`), Direct3D 11's into textures of their own (`hw::Keeper`), VA-API's by `vaCopy` into surfaces of their own where the driver has it (Intel's), and the CPU's decoders decode into buffers of vidcut's own (`buffers.rs`). The others still hold their pools:

- **VA-API on Mesa** (AMD): no exact copy (gotchas.md), so a kept frame is the decoder's surface, from a pool the decoders share (`hw::vaapi::share_frames`), which goes once nothing uses it. A pool keeps its peak while any of its frames is kept: with a 4K and a 1080p clip taking turns under a 1 GB budget, memory peaks at 2.3–2.5 GB while one size's pictures give way to the other's, and comes back to 1.3 GB. A held picture (the plan played next's) keeps its pool whole. Letting all of a pool's pictures go at once when another pool needs the room, or bringing a dead pool's last few pictures down to memory, would end that. The budget also counts a surface by its samples, where AMD's take more (960×540 P010: 337 pictures counted as 499 MB took about 700 MB): DRM PRIME's export gives each surface's objects' sizes. - **Vulkan** (ProRes on NVIDIA's GPU): a copy into images of vidcut's own needs a submission on FFmpeg's queues (`lock_queue`, deprecated), preview.md §3.
- **libdav1d** (AV1 on the CPU): its frames come from a pool of its own, and it takes no `get_buffer2` (no `AV_CODEC_CAP_DR1`). Only a copy frees them.
- **The deinterlacer** (interlaced video in memory): its frames are buffers of the filter graph's pools (libavfilter's, one for each link), freed after the graph and its last frame.

How much it costs shows where a decoder closes while its pictures are kept: proxies switched on and off, a cut to another file and back, each prepared picture's reader.
